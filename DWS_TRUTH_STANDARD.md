Thin meta-registry listing every internal source-of-truth file in DWS6. Answers one question: _"Where is the canonical definition of X?"_

This file does not define facts. It is a directory. Sibling to `DWS_STANDARDS_LIBRARY.md` (which catalogs _external_ standards adopted by DWS); this catalogs _internal_ registries DWS itself owns.

The Roles chapter pulls from `DWS_ROLES_REGISTRY.md` (registered as §2 row 15). The extension is optional — not every standard needs all three; force-fit creates noise.

## 2A. Canonical Positioning Pillars (above the moats)

Three reasoning pillars sit **above the 19 moats the way Layer 0 does** — they are not moats; they are the framework that keeps the moats (and all positioning) from drifting back into techno-romantic narrative. Every positioning, pitch, product, marketing, or moat claim must be consistent with all three. Registered as §2 row 22.

**Pillar 1 — Doctrine Shift** _(the structural "why now," non-expiring)_. EU industrial compliance is moving from **voluntary ESG to mandatory sovereign infrastructure**. DWS is shaped for the new doctrine, not the old one. Lead with the doctrine the buyer is already moving toward; never pitch DWS as a better voluntary-ESG tool.

**Pillar 2 — Orchestration Over Components.** The product is the **governed outcome layer**, not the agents underneath. Agent count — and skill/workflow count — is **implementation detail**, never a headline or selling claim. This is the root kill-switch for the agent-count drift trap.

**Pillar 3 — Buyer-Side Truth Registry** _(the Ari test)_. ## 2G. AI-Generated Content Labelling — EU AI Act Article 50(4) & the EU Icons

**The icon is optional. The label is not.** This is the single sentence this section exists to protect, because it is the claim most likely to drift in both directions — into "we use the EU icon, therefore we comply" and into "the icons are voluntary, therefore labelling is voluntary". Both are false.

**The instrument.** EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — **Article 50** (transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems). Two paragraphs matter:

- **Article 50(2) — providers.** Output of a generative AI system must be marked in a **machine-readable** format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.
- **Article 50(4) — deployers.** Two disclosure duties, in the Commission's own wording:
  - **Deep fakes** — "AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful."
  - **AI-generated or manipulated text** "published to inform the public on matters of public interest, **which did not undergo human review or editorial control and where editorial responsibility was not assumed by any legal or natural person**."

**Not everything AI touches needs a label.** The Commission states plainly: "Not all AI-generated or manipulated content needs to be labelled." The duty is bounded by the two categories above. Over-labelling is its own drift — it trains readers to ignore the label.

**Note the structure of the text duty.** The human-review carve-out is written **into the scope of the obligation**, not bolted on as an afterthought: text is in scope only where it did **not** undergo human review or editorial control **and** no person assumed editorial responsibility. To be out of scope you need **both** — review/control **and** an accountable person.

**Three exceptions (Commission wording).**

1. **Evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous works** — where deep fake content is part of such a work, transparency is limited to "disclosure in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work."
2. **Authorised by law** — the duty does not apply where use of deep fakes or published text on matters of public interest is authorised by law "to detect, prevent, investigate or prosecute criminal offences."
3. **Human review or editorial control for text** — as above.

**Applies from 2 August 2026.** _(Article 113 applicability date; not stated on the EU Icons page itself — see verification note.)_

**The EU Icons.** Free, published by the Commission / AI Office as **an integral part of Section 2 of the Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content** (referred to elsewhere as the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content — same instrument, both names in circulation; use the Commission's own phrasing when quoting Section 2).

**Three icons, and they are not interchangeable:**

| Icon | When to use |
| --- | --- |
| **Basic** | AI was involved in creating deep fake content or published text, **or** where a custom text label or interactive second layer is implemented |
| **Fully AI-Generated** | The **entire** deep fake or text is AI-generated with no human-created elements and no human editorial control **apart from prompting** |
| **Partially AI-Modified** | **Pre-existing, human-made** content was partially modified with AI, turning it into a deep fake or into text on matters of public interest |

Four variations each — black, white, black at 50% transparency, white at 50% transparency — downloadable as ZIPs in **SVG and PNG**.

**User-testing finding (Commission):** performance improved **across all measures** when the basic icon was accompanied by a **text label** (e.g. "modified"). Icon alone is the weakest form; icon **plus** plain-language text is the tested best.

**Placement (summary of Section 2 — not a complete overview; signatories commit to the full specs):**

- Clearly perceivable and distinguishable **at the latest at the time of first exposure** of a natural person to the content.
- Placed where **no intervening overlay elements** exist.
- **Directly embedded** into the deep fake or published text (except creative works), unless an equivalent alternative such as a UI overlay is available — and **it must remain visible when the content is reshared or downloaded**.

**Accessibility (encouraged):** clearly visible size; plain language, no jargon, **no abbreviations other than "AI"**; readable by assistive technology via **alt text or ARIA labels** stating the content is AI-generated or manipulated; if the disclosure is time-limited, long enough to be read by users with cognitive or processing difficulties; if a second interactive layer carries more information, the icon must signal that and the layer must be navigable by assistive technology.

**Licence and what it does *not* signal.** Free for anyone to use, **no attribution** to the Commission or the AI Office required. But: signatories must use the icon per the placement specs, and **use of the icons by a non-signatory must not be construed as signalling adherence to the Code**. DWS has not signed the Code — so if DWS ever displays an icon, it must not be presented as, or allowed to imply, signatory status.

- **Icon use is optional; the Article 50 labelling requirements are not.**
- **Using an icon does not establish legal compliance by itself.** The deployer remains responsible for ensuring the disclosure meets Article 50. Never write or imply "EU-icon labelled, therefore Article 50 compliant".

**What this binds inside DWS.** DWS is a **deployer** on its own public surfaces. AI-generated or machine-translated text on `onelifetime.world/`, `dws10.com/`, `dws6.com/` or `lifetime.fi/` that informs the public on matters of public interest is in scope from 2 August 2026 unless it had human review or editorial control **and** a person assumed editorial responsibility. **Machine translation counts** — a translated page is AI-generated or AI-manipulated text.

- **HIC decision 27.07.2026 — labelling is the minimum, review is the target.** Risto: _"A is ideal choice but B is minimum requirement."_ Every machine-translated DWS page **ships labelled by default**; a named human review is what removes the label. Implemented for the four LSJ translations in PR #1758 (`dont-train-a-base-model-own-the-evidence-layer.{,sv.,de.,fr.}html`) as an `<aside class="ai-notice">` before the article body, generated per language by `scripts/i18n/build-i18n-pages.cjs` and controlled by `aiNotice.langs` in the target config. - **No EU Icon asset is bundled.** The icon is optional, ec.europa.eu blocks automated download, and a hand-drawn lookalike would misrepresent an official mark. ## 7. Skill _(pilot — template chapter)_

The skill that operationalizes this standard ad-hoc. Recommended, not yet built.

- **`/truth lookup <domain>`** — read §2; return the canonical file + structured mirror for the named domain. Example: `/truth lookup industries` → `/INDUSTRIES.md` + `dws10.com/config/industries.json`.
- **`/truth check <claim>`** — parse a public-copy claim, infer the domain, load the relevant mirror's `approvedDisplayPhrases` / `deprecatedDisplayPhrases` / `ambiguousDisplayPhrasesNeedingContext`, return PASS / WARN / FAIL with the canonical phrasing on mismatch.
- **`/truth scan [<path>]`** — walk public-layer files (per §5 scope), report any deprecated phrases. No edits, findings only.

**Status:** built 2026-06-09 as `/truth-check` (`.claude/commands/truth-check.md`, backed by `scripts/lint-truth-claims.mjs`). `/truth-check <claim>`, `/truth-check <path…>`, and `/truth-check --changed` all work; `/truth lookup` and `/truth scan` are covered by the same script's path/JSON modes. ## 8. Workflow _(pilot — template chapter)_

The CI/automation gate that enforces this standard automatically. Recommended, not yet built.

- **`lint-truth-claims` GitHub Actions workflow** — iterates every structured mirror with `structuredMirror: true`; loads the phrase lists; scans the public-layer scope.
  - **PR-time:** scans the diff only, comments inline on any deprecated phrases the change introduces. Non-blocking warning initially; blocking after stabilization.
  - **Nightly:** scans full public layer; opens an issue if drift detected.
  - **On-deploy:** runs as deploy-gate per `DWS_DEPLOYMENT_STANDARD.md` §4 — fails the deploy if build output contains deprecated phrases.

**Status:** PR-time gate built 2026-06-09 (`.github/workflows/lint-truth-claims.yml`) — runs `lint-truth-claims.mjs --changed` on every PR touching `.md` / `.html` / `.txt`, posts findings to the job summary, **non-blocking** for now (`continue-on-error`); flip to blocking after it stabilizes. Nightly full-scan and on-deploy gate are the remaining follow-ups.

Independent Ontology

Technology changes. Understanding must remain.

People change roles.

Systems are replaced.

AI models evolve.

But an organisation's ability to understand its own operations must not disappear with the change.

Independent Ontology provides a structure that lets an organisation keep its concepts and their relations across generations of systems, vendors and technology, and lay the foundation for managing ownership and the reasons behind decisions over the long term.

What happens when understanding is lost?

Most organisations know what was decided.

Fewer can answer, years later:

  • Why was the decision made?
  • Which observations was it based on?
  • Who owned the matter?
  • What effects were intended?
  • What did we learn?

When the link between observations, decisions and effects breaks, the organisation's ability to learn weakens too.

The same investigations are done again.

The same mistakes are repeated.

Tacit knowledge leaves with people.

Independent of technology

Independent Ontology does not tie an organisation's understanding to a single:

  • software product
  • database
  • AI model
  • vendor
  • platform

It provides a more durable structure for managing information and concepts, and a foundation for managing ownership.

Technologies can be replaced.

Understanding does not have to be replaced with them.

A shared language for the organisation

As organisations grow, parallel views of the same things emerge.

One talks about customers.

Another about processes.

A third about data.

A fourth about business goals.

Independent Ontology helps form a shared structure, so the same things are spoken about in the same way across the organisation.

Continuity of decision-making

Decision-making is not a single event.

It is a chain of observations, choices, responsibilities and effects.

Independent Ontology is designed to keep these links understandable years from now.

The goal is not to collect more data.

The goal is to keep more understanding.

The sovereignty principle

An organisation owns its ontology, concepts, relations and knowledge capital regardless of vendor, technology or infrastructure.

You own your own model: the concepts, relations and data built for your organisation. The 25-industry base pack and its updates are licensed. When the licence ends, your own model stays with you.

Independent Ontology can be delivered:

  • into your own environment
  • into a cloud you control
  • into a private environment
  • into a fully air-gapped environment
  • without a continuous connection to Lifetime services

You are not buying a SaaS service but an understanding asset of your own. It can be kept in your own data centre, a closed network, a government or defence environment, or critical infrastructure, with no external dependencies.

Hardware

The ontology itself needs no DGX or other special hardware: it is delivered as versioned artefacts. If you also want to run your own AI model stack in a closed environment, it is delivered separately on NVIDIA hardware, for example DGX Spark, Acer Veriton GN100 or, for industrial environments, IGX Orin. Hardware and the on-prem pack (LWM-ONT-SOVEREIGN) are in the price list.

The buying path for enterprise customers

A customer does not have to jump straight to the annual licence. The path goes step by step.

1. Prove the value

Independent Ontology Pilot, one site

LF-ONTOLOGY-PILOT · Duration 3 months

See the price in the price list

The pilot tests us, not you and not the technology.

The pilot succeeds if at least one of the following three goals is achieved and accepted by you. Each goal has measurable criteria.

A. Understanding the business

We can model your business in a way that your named responsible people accept.

  • at least 50 core concepts identified and documented
  • at least 100 relations between concepts modelled
  • at least 3 of your key people assess that the model describes the business correctly
  • at least 80% of the reviewed concepts and relations assessed as correct
Do we identify the core concepts of the business and their relations correctly?

B. Ownership and responsibilities

We can identify the ownership and responsibility structures that matter for the business.

  • at least 20 key objects or processes mapped
  • a responsible owner identified for at least 90% of them
  • for every mapped object, documented whether ownership is clear or unclear, with the unclear ones named
  • you accept the ownership map
Do we understand who owns what, and why?

C. Compliance and governance

We can build a consistent model of your compliance environment.

  • at least one regulatory area modelled
  • at least one chain of responsibility documented end to end
  • at least one concept map needed for audit or reporting produced
  • you accept the model as the basis for further development
Do we understand the compliance requirements well enough that they can be managed systematically?

At the end of the pilot we deliver:

  • a first ontology skeleton
  • a documented concept model
  • a relationship map
  • an ownership map (where applicable)
  • a compliance map (where applicable)
  • a recommendation on next steps

Go / no-go decision: does the ontology create enough value for you that the licence and implementation are justified?

  • GO: implementation (LF-TRAIN-IND) and licence (LF-ONTOLOGY-25)
  • HOLD: extended pilot
  • NO-GO: the pilot ends

All three are acceptable outcomes. What decides in the end is not the number of concepts or relations, but this:

Do you accept that the ontology describes your business, responsibilities or compliance environment accurately enough to have business value?

The pilot does not promise automatic learning or ready-made decision tracking.

Credit: 100% of the pilot fee is credited against the first year of the LF-ONTOLOGY licence if the licence agreement is signed within 90 days of the pilot's end. You pay for either the pilot or the first licence year, not both.

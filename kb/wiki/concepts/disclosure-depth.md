# Disclosure Depth — Decision Figures on the Page, Implementation in the Whitepaper

**Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** Active (HIC 01.10.2026) · **Scope:** every public DWS page · **Canonical:** `DWS_TRUTH_STANDARD.md` §2J, row 47

## The rule

A product or details page gives a buyer the figures needed to decide: what it is, who it is for, price, duration, what the customer gets and owns, acceptance criteria in one line each, and the sovereignty layers. It does not give a competitor the build instructions.

Implementation detail goes to a whitepaper: formats and algorithms, file layouts, commands, key custody, seal cadence, storage lock settings, verifier design, step-by-step procedures. The whitepaper's abstract and contents are public; the full text is sent on request through a form that mails `risto.anton@lifetime.fi`. The page cites it with an Oxford-style footnote.

Blog posts describe the problem and the solution at a general level and link to the request page.

## First application: DWS IQ 7 Aegis

- Whitepaper: WP13 _When the Link Drops — Aegis acceptance and evidence_ (`docs/whitepapers/WP13_WHEN_THE_LINK_DROPS.md`).
- Request page: `dws10.com/whitepapers/wp13-when-the-link-drops/` (5 languages, generated by `scripts/i18n/build-wp13-request-pages.cjs`).
- Form: `/api/contact` with `whitepaper: "WP13"`, routed to `WHITEPAPER_TO` (default `risto.anton@lifetime.fi`).
- FI pilot text v2.1.0 in `Store/PRODUCT_AEGIS_PILOT.md` cites WP13 as footnote ¹.
- Vocabulary: `ONTOLOGY.md` §16.7; dictionary keys `acceptance_test`, `stop_authority`, `evidence_bundle`, `retention_lock`, `whitepaper_on_request`.

## Why

A details page that names the hash construction, the signature format and the lock settings is a free specification for a competitor and adds nothing to a buyer's decision. The buyer's engineers and auditors still get every detail, in the whitepaper, from a named person.

# Runtime-Evidence Doctoral Spine

**Version:** 1.0 · **Status:** Active · **Scope:** LUT doctoral framing for autonomous agents in sovereign edge computing

## The claim

> **The doctoral core should be built around runtime evidence of agent behaviour — what can be monitored, what evidence costs, how the governance layer produces traceable proof, and how regulated industry adopts that governance.**

This framing shifts the work away from two weaker postures:

1. **Declared intent as trust proxy.** An agent's stated reasoning or intention is not treated as sufficient evidence of what it actually did.
2. **Institutional adoption as research result.** A standardization outcome is not assumed to be the finding; runtime evidence and falsifiable governance properties are.

## The four-part spine

1. **Runtime monitoring.** Which safety and trustworthiness properties are monitorable during execution so that deviations are detectable and verifiable?
2. **Cost of safety evidence.** What latency, compute, and energy cost is paid for producing runtime safety/compliance evidence at the edge, relative to the coverage gained?
3. **KYA governance-layer design principles.** Which principles are necessary, and which are case-specific, for a governance layer that yields traceable evidence and regulation-grade logging?
4. **Organizational adoption.** How do regulated industrial organizations integrate agent governance into existing management and risk systems with minimal disruption?

## Why this matters

The structure turns the doctoral work into one coherent chain:

- **observe** the agent at runtime,
- **price** the evidence burden,
- **design** the governance layer that makes the evidence useful,
- **embed** that governance into industrial operating practice.

That is a stronger scientific spine than treating future standardization or the agent's own narrative of its behaviour as the central object.

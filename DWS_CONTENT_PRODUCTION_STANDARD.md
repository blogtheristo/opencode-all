## 1. Purpose

The **DWS Content Production Standard** is the internal control standard that defines how Lifetime Studios content is researched, journalistically processed, claim-mapped, protected-argumented, HIC-approved, localized, published, distributed, and fed back into the ontology.

It separates DWS content from ordinary content marketing: **every publication is a sourced research and sales-funnel building block**, not a standalone marketing text. The standard applies uniformly to all 125 core cards and their variations.

**Commercial credibility principle:** *truth first, both sides visible, useful analysis for the decision-maker, commercial step only when it genuinely follows from the problem.* — *Totuus ensin, molemmat puolet näkyviin, päätöksentekijälle hyödyllinen analyysi, kaupallinen askel vain kun se seuraa aidosti ongelmasta.*

## 2. Scope of the Standard

The standard governs the full content lifecycle:

1. Research (primary public sources)
2. Journalistic processing (source vs. claim vs. analysis vs. hypothesis)
3. Source and claim map
4. Protected argumentation (limitation handling)
5. HIC approval (Rule 13)
6. Localization (five languages)
7. Publication (onelifetime.world + landing-pages mirror)
8. Distribution (class-1 social pools)
9. Ontology feedback (card structure feeds the DWS ontology/graph)

## 3. Core Card Structure — Seven Steps

Every Business Case card is built on the same seven-step core structure, in order. Every DWS material follows the seven-step structure without exception:

| Step | Name | Definition and editorial requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Industry, enterprise, and buyer** | Define the target organization, industry context, and the decision-maker's role precisely (e.g., CxO, Compliance Officer). |
| **2** | **Compliance situation and limitation** | Identify the regulatory environment and honestly name the first boundary condition or data/process limitation (*voluntary openness*). |
| **3** | **Sources and claim map** | Anchor claims to public sources, own data, the ontology, and World Model evidence. Separate fact, analysis, and hypothesis. |
| **4** | **Effect on the decision** | Describe without understatement or exaggeration what the identified limitation means for business or legal decision-making. |
| **5** | **Management measure** | Show a methodological solution to the risk (e.g., bounded use case, airtight audit chains, explicit marking of deviations). |
| **6** | **Residual uncertainty** | Explicitly bound how far the solution carries. Do not promise a complete "magic button", which protects expert value. |
| **7** | **Possible demo and next step** | Present the demo or tool as a neutral continuation for verifying the management measure in practice — not a hidden sales pitch. |

## 4. Editorial Lifecycle and Workflow

For a text to meet the *DWS Content Production Standard*, it must pass the following lifecycle before distribution and analysis:

1. **Research and Claim Map — evidence and anchor discovery.** Map public sources, company-specific evidence, and the anchors offered by the ontology. Build the claim map in which every claim is anchored to a data source.
2. **Journalistic Processing and Writing — protected argumentation (steps 1–7).** Apply the seven-step framework. Handle opposing views and limitations (Crowley & Hoyer 1994; Eisend 2006). Ensure the text does not generate psychological reactance in the buyer (Brehm & Brehm 1981).
3. **HIC Approval (Human-in-Command) — human-in-the-loop verification.** A substance expert and the editorial function audit the realization of the claim map, the ethical boundary-setting, and the precision of sourcing before publication permission.
4. **Localization, Publication, and Distribution — context- and buyer-specific shaping.** Repeat the core claim in different forms and channels (blog, LinkedIn, white paper, demo, seminar) using the *illusory truth effect* mechanism, without identical copying.
5. **Ontology Feedback — enriching the evidence chain.** Reader and customer reactions, follow-up questions, and demo data are fed back into the ontology and World Model as base evidence for subsequent publications.

The standard guarantees that repetition rests on **provability, not merely on visibility**.

## 5. Protected Argumentation Model

*Limitation → effect on the decision → management measure → residual uncertainty → possible demo.*

The model is the protected-argumentation backbone of the card structure:

1. **Identify and name the limitation** — voluntary openness creates the basis for trust; place the limitation early in the analysis
2. **Define the effect on the decision** — realistic assessment without understatement or exaggeration
3. **Present a clear management measure** — turning the limitation into a bounded functional solution
4. **Acknowledge the residual uncertainty** — protects the article's expert value
5. **Offer the demo as a natural continuation** — a consequence, not a hidden lead-in; the demo is a way to test the presented management measure in practice

**Attribution framing.** Handling limitations voluntarily and proactively makes the reader perceive an honest actor (internal attribution) rather than a pure sales motive (external attribution), maximizing source credibility without diminishing product value.

**Editorial principle:** *do not hide the limitation, do not exaggerate it, show how it is managed.* — *Älä piilota rajoitetta, älä paisuttele sitä, näytä miten sitä hallitaan.*

## 6. Production Chain

**Matrix row → cluster company → sources and claims → card → HIC review → journalistic gate → five language versions → blog, LinkedIn and Facebook → ontology feedback → demo → Captain.**

Every step has an owner; no step may be skipped or reordered. The source and claim map (step 3) is what keeps the journalistic gate and the demo bound together: the demo demonstrates exactly what the sourced claims support.

## 7. Editorial Obligations

Every card and post applies four editorial obligations:

1. **Address limits, uncertainties, and alternative interpretations** alongside the case — no one-sided selling
2. **Separate the source, the company's own claim, the journalistic analysis, and the Lifetime-product hypothesis** (the four are never conflated)
3. **Give the reader enough information to draw their own conclusion** — the article informs; it does not decide for the reader
4. **Keep the demo a possible next step, not a hidden conclusion of the article** — the commercial step must follow genuinely from the problem, not be grafted on

## 8. Source and Claim Map

The source and claim map is a mandatory component of every card (step 3 of the core structure). It maps every fact and claim to its primary public source, with sourced facts separated from analysis and hypotheses. It is produced **before** the card is written, because it keeps the journalistic gate and the demo bound together: without it, the demo can claim what the sources do not support.

## 9. Journalistic Ground Rules

Company-specific Business Cases are journalistic research (Outokumpu model): public sources only; sourced facts separated from analysis and hypotheses; no claims of law-breaking without public evidence; comment opportunity before publication; no implication of endorsement or customer relationship. Every card must verify regulation status against a primary source (Council, Commission, Official Journal) before publication.

## 10. HIC Approval (Rule 13)

No version is published in any language without a named human reviewer. An agent may produce a draft; HIC (Risto) approves every version. Agent drafts are never self-approved. Reviewer capacity per language is budgeted visibly before production (see the operational plan §10).

## 11. Localization and Publication

Each flagship story is localized into FI/SV/DE/FR/EN. The same structure is transformed into a blog post, a LinkedIn post, and a Facebook long-tail text. Target: 800 flagship stories → ≈4,000 language versions. Publication uses onelifetime.world/posts/ + the landing-pages mirror and the idempotent blog-index updater.

## 12. Scientific Grounding

The standard rests on the scientific literature, cited in Oxford notation in `docs/LIFETIME_STUDIOS_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md` §13.1–13.2:

- **Illusory truth effect:** repetition increases perceived truthfulness even for false claims; even a single repetition increases perceived believability, and marginal benefit flattens after roughly 3–5 repetitions. The 40-touchpoint figure is a distribution target across varied formats, not a threshold. Principle: *repetition builds recall, evidence earns trust.*
- **Two-sided persuasion boundary conditions:** acknowledging a weakness raises source credibility, but a limitation critical to core product function without a management measure can weaken brand attitude. Best practice is a secondary-attribute limitation with a clear management measure or compensating benefit (Crowley & Hoyer 1994; Eisend 2006).
- **Attribution framing:** voluntary, proactive disclosure creates internal attribution (honest actor) instead of external attribution (sales motive).

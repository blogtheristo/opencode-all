DWS IQ 6 Resilience on Build

20.08.2026 · Risto Päärni · Espoo, Finland

HIC-approved field note · This page was drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by a named human reviewer before publication (human-in-the-loop, EU AI Act Article 14).

The Beef

If an entire development workflow stops the moment one AI vendor changes direction, that is not a resilient stack. It is a dependency.

Models change every month. Pricing changes. Policies change. The product has to keep getting built through all of it.

At Lifetime Oy we made continuity a design objective.

Three decisions carry it:

  • Local open-source models are the default for daily development. The foundation stays in the building.
  • Frontier models enter by invitation. When a task is too difficult for the local model, the frontier model does the heavy lifting and hands the work back.
  • The standards travel with the work, not with the vendor. Our Truth Standard, the DWS Ontology and domain dictionaries guard traceability and keep unsupported claims and AI bloat out of the codebase, whichever model wrote the code.

I am not claiming this makes us vendor-proof. Continuity is the objective, and you only find out how close you are by testing one question honestly:

If your primary AI provider changed pricing or policy tomorrow, how much of your development workflow would keep running?

The Story

June 2026 was my best month building with Claude. Since October 2025 the learning to code with AI and build the Firehorse production model put in speed and created 116 products in a year — not to forget the first months with Cursor.AI and Visual Studio Code when code was still prepared by human-in-the-loop. But overall Claude Code's growth as a connected tool has made me 10x stronger in the last 10 months. Thank you, Anthropic, Cursor Team, Microsoft, GitHub.

Today, the focus is to push every workload I can onto local models first: 1) to learn sovereign AI development, models, harnessing and so on; 2) to be independent from the frontier models and save a little money (not yet seen).

At my company Lifetime Oy we build with local open-source models day to day. Today Claude sits on hot standby: when a task gets too difficult for the local model, the frontier model steps in, does the heavy lifting, and hands the work back. The foundation stays in the building. Second is GitHub Copilot, which resourcefully updates my commits, fixes the latest issues found in the packages, and is a really valuable tool for managing multiple git repositories and customer installation versions.

The Potatoes

Models change every month. Pricing changes. Policies change. The product still has to get built through all of it.

That is why I wrote a dozen DWS standards to guard the work. The Truth Standard keeps unsupported claims out. The DWS Ontology keeps the structure honest. Domain dictionaries keep each knowledge area precise. Together they give traceability and stop AI bloat before it spreads.

Sovereign AI in practice is discipline about what runs where: local open-source models carry the daily work, and you step up to a frontier model only when the fix demands it.

Here is what that looks like on my desk, as of August 2026. If you need help setting up local-model-based development, DM me — I am here to help.

My desk & workflow — August 2026

Our daily coding runs on development boxes in the office. Cloud and frontier models only enter when we invite them.

The actual stack at Lifetime Oy right now:

  • Local, every day: OpenCode on a DGX Spark, models served through Ollama.
  • DeepSeek V4 and Qwen3 Coder for fast edits.
  • Nemotron-3-Super when the problem needs domain reasoning for the Lifetime World Model work.
  • Repo and git flow: GitHub Copilot CLI.
  • Cloud review: Gemini reads every PR — large context, multi-modal, a mandatory quality gate before anything merges.
  • Escalation only: Claude Code for the hard architectural calls. Hot standby, not daily driver.

The order matters more than the tools. Local is the default. Human-in-the-loop is HIC duty. Each cloud service has to justify its tier and its job, and everything it touches has already passed a human on this side of the wire. Quality gates are real.

I wrote KYA-S1 — v1.5 is a normative spec draft with a public comment window open — and I am building DWS IQ on it. KYA v2 is under development and planned for publication later this year.

We run our own sovereignty rules before we ask anyone else to.

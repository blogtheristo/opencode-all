Visma Sale Creates Once-in-a-Decade ERP Disruption for Nordic Industry

Risto Anton Päärni · Lifetime Oy · 8 min read

Visma is being sold. This is not routine M&A. This is the largest Nordic ERP transaction in a decade, and it will reshape how 500,000 companies think about their technology stack.

The Signal

When Hg Capital and other shareholders began exploring a sale of Visma — valued at approximately €20 billion — the immediate reaction was financial: who buys, at what multiple, what happens to the bonds. That's the wrong lens.

The right question is: what happens to the 500,000 companies running their business on Visma's ERP, payroll, and accounting systems?

History is instructive. When Infor was sold to Koch Industries in 2020, product roadmaps shifted within 18 months. When Epicor changed hands repeatedly (2011, 2016, 2020), customers experienced integration fatigue, support quality fluctuations, and pricing uncertainty. The Oracle-PeopleSoft acquisition (2005) resulted in a decade of migration pressure.[1]

Why This Time Is Different

Three forces converge that make the Visma situation uniquely disruptive for Nordic industry:

1. Regulatory Deadlines Are Non-Negotiable

CSRD reporting starts in 2026. EPBD energy performance requirements hit in 2027. ETS Phase 4 carbon pricing is already in effect. CBAM quarterly reporting is mandatory. These deadlines do not wait for M&A integration timelines. If your ERP vendor's compliance roadmap stalls for 12-18 months during ownership transition, you're exposed.

2. EU Data Sovereignty Is Now a Board-Level Issue

The CLOUD Act gives US authorities jurisdiction over data held by US-controlled companies — regardless of where the data is physically stored. If Visma's new owner is a US-domiciled entity, every piece of employee payroll data, customer financial data, and compliance documentation may fall under US legal jurisdiction. For companies subject to GDPR, NIS2, and the EU AI Act, this is not hypothetical risk — it is an audit finding waiting to happen.[2]

3. AI Agent Architecture Changes the ERP Game

The traditional ERP vendor pitch was "everything in one system." In 2026, the winning architecture is a compliance intelligence layer that sits above any ERP — reading operational data from SAP, Visma, IFS, or Monitor, and outputting audit-ready compliance reports. This decouples your compliance capability from your ERP vendor's ownership situation. Your ERP vendor changes hands? Your compliance layer keeps working.

What This Means for Your Business

If you're a Visma customer today, you have three options:

  1. Wait and see. Hope the new owner maintains product quality, pricing, and data residency commitments. This is what most companies do. It's also the highest-risk option, because you're betting regulatory compliance on someone else's M&A integration plan.
  2. Switch ERP. Evaluate Monitor, IFS, SAP Business One, or Microsoft Dynamics as alternatives. This takes 12-24 months and costs 5-20x your annual license. Realistic for some, impossible for most.
  3. Decouple your compliance layer. Keep your current ERP for operations. Add an independent compliance intelligence layer that reads from your ERP and produces audit-ready output — regardless of who owns your ERP vendor. This takes weeks, not months.

The Uncomfortable Truth

No ERP vendor — not Visma, not SAP, not anyone — has solved EU compliance automation at the level that 2026 regulations demand. The gap isn't in any single vendor's product. The gap is in the architecture assumption that compliance lives inside the ERP. It doesn't. Compliance spans every system: ERP, BIM, IoT, energy meters, supply chain, fleet management. It requires a cross-system intelligence layer.

The Visma sale is a market signal. It's telling 500,000 companies: your ERP vendor is a dependency you didn't plan for. The companies that decouple their compliance capability from their ERP vendor — regardless of which ERP they use — will navigate the next decade with confidence. The rest will navigate it with anxiety.

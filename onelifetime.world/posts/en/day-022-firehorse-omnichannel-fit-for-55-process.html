The Human-in-the-Loop Gate

EU AI Act, Article 14 — Human Oversight of High-Risk AI Systems

If your compliance reports are generated by AI with no human gate, you have an Article 14 problem. Auditors will flag it. Procurement will reject it. It does not matter how accurate the AI is.

Here is how our gate works:

  1. AI generates the report or content artifact.
  2. The system assigns a named human reviewer — not a role, a person.
  3. The reviewer reads, then approves or rejects with written notes.
  4. The system records who reviewed it, when, and what they noted.
  5. That record is append-only. It cannot be edited or deleted.
  6. Only after approval can content publish, distribute, or reach external parties.

The result is a court-defensible chain of custody for every document your company produces. When a compliance officer asks "who approved this carbon report?" — you have a timestamped answer with the reviewer's name and notes attached.

The Problem: Compliance Is Invisible to Your Clients

Most companies manage Fit for 55 in spreadsheets. Some hire consultants. A few use ERP add-ons. But none of them can answer this question from a procurement team:

"Show me your EU emissions process. How do I know you'll still be compliant next year?"

If the answer is "we have a consultant who handles it" — the conversation is over. The companies winning enterprise contracts in 2026 are the ones who can show a living, automated, auditable process with human oversight built in.

Lifetime Oy works with Kela, Finland's Social Insurance Institution, as a public sector pilot reference for the DWS IQ platform.

Why this pilot matters

Public sector institutions face the same EU regulatory pressure as private industry — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) alignment, Fit for 55 transition reporting, and ESG transparency obligations — but typically with stricter data sovereignty constraints and a lower tolerance for vendor lock-in.

Kela's pilot demonstrates that DWS IQ's skilled-agent platform can produce audit-defensible ESG output for a Finnish public institution while keeping every byte of data inside EU-governed infrastructure. No CLOUD Act exposure. No third-country model training. No vendor capture.

The pilot scope

  • Sector: Public sector — social insurance.
  • Geography: Finland (EU member state, eurozone).
  • Regulatory horizon: CSRD ESRS modules, EU public sector ESG disclosure expectations.
  • Platform: DWS IQ 6 — skilled agents with observe-think-act loops, MCP and CLI access.
  • Hosting: EU-sovereign infrastructure, Finland data residency.
  • Languages: Finnish, Swedish, English.

What DWS IQ replaces

Manual CSRD reporting cycles in large institutions typically consume 200–600 person-hours per period, distributed across sustainability, finance, legal, and operations teams. The work splits roughly into three phases:

  1. Data acquisition from heterogeneous source systems (ERP, HR, facilities, vehicle fleet, energy).
  2. Reconciliation and validation against ESRS taxonomy and audit expectations.
  3. Narrative composition and sign-off for the board / governance layer.

DWS IQ targets each phase with skilled agents — observation agents handle ingestion, validation agents enforce ESRS structure, and narrative agents draft the audit-ready text — under explicit human approval gates. Across our pilot portfolio we target a 60–90% reduction in manual hours per reporting cycle; the Kela-specific reduction will be published in the verified case study on 15 June 2026.

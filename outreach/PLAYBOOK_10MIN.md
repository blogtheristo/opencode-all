## Why 10, not 20

- **20 min** signals "discovery call" — Sustainability/IT vocabulary. KAMs block 30 seconds for "discovery."
- **10 min** signals "I know exactly what I want to show you and I respect your calendar." Higher accept rate, lower no-show rate, forces _us_ to be sharp.
- It pre-qualifies us: if we can't make the case in 10, we don't deserve a 60-day pilot on a KAM's named account.

## Vocabulary rules during the 10 minutes

- **Never say:** sustainability, ESG, platform, AI, SaaS, dashboard, ML, transformation, "compliance" as a noun.
- **Always say:** PO, order, the named end-customer, declaration, win, save, mill data, number.
- **Peer frame:** "you" and "your account" — never "your team" or "your company." KAM = one human running one set of relationships.
- If they go past 10 because _they_ want to, fine. If _we_ go past 10, we lose.
- No company history. No team slide. No regulatory explainer. KAMs already know the CBAM clauses their customers cite at them.

## Same-day follow-up

1. One-page recap email — what they said, what we'll do, MNDA attached, the **one** account name they picked, and the customer-side contact if they gave one.
2. If they did **not** pick an account in the call: one nudge 48h later with one external proof point — a public statement from a peer OEM in their segment asking suppliers for verified mill CO2. Then stop.

## Escalation handling

If during the call the KAM says "I need to loop in [my manager / VP / Sustainability]" — accept once, do not push. Reply: "Good — send them the recap I'll have in your inbox today. The pilot still lives on your account; they're informed, not gating." Going around a KAM to their boss kills the relationship even if it gets the meeting.

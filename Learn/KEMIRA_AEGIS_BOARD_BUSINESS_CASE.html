Carbon compliance as a sovereign advantage

A business case for DWS IQ Aegis Defence — turning Kemira's SBTi-validated carbon obligations and first-year CSRD reporting into an auditable, EU-sovereign capability rather than a recurring cost.

Prepared for: Maria (Hintikka) Sederholm · Mikko Pohjala Kemira Oyj — Fiber Essentials & Group Strategy Version 1.0 · 24 June 2026 Author: Risto Päärni, Lifetime Oy

0Executive summary

Kemira has hard, externally-validated carbon targets and, from FY2024, a CSRD/ESRS sustainability statement that auditors and customers now scrutinise. The work of producing, governing and attesting that carbon data — CBAM declarations, product carbon footprints (PCFs) for customers, Scope 3 collection from suppliers — is growing every year, and the AI tooling reached for to do it typically runs on US clouds, which for a critical-infrastructure chemicals supplier is a CLOUD Act exposure surface and an EU AI Act gap.

DWS IQ Aegis Defence helps Kemira turn carbon-compliance work into an attestable advantage — KYA-governed AI agents and EU AI Act Article 12 audit on Finnish-hosted, CLOUD Act-free infrastructure — so CBAM, CSRD and customer PCF demands are met faster, with the audit trail regulators and customers ask for, and Kemira's process data stays sovereign.

The ask

A paid scoping (free, 1–2 weeks) → pilot on one Fiber Essentials product line, aligned to Aegis GA (Sep 2026), measuring carbon-declaration cycle time, audit coverage and hours reclaimed.

The offer

Aegis Defence — €89,990 onboarding + €1,999/mo. Standard tier (€59,990 + €999/mo) is the fallback if the dual-use/EDIP layer proves out of scope.

Why this is a data & audit problem, not just a target

  • Scope 3 is the hard 32.5%. It depends on supplier and product-level data. Kemira already runs a Supplier Product Carbon Footprint (PCF) Reporting Guideline (April 2025) for direct-material suppliers — i.e. it is already collecting PCF data upstream Confirm. The same engine that ingests supplier PCFs produces customer-facing PCFs downstream.
  • Customers demand embedded-emissions data. Pulp, paper and packaging buyers increasingly require per-product carbon footprints and EPDs in tenders — work that today is manual and inconsistent.
  • CSRD/ESRS raises the evidence bar. A first-year sustainability statement must be reproducible to source data under assurance. AI used to draft or compute any of it falls under EU AI Act expectations — an audit-log requirement, not a nicety.
Honesty box — what we did NOT verify. The deep research could not substantiate, from primary sources, the following (treat as open and confirm in scoping): which specific Kemira installations fall under EU ETS Phase 4; Kemira's CBAM precursor-import exposure or downstream embedded-emissions demand volume; absolute Scope 1/2/3 tonnages (only % targets are confirmed); and any CER Directive / NIS2 / dual-use export-control obligations. The dual-use/defence angle that motivates the Defence tier is a hypothesis to test, not an established fact.

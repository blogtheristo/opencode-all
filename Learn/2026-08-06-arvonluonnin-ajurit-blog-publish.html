What happened

The session started as a research task — map Lifetime Oy’s current value-creation drivers and fold the findings into the consulting handbook. It ended as a fully published, 5-language public blog post, via a chain of HIC corrections that changed the deliverable at each step.

  1. Research → handbook. Mapped six commercial value dimensions (58 agents/88 capabilities, EU AI Act Article 12/50 compliance automation, LWM ontologies with zero key-person risk, a two-person core team + 50-partner network, Finnish cultural differentiation, the continuous learning loop) plus a seventh, deliberately separate academic dimension (LUT doctoral track, KTH TECoSA, Metropolia). Landed as KONSULTOINNIN_KASIKIRJA_2026.md v1.9 (§2.1) and v1.10 (§2.2), pushed as standalone commits directly to main on explicit HIC instruction — kept separate from the blog work so an unapproved draft could never ride along with an approved document.

  2. Handbook → blog draft, twice corrected on images. Asked to turn the same material into a blog post, first pass used two mismatched infographics split awkwardly by language; HIC said “wrong photos.” Checked how the site’s actual EN/FI post pairs handle images — one shared photo, not a language split — and corrected. HIC then supplied two custom infographics directly (uploaded, and separately confirmed present in Sales/IMG_6895.png / IMG_6896.png on main, byte-identical to the uploads) — those became the real hero images.

  3. 5 languages, and the HIC out-translated the draft. Asked to extend to 5 languages (EN/FI/SV/DE/FR) with Swedish “especially valuable” (the KTH-Stockholm partnership gives Swedish readers a direct stake). Drafted all 5. The HIC then supplied their own structured Swedish text (more detailed than the free translation — current-scope/operational-reach framing, a regulatory timeline, Climate IQ pricing), then the equivalent in French (“compare gemini version”) and German. All three were rewritten to match the HIC-supplied text rather than the original narrative translation — each time confirmed against the source, one typo fixed (FR: “strictly” → “strictement”).

  4. Approval, and a compliance gap caught before publish. HIC approved all 5 explicitly, with hero-image assignment (EN infographic for EN/SV/DE/FR, FI infographic for FI). Building the actual HTML surfaced a second issue on the way: DWS_BLOG_STANDARD.md §3.2.1 requires an AI-content notice (EU AI Act Article 50(4)) on any AI-drafted page covering a regulatory topic unless a named human reviewer is credited. None of the 5 pages had that credit recorded, so all 5 shipped with the notice rather than silently omitting it — a page that never says “AI wrote parts of this” is a legal claim, not a convenience.

  5. Published. All 5 pages live on the feature branch (onelifetime.world/posts/), dual-folder synced to landing-pages/onelifetime-world/, registered in the post registry (#49–53), EN+FI prepended to latestPosts (Text of the Day now points to the EN post automatically), and the nav injector re-run — which updated the previous post’s “next” link and required its own dual-folder sync, a knock-on effect that would have gone unnoticed without a full-repo diff check.

The insight, stated plainly

“Approved” is not “correct,” and the two most expensive mistakes this session were caught by checking the site’s own conventions rather than trusting a first draft. The wrong-photo mixup and the missing AI-notice would both have shipped clean — no error, no crash, nothing a build would catch — if the site’s existing EN/FI post pairs and BLOG_STANDARD.md §3.2.1 hadn’t been checked against directly. Neither was flagged by the HIC; both were caught by reading the actual repository state before building. The corollary for any agent building on this pattern: the safe default when a page is AI-drafted and covers a regulated topic is to ship the notice, not to assume approval of the content is also an implicit review credit for the authorship claim — those are different questions, and DWS policy makes the second one deliberately stricter than the EU AI Act’s own minimum.

The Problem in Numbers

Without a path from Outokumpu's ISO 14067 certificates to a CBAM annual declaration, fabricators default to the EU punitive default emission factors. Those defaults reflect worst-case non-EU production — not Outokumpu's 75%-below-average footprint.

Sources: EU default from Commission Implementing Regulation 2023/1773; ETS price EU average 2024. Illustrative basis: 1,000 tonnes steel import.
¹ CBAM is levied on the importer when goods cross an EU border from a non-EU country. Outokumpu produces in Finland, Germany, and Sweden — all inside the EU. No cross-border import occurs, so no CBAM certificate is required. Outokumpu does pay EU ETS allowances on its own production emissions; those costs are already priced into the steel. The €0 here is the fabricator’s CBAM certificate cost only.

The fabricator switching from non-EU default to Outokumpu EU steel saves up to €120,000 per 1,000 tonnes in CBAM certificates. That number belongs in their procurement meeting, not just their sustainability report. The 60-day pilot puts it there.

A Revenue Argument, Not a Sustainability Argument

Outokumpu's carbon credentials are already documented and TÜV-validated. The gap is that fabricator procurement teams cannot put a euro figure on them without compliance tooling. Once they can, Outokumpu steel has a quantified price advantage in every competitive bid against non-EU alternatives — and that advantage grows as ETS prices rise.

The pilot converts an intangible sustainability claim into a line-item CFO decision before the first account manager conversation.

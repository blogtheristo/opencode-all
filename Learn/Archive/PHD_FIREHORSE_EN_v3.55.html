    1.4 Motivating Thesis: The Carbon Balance Sheet

    The central hypothesis motivating this doctoral research is that EU climate compliance — spanning ETS, CBAM, CSRD, and the EU Taxonomy — is not a reporting problem but a ledger problem. The same physical quantity of CO2e flows through multiple regulatory touchpoints: a steel mill's ETS surrender becomes an importer's CBAM embedded emission, becomes a construction company's CSRD Scope 3 upstream disclosure, and determines their Taxonomy alignment. Current compliance tools treat each regulation as a separate reporting workflow, producing duplicated data, inconsistent factors, and ±40% Scope 3 uncertainty. This thesis proposes that carbon compliance requires double-entry bookkeeping — the same architectural solution that financial accounting adopted centuries ago. Every compliance event produces a journal entry (debit or credit) in dual units (EUR and tCO2e). The resulting carbon balance sheet provides a net carbon position that is auditable, reconcilable, and machine-readable. Why this frames the Firehorse operations research: The Firehorse Operations Model (Sections 3–13) documents the engineering infrastructure — multi-agent orchestration, edge deployment, ERP integration, regulatory intelligence — required to build a carbon balance sheet that works across 20 EU-regulated industries. Without the harness architecture (Section 14.12), the cross-industry ledger cannot maintain audit integrity. Without the MCP orchestration, the cross-regulation reconciliation cannot happen in real-time. Without the edge deployment, industrial sites cannot contribute verified emissions data. Three research questions emerge from the carbon balance sheet thesis:
  • Structural isomorphism: Is the analogy between financial double-entry bookkeeping and carbon compliance tracking formally valid, or merely metaphorical? Can the same data structures, reconciliation algorithms, and audit guarantees transfer?
  • Cross-vertical intelligence: Does a multi-industry carbon ledger produce emergent intelligence that single-vertical tools cannot replicate? Specifically: when a steel mill's verified ETS emissions replace a construction company's CBAM default values, does the accuracy improvement (hypothesized ±3% vs ±40%) materialize in practice?
  • Network effects in compliance data: At what scale (number of industries, entities, transactions) does the cross-regulation reconciliation become self-reinforcing — where adding one more industry to the ledger improves data quality for all existing industries?
    • The role of open source connectors: A carbon balance sheet requires verified data from heterogeneous industrial systems — ERP platforms (SAP, IFS, Dynamics, Visma, Jeeves), EU registries (EUTL, CBAM Transitional Registry), emission factor databases (EEA, IPCC), IoT sensors, and ESG platforms (Worldfavor, PositionGreen). This thesis argues that these connectors must be open source (MIT-licensed) for three reasons: (1) auditability — auditors must inspect the data path from source system to ledger entry, not trust a vendor's black box; (2) network effect acceleration — open source lowers adoption barriers, enabling the cross-vertical intelligence to emerge faster; (3) regulatory defensibility — EU AI Act Article 12 requires auditable AI pipelines, which open source satisfies by design. The connector architecture extends the ERP integration patterns already implemented in DWS (`src/integrations/erp/`), treating each connector as a standardized adapter (input schema → journal entry) that plugs into the ledger's append-only ingestion API. These questions extend beyond the Firehorse e-commerce operations into the domain of compliance infrastructure design — the transition from "tools that generate reports" to "infrastructure that maintains a system of record for carbon." The DWS 7 implementation (Phase 2.6, PRs 093A–093H) provides the empirical test bed.

    1.7 Ontology — Etymology and Philosophical Background

    The doctoral programme situates the Firehorse operations model inside a formal enterprise ontology — the DWS Ontology Standard (see `ONTOLOGY.md` v1.3 in the project repository). To anchor the engineering and regulatory layers in their conceptual heritage, this subsection reviews the origin of the term. Etymology. The word _ontology_ derives from two Greek roots: ὄν (ón) — "that which is", the present participle of _einai_ ("to be") — and λόγος (lógos) — "doctrine, account, rational explanation". Literally, ontology is "the doctrine of being". Philosophical background. Ontology is one of the central branches of metaphysics. In classical Greece, philosophers such as Aristotle examined what it means for something to _be_, what kinds of entities exist (objects, properties, relations, events), and what the basic structure of being is. The term _ontology_ itself was consolidated only in the 17th–18th centuries, within scholastic and early-modern philosophy, when metaphysics was systematised into distinct sub-fields. Modern usage.     • 13. Prompt Caching Architecture for AI Agents

    13.1 Engineering Principle: Cache Rules Everything

    The Firehorse Operations Model relies on long-running AI agent sessions (Claude Code) for store management, product updates, and compliance automation. Prompt caching is the foundational engineering principle that makes these sessions economically viable. Source: Thariq Shihipar (Anthropic), "Lessons from Building Claude Code: Prompt Caching Is Everything" (2026). Core insight: Prompt caching reuses computation from previous API roundtrips via prefix matching. The API caches everything from the start of the request up to each `cache_control` breakpoint. A high cache hit rate reduces costs and latency significantly.

    13.2 System Prompt Layout for Caching

    The order of prompt content matters — static content first, dynamic content last: 
    
1. Static system prompt & Tools     (globally cached)
2. CLAUDE.md / project config       (cached within a project)
3. Session context                   (cached within a session)
4. Conversation messages             (new per turn)
    Fragility warning: Cache ordering breaks when:
  • Timestamps are placed in the static system prompt
  • Tool definitions are shuffled non-deterministically
  • Tool parameters are updated mid-session (e.g., available agents)

    • 13.3 Six Engineering Rules for Agent Caching

    | Rule | Principle | Implementation | | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | 1. Static first, dynamic last | Maximize shared prefix across sessions | System prompt → CLAUDE.md → session context → messages | | 2. Use system messages for updates | Never modify the static prompt mid-session | Pass time, file changes, mode switches via `` tags in the next message | | 3. Never change models mid-session | Prompt caches are model-specific | Switching from Opus to Haiku at 100k tokens costs more than keeping Opus (cache rebuild) | | 4. Never add/remove tools mid-session | Tools are part of the cached prefix | Use tool stubs with `defer_loading: true` for optional tools; discover via ToolSearch | | 5. Design features around the cache | State transitions via tools, not tool set changes | Plan mode = system message + ExitPlanMode tool, not swapping the tool set | | 6. Fork operations share parent prefix | Compaction/summarization must reuse cache | Use identical system prompt, tools, and message history for forked requests |

    13.4 Compaction: Cache-Safe Context Forking

    When running agents approach the context window limit, compaction (summarization) must preserve the cache: 
    
WRONG: New API call with different system prompt + no tools
       → Full cache miss, expensive

RIGHT: Same system prompt + same tools + parent message history
       + compaction prompt appended as new user message
       → Cache hit on parent prefix, only new tokens are the prompt
    This requires reserving a "compaction buffer" — enough room in the context window for the compact message and summary output tokens.

    13.5 Operational Impact on Firehorse

    For DWS10.com store operations, these caching principles translate to: | Operation | Cache Impact | Practice | | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | | Product catalog updates | Long session, many file edits | Keep same model throughout | | Feed generation | Tool-heavy workflow | All tools defined at session start | | Compliance checking | Context-heavy (regulations) | CLAUDE.md cached as project config | | Multi-file sync (dual folder) | Sequential dependent operations | Chain in single session, don't fork | Monitoring: Cache hit rate is treated as an uptime metric.     Section 14.10: Agent System Hardening — Production Reliability

    The 31-agent system accumulated 6 TypeScript compilation errors and 3 test failures across module boundaries. These were not design flaws but integration debt — the kind that emerges when independently correct modules evolve at different speeds. Fixes applied:
  • SandboxContext type alignment — `SandboxContext.stage` in `types.ts` was missing the `'opensandbox'` literal that `SandboxStage` in `sandbox/index.ts` defined. This blocked agent execution in Stage 1.5 (Acer Veriton Docker).

  • HIL route wiring — The Human-in-the-Loop endpoint (`/api/hil`) for morning briefing task completion was unreachable. Captain Pre-Briefing "Done" buttons returned 404.

  • Resend batch type safety — Email service `.map()` call failed on `CreateBatchSuccessResponse` union type. Added runtime array detection.
  • Dependency hygiene — `luxon` was imported but missing from `package.json`.
    • Result: 0 TypeScript errors (was 6), 47/47 tests passing (was 44/47). Research insight: In multi-agent architectures, integration debt accumulates at module boundaries, not within modules.     14.14 Competitive Analysis: Domain-Specific vs Generic AI Harness

    A March 2026 benchmarking study compared the DWS6 harness architecture against everything-claude-code (ECC) — a 120,000-star open-source framework providing 136 skills, 30 subagents, and 60 slash commands. The fundamental trade-off: ECC optimizes for breadth (136 skills × 8 languages × 5 IDEs); DWS6 optimizes for depth (21 skills × deep EU regulatory knowledge × structural enforcement). Six competitive differentiators identified:
  • Domain-specific skills encoding EU regulation (ETS, CBAM, CSRD, EN 15978, EN 13001) — not replicable by generic skill count
  • Six-layer information architecture (L0-L6) controlling context injection at every layer — ECC operates at L2-L3 only
  • Hard gates (verification-gate, design-first, dual-folder-sync) using exit code 2 (BLOCK) — ECC skills are advisory-only
  • Trust boundary zones (GREEN/YELLOW/RED) with risk-proportionate action classification — ECC has binary secret scanning only
  • gstack workflow with enforced cognitive role separation — ECC skills are independent and unorchestrated
  • Agent-first product rule (5-gate shipping checklist: CLI, JSON output, MCP tool, Claude skill, markdown docs) — skills are product interfaces, not developer convenience
    • Research significance: At the token budget level, breadth is counterproductive for regulated domains. Loading 136 generic skills consumes context window capacity that domain-specific agents need for regulatory reasoning. The DWS6 constraint of <150 words per discipline skill is a token budget optimization enabling deeper domain reasoning. This validates the harness engineering thesis (Section 14.12): a 21-skill domain-specific harness outperforms a 136-skill generic harness when regulatory precision matters.

    14.17.1 From "GodAgent" to a Three-Tier Naming System

    The Firehorse Operations Model earlier used "GodAgent" as a single label for industry-scoped specialist agents. Anthropic's 2026-04-16 essay "Stop Building Agents, Start Building Skills" (see §14.16.4) externally validated the skill-first architecture embedded in the operating model (§14.14 Domain-Specific vs Generic AI Harness). With that validation, the single-label convention became a positioning liability: a brand, a specification, and a UI category are three distinct registers, and no single term can serve all three without diluting each. This section introduces the three-tier naming taxonomy that replaces "GodAgent" in the Firehorse operating model.

    14.17.2 The Pattern

    
Skilled <Axis>                 ← BRAND   (what we SELL)
    └─ Industrial Specialist <Axis>    ← SPEC    (what they ARE)
            └─ Industry <Axis>         ← UI      (how we LIST them)
    Three axes are currently active in the Firehorse operating model: | Tier | Agents | Workflows | Jurisdictions | | ------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | | 1 Brand | Skilled Agents | Skilled Autonomous Workflows | Skilled Jurisdictions (EU, US, Aiven) | | 2 Spec | Industrial Specialist Agents | Specialist Workflows (long-form: _Industrial Specialist Workflows_) | Regulatory Specialist Jurisdictions | | 3 UI | Industry Agents | Industrial Workflows | Jurisdictions | "Autonomous" appears only in the workflow brand tier because workflows execute on schedule or event without a human in the loop — a property the operating-model store copy must earn for every product that claims recurring compliance artefacts (see §14.16.1 Firehorse Report Engine scheduler). "Regulatory" appears in the jurisdiction spec tier because jurisdictions are defined by their regulatory surface (AI Act, GDPR, CBAM, ETS, CSRD for EU; sector rules for US; data localisation for Aiven), not by geography alone.

    14.17.3 Operations-Model Significance

    Three implications follow for the Firehorse Operations Model:
  • SKU copy discipline. The `brand:` field in `products.json` and store-page metadata must use the brand-tier term (`Skilled Agents`, `Skilled Autonomous Workflows`) to align the outward-facing positioning with the Anthropic Skills paradigm validation (§14.16.4). Spec- and UI-tier terms do not belong in hero copy.
  • Scheduler copy discipline. Firehorse Report Engine (§14.16.1) and the forthcoming Firehorse Automation module produce scheduled deliverables; their product descriptions must reach for the workflow tier ("Skilled Autonomous Workflows"), not the agent tier, to avoid under-selling the autonomy property.
  • Jurisdiction packaging. The Aegis / Sovereign Infra / Connect segmentation (see V3Thesis §10.21.5) is already organised by regulatory surface. The taxonomy names that structure explicitly: jurisdictions are branded ("Skilled Jurisdictions") rather than treated as deployment metadata.

    • 14.17.4 Count Dynamism

    The number of entries in each axis will shift as industries, workflows, and jurisdictions are added. Three acceptable patterns:
  • Open-ended counts in platform copy: `18+ Skilled Agents`, `20+ Skilled Jurisdictions` — the `+` absorbs growth.
  • Registry-anchored references in specs: "see `docs/Deputy-Agents-Architecture.md` §3 for the current Industrial Specialist Agent registry".
  • Product-scoped counts in SKU copy: "Climate Intelligence ships with 8 Skilled Agents" — precise but treated as part of the SKU specification in `products.json`, not as free-floating copy.

    • 14.17.5 Exclusions

  • Docker / infrastructure identifiers (`dws-godagent-llm` network, `dws-godagent:latest` image, label `dws-godagent`) remain unchanged; their renaming is an infra migration, not a doc change.
  • Pantheon "God Agents" on `onelifetime.world` (Greek-mythology characters — see §14.9 Climate IQ Pantheon) is a distinct brand and remains unchanged.
  • Historical records (field notes, dated folders, time-tracking and learning journals) are not retroactively edited.

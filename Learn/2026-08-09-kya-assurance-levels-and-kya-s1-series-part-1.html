What happened

The HIC supplied an approved Finnish text for part 1 of an eleven-post KYA-S1 blog series and asked for the English version to be checked against it. Checking surfaced three things, and all three were decided by the HIC in the same session.

1. The attestation tiers were renamed. The approved Finnish text used KYA-AL1 (Standard) / KYA-AL2 (Enhanced) / KYA-AL3 (Critical), while Legal/KYA_S1.md v1.3 §8 and the English blog post used Bronze / Silver / Gold. The thresholds behind the names were identical in both — annual / quarterly / continuous cadence, and 15 min / 5 min / 60 s on IDV revocation and TC4 propagation — so this was a naming conflict, not a substantive one. Surfaced rather than averaged (Rule 7). HIC decision: AL names stay. Legal/KYA_S1.md bumped v1.3 → v1.4, §8 retitled “Assurance Levels (KYA-AL)” with a Rule 12 supersession note mapping AL1=Bronze, AL2=Silver, AL3=Gold. Propagated to DWS_TRUTH_STANDARD.md row 35 (metal names now marked deprecated/FAIL) and DWS_STANDARDS_LIBRARY.md §3.3.

The rename also removes a real collision: dws10.com/partners/ runs a Lifetime Certified Partners programme whose tiers are literally named Bronze / Silver / Gold. Two unrelated ladders sharing three names in the same buyer-facing estate is a support ticket waiting to happen. The metal names now belong solely to the partner programme; assurance levels are KYA-AL*.

2. The English post was a different post, not a translation. docs/blog-drafts/kya-s1-one-signed-page.md (6 Aug) was never part of an eleven-post series and carried three chapters the approved Finnish text does not have: the public-comment window to 4 November 2026, the “why we say specification and not standard” chapter, and the engineers’ chapter positioning KYA-S1 next to SOC 2 / ISO 42001 / FedRAMP and Palantir’s MA-S2. HIC decision: the English version is the Finnish one at 100 % correspondence. The three chapters were handed to the HIC as a copy-paste block and remain intact in the superseded draft, which was not touched (Rule 12).

3. Five languages. Part 1 now exists as docs/blog-drafts/know-your-agent-kya-s1{,.fi,.sv,.de,.fr}.md. FI and EN carry Status: APPROVED on explicit HIC approval. SV, DE and FR are Status: DRAFT and carry ai_disclosure: required — the HIC approved FI and EN by name, and a general go-ahead is not approval of a specific post (Rule 13). They also need the machine-translation label at publication (docs/BLOG_STANDARD.md §3.2.1, EU AI Act Art. 50(4)).

The insight

A naming conflict between a specification and its own marketing copy is a signal that the specification is not the source anyone actually reads. The FI text was written by the HIC against what KYA-S1 means, and it independently arrived at “assurance level” — the term the audience already knows from eIDAS LoA and NIST IAL/AAL. The spec had metal tiers because the v1.0 session reached for a familiar ladder without checking that the same ladder was already spoken for elsewhere in the estate. The copy was right and the canonical document was wrong, which is the inverse of the usual drift direction and worth noticing: DWS_TRUTH_STANDARD.md §2E (“repository-first”) protects against copy drifting away from the repo, but it does not by itself catch the repo being the stale one.

What was found but not fixed

Legal/KYA_S1_v1.3.md is a second, divergent v1.3 (07.08.2026, session claude/agent-use-cases-comparison-h7vl25). It carries controls that Legal/KYA_S1.md does not — TRS-4 (atomic authorization binding), TRS-5 (fail-closed authorization), PRV-4 (human decision provenance), TC4-5 (capture precedes release), FLT-4 (bounded attribution clock) — and it is still titled “Standard”, the wording the 06.08 HIC correction removed from the other file. The two v1.3 lines never merged.

This matters beyond bookkeeping: the approved blog text cites PRV-4 under EU AI Act Article 14, and PRV-4 exists only in the unmerged file. As of this session the published series would cite a control that the canonical specification does not define.

Not resolved here. Merging two divergent canonical lines is a structural decision, and picking a winner silently is exactly the averaging Rule 7 forbids.

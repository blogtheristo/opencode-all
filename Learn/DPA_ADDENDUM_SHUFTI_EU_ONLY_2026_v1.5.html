Why we wrote this

Shufti Compliance's 2 April 2026 review surfaced four operational changes that, taken together, make the underlying DPA materially better for a sovereign-EU partner like Lifetime Oy: EU-only processing, the GCP EEA lock, the 30-day sub-processor notification window, and an explicit EU-only article in the DPA body. Shufti Compliance has already approved each of the four in writing. We're now asking Shufti Legal to incorporate those four changes into the DPA body and issue the signature copy. That signature copy, with the four changes incorporated, is the binding instrument we will sign.

The second half of this memo — the KYA Memory Binding section — is something different. It is a forward-looking partnership invitation, not a request for contract changes. We'd like Shufti to see where we're going. Lifetime Oy is building Aegis on the public KYA (Know Your Agent) Standard v1.5, which treats memory binding — tenant isolation, no shared-model promotion, immutable mutation logs — as a first-class trust property of any AI-touching service. Identity verification is one of the highest-stakes places this matters.

The KYA themes below are how we'd phrase the partnership at its mature state. They are not a redline on your DPA. They are an invitation: if any of these resonate with Shufti's roadmap, we'd love to talk about how the two products grow together.

Acknowledged Trade-Off: EU-Only Processing Mode

Shufti Compliance flagged on 2 April 2026 that EU-only mode constrains:

  • No model training on Lifetime Oy's verification data (no feedback loop to Shufti's global models).
  • No manual human verification fallback when an automated check is uncertain.

Lifetime Oy accepts this trade-off. The absence of model training and non-EU manual review aligns with our stated sovereignty posture and is, for our segment, a feature rather than a constraint.

Lifetime Oy Direction (Forward-Looking — Non-Binding)

The five themes below describe how Lifetime Oy intends to operate Aegis going forward. We share them so Shufti can decide whether and how to align — at Shufti's pace and through Shufti's own product channels. Nothing in this section creates an obligation for Shufti.

Theme 1: EU-Only as the Default Posture

1.1 Lifetime Oy intends to keep all Personal Data — biometric, document, verification metadata — within the European Economic Area for the foreseeable future.

1.2 Where a sub-processor falls outside the EEA, Lifetime Oy will look for partners who can offer an EU-only processing mode of the kind Shufti has now made available.

Theme 2: Region-Locked Infrastructure

2.1 Cloud infrastructure underlying Lifetime Oy's verification flows is expected to remain region-locked to the EEA. Shufti's GCP-EEA lock matches this expectation cleanly.

Theme 3: Reasonable Sub-Processor Notice

3.1 Lifetime Oy treats a 30-day advance notice of sub-processor changes as a healthy industry norm and welcomes Shufti's adoption of it.

Theme 4: ML-Training Restraint on Customer Verification Data

4.1 Lifetime Oy's preference is that customer verification data (biometric, document images, verification results) is not used for training, fine-tuning, or improving general-purpose ML models without explicit, separately-negotiated consent. Shufti's EU-only mode meets this preference operationally.

4.2 If Shufti at any point wishes to formalise this as a contractual term, Lifetime Oy welcomes that conversation.

Theme 5: GDPR Article 9 Legal Basis

5.1 For biometric processing (Article 9 special category data), Lifetime Oy operates on the legal basis of explicit consent (GDPR Art. 9(2)(a)), obtained from the data subject before each verification request. Lifetime Oy is solely responsible for obtaining valid consent and stands behind that responsibility in its capacity as Data Controller.

Theme 6: Breach Notification Choreography

6.1 Lifetime Oy treats a 48-hour processor-to-controller breach notification as the operational target that allows the Data Controller to comfortably meet the GDPR Article 33 statutory 72-hour deadline to the Finnish Data Protection Authority. Lifetime Oy's internal choreography is documented in scripts/daily/check-breach-deadlines.ts and runs daily.

The Bigger Conversation: KYA Memory Binding

This is the part we'd most like to discuss with Shufti.

Lifetime Oy has published the Know Your Agent (KYA) Standard v1.5 at https://dws10.com/kya-standard/. KYA is Lifetime Oy's framework for thinking about AI-touching services — the principles by which an AI agent (or AI-touching service like an IDV provider) earns and keeps trust. Section 3, Memory Binding, says three things:

(a) Tenant Isolation. A customer's data is stored, indexed, and retrieved in a way that prevents co-mingling with data from other customers. Access controls enforce tenant boundaries at the record level.

(b) No Shared-Model Promotion. A customer's data is not promoted, aggregated, or anonymised into any shared knowledge base, shared model, or shared analytics pool used to serve other customers — except with explicit consent and, even then, only as anonymised aggregated patterns that do not permit re-identification.

(c) Memory-Mutation Audit Trail. Every operation that creates, updates, supersedes, or deletes a record is captured in an immutable log: operation type, timestamp, actor, record id, reason. The log is retained for the contract term plus five years (consistent with EU AI Act Article 12 logging).

We see KYA Memory Binding as a natural complement to what Shufti has already done with EU-only mode. EU-only answers where data lives; Memory Binding answers how it lives — whether it stays bound to the customer who provided it, or quietly leaks into shared infrastructure that serves everyone.

If Shufti's product roadmap is heading in a similar direction, we'd love to compare notes. Concretely, we'd be interested in:

  • Whether Shufti can offer per-tenant audit-log export (the kind of memory-mutation log described above) on request.
  • Whether Shufti's EU-only mode includes per-tenant storage isolation at the record level.
  • Whether Shufti would be open to publishing a brief KYA-aligned posture statement (the IDV vendors who do this first will, we believe, win the EU-sovereign segment for the next decade).

None of this is a precondition for the partnership. We are signing the standard DPA. This is a conversation we'd like to keep having.

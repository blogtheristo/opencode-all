## 1. The Answer (board-approved)

> **Services-as-Software.** Software does the regulated work and delivers the artifact; the service layer just gets the customer live. That keeps the core software-led and the wrapper thin.

**Tighter (one line):**

> **Software-led, with a thin service wrapper around a product that does the actual work.**

**Tightest (investor one-liner — lead with this):**

> **Software-led, priced like labor, with software margins.**

We are **not** a services company. We are **not** classic SaaS. The product does the job and hands over the finished artifact — priced as labor, earning software margins.

## 1a. The Investor Ladder

How to deploy the positioning in a pitch. Climb only as far as the investor pulls you.

**1. Software-led, priced like labor, with software margins.**
*The Lead.* The anchor — open here. Defines the economic destination: capture labor budgets while keeping software scalability.

**2. Software-led, with a thin service wrapper around a product that does the actual work.**
*The Shape.* Deploy when they ask *how* it works. Shows the delivery mechanism without leaving the tech-first narrative.

**3. The three-way SaaS / services / Services-as-Software table (§2).**
*The Deep Dive.* Bring out **only once engaged** — data-dense validation for active due diligence, not to introduce the concept. Never open with "third category."

> **The underlying truth (board-approved):**
> *"Software does the regulated work and delivers the artifact; the service layer just gets the customer live."*

## 1b. Suomeksi (kotimainen)

> **Services-as-Software.** Ohjelmisto tekee säännellyn työn ja toimittaa valmiin lopputuotteen; palvelukerros vain saa asiakkaan tuotantoon. Näin ydin pysyy ohjelmistovetoisena ja kuori ohuena.

**Tiiviimmin (yksi lause):**

> **Ohjelmistovetoinen — ohut palvelukuori sellaisen tuotteen ympärillä, joka tekee varsinaisen työn.**

**Tiivein (sijoittajalause — aloita tällä):**

> **Ohjelmistovetoinen, hinnoiteltu kuin työ, ohjelmiston katteilla.**

Emme ole palveluyritys. Emme ole tavallinen SaaS. Tuote tekee työn ja luovuttaa valmiin lopputuotteen — hinnoiteltuna työnä, ohjelmiston katteilla.

> **Käyttövaroitus (Boardy):** älä aloita sijoittajakeskustelua ilmauksella "kolmas kategoria" / "uusi kategoria" — se voi kuulostaa siltä, *ettei kaistaa ole vielä valittu*. Aloita: "ohjelmistovetoinen, hinnoiteltu kuin työ, ohjelmiston katteilla." Käytä kolmijakoa (§2) vasta, kun sijoittaja on jo mukana ja kysyy *miten* eroatte SaaS:ista ja palveluyrityksestä.

**Käännösmuistiinpanot:**

## 2. The Three-Way Distinction

This is the load-bearing diagram. SaaS-vs-services is the wrong axis — there are three categories, not two.

| | **SaaS** | **Services company** | **DWS — Services-as-Software** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who does the work | Customer's humans, using the tool | Your humans (consultants) | **The software (Skilled Agents)** |
| What you sell | Functionality / access | Hours / expertise | **The finished artifact** (EN 15978 LCA, CSRD/CBAM gap analysis, EU AI Act conformity pack, site-safety assessment, Themis-coordinated case) |
| Priced by | Per seat | Per hour / day | **Labor unit** — per-outcome (~10–20% of consultancy day-rate) or per-agent-month (~15–30% of fully loaded FTE) |
| Scales with | Logins | Headcount (linear) | **Compute (sub-linear) — margin expands as inference cheapens** |
| Gross margin | 70–85% | 20–40% | **>80% at list** |
| Valuation multiple | High (software) | Low (1–3× rev) | **Software multiple, attacking the services/labor TAM** |

DWS 365 Skilled Agents are mapped to **business processes / the role each replaces** — which is why the output *looks* like a service. But software produces it. **Sold like services, delivered like software, priced like labor.**

---

## 3. Why the Category Is Load-Bearing (the stakes)

This is not semantics. The category determines the **economics and the valuation multiple**:

- Call ourselves a **services company** → we inherit services economics: revenue linear with headcount, 20–40% margin, 1–3× revenue multiple. The entire SAFE / Series-A thesis (>80% GM, margin expanding as foundation models commoditize, software multiple) **collapses**.
- Hold the line as **Services-as-Software** → revenue scales sub-linearly with compute, margin compounds, and we are valued as software while addressing the much larger services/labor budget.

The TAM is sized against **labor, not SaaS** (`docs/investor/data-room-2026Q2/TAM_REFRAMED.md` — *"TAM Reframed (Labor, Not SaaS)"*): EU advisory + compliance-officer headcount, not software seats. The pricing anchor is **human cost, not inference cost** (`PRICING_MODEL.md`). Both depend on this category answer being right.

## 4. The Shape — Software Core, Thin Service Wrapper

There *is* a real service layer. It is deliberately kept thin and on-ramp-only.

| Layer | What it is | Economics rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Core (the company)** | The Skilled Agents producing signed artifacts | Must stay software-economics. This is where value, margin, and the moat live. |
| **Wrapper (the on-ramp)** | Onboarding, the workshop (€150/hr), brand implementation, the pilot's setup | Exists only to *get the customer live*. Deliberately minimized — the one-click-buy / template-portfolio motion (`NORDIC_SALES_STRATEGY.md` §11) exists to stop the wrapper from growing into a consulting body shop. |

**Drift test:** if per-customer human-delivered services start to dominate revenue or pull blended gross margin below software levels, we have drifted into being a services company and must correct — productize the recurring service into an agent, or template it out of the human path.

## 5. The Moat Sits On Top

Category (this doc) and moat are separate, stacked claims:

- **Category:** Services-as-Software — software does the regulated work (this doc).
- **Moat:** EU sovereign cloud + AI — the work is done under EU jurisdiction, on top of the customer's Microsoft estate, where a US hyperscaler structurally cannot follow (`docs/field-notes/2026-06-07-ceo-review-tech-partner-wedge-eu-sovereign-moat.md`).

One line for the board: *"Services-as-Software with an EU-sovereignty moat — software does the regulated work and delivers the artifact; sovereignty is why it can't be copied from a US cloud."*

## 7. Usage Rules

- **Use the category statement (§1) in:** investor decks (category slide), the data-room thesis, board materials, partner strategy docs, and any answer to "are you SaaS or services?"
- **Lead with** "software-led, priced like labor, with software margins." **Do not lead with** "third category" / "new category" in investor conversations — bring out the three-way framing (§2) only once the investor is engaged and asking how you differ (Boardy, v1.2).
- **Do not** describe DWS as a "services company," "consultancy," "agency," or "SaaS tool" in any external artifact. If a draft does, it is wrong and gets corrected against this file (Truth Standard §3 conflict rule).
- **Do not** put the category label on store/product-card copy or hero kickers — use the Brand §22 customer-facing lines there.
- Keep this doc and the data-room economics in lockstep: if the pricing/TAM model changes category implications, update both in the same change.

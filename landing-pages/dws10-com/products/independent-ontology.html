Independent Ontology

Technology changes. Understanding must remain.

People change roles.

Systems are replaced.

AI models evolve.

But an organisation's ability to understand its own operations must not disappear with the change.

Independent Ontology provides a structure that lets an organisation keep its concepts and their relations across generations of systems, vendors and technology, and lay the foundation for managing ownership and the reasons behind decisions over the long term.

What happens when understanding is lost?

Most organisations know what was decided.

Fewer can answer, years later:

  • Why was the decision made?
  • Which observations was it based on?
  • Who owned the matter?
  • What effects were intended?
  • What did we learn?

When the link between observations, decisions and effects breaks, the organisation's ability to learn weakens too.

The same investigations are done again.

The same mistakes are repeated.

Tacit knowledge leaves with people.

Independent of technology

Independent Ontology does not tie an organisation's understanding to a single:

  • software product
  • database
  • AI model
  • vendor
  • platform

It provides a more durable structure for managing information and concepts, and a foundation for managing ownership.

Technologies can be replaced.

Understanding does not have to be replaced with them.

A shared language for the organisation

As organisations grow, parallel views of the same things emerge.

One talks about customers.

Another about processes.

A third about data.

A fourth about business goals.

Independent Ontology helps form a shared structure, so the same things are spoken about in the same way across the organisation.

Continuity of decision-making

Decision-making is not a single event.

It is a chain of observations, choices, responsibilities and effects.

Independent Ontology is designed to keep these links understandable years from now.

The goal is not to collect more data.

The goal is to keep more understanding.

Who is it for?

Independent Ontology suits organisations that want to:

  • reduce dependence on key individuals
  • keep critical knowledge capital
  • improve the quality of decision-making
  • build continuity in the middle of change
  • use AI responsibly
  • protect their competitiveness over the long term

Why now?

AI accelerates change.

New tools appear all the time.

More information is created than ever.

At the same time, keeping understanding becomes more important.

Organisations that can keep and develop their understanding as technology changes build a lasting competitive advantage.

The outcome

Independent Ontology is not a report.

It is not a database.

It is not an AI model.

It is the foundation for continuity of organisational understanding.

Continuity creates security.

Understanding creates competitiveness.

Learning enables growth.

The buying path for enterprise customers

A customer does not have to jump straight to the annual licence. The path goes step by step.

1. Prove the value

Independent Ontology Pilot, one site

LF-ONTOLOGY-PILOT · Duration 3 months

See the price in the price list

The pilot tests us, not you. It answers one question:

Do we understand your business well enough that the ontology creates measurable, lasting value for you?

At the end of the pilot we present to you:

  • A shared concept model: the core business concepts, their relations and a shared vocabulary.
  • A first ontology in your environment: ontology skeleton, concepts, relations and the first use cases.
  • Value verification: at least one important question the ontology helps answer better than your current way of working, for example identifying ownership, unifying concepts, reconciling data sources or making decisions more transparent.
  • An assessment of the business value and a recommendation on next steps.

The key measure is not how much data or how many concepts were modelled, but this: did we find business-relevant understanding that was not visible before?

The decision at the end: continue as a licence customer, extend the pilot, or stop. All three are acceptable outcomes.

The pilot does not promise automatic learning or ready-made decision tracking. It shows whether an ontology-based approach fits your business.

Credit: 100% of the pilot fee is credited against the first year of the LF-ONTOLOGY licence if the licence agreement is signed within 90 days of the pilot's end. You pay for either the pilot or the first licence year, not both.

The legal risk you can’t ignore

The US CLOUD Act (2018) allows US authorities to compel data disclosure from US-incorporated companies — even when servers are physically in the EU. AWS (Amazon), Azure (Microsoft), and GCP (Google) are all US-incorporated. Your EU customer data on these platforms is within CLOUD Act reach.

GDPR Article 48 prohibits data transfers based on foreign court orders without MLAT agreements. This creates a direct conflict: US law says hand over the data, EU law says don't. Your company is caught in the middle — unless your data is on EU-owned infrastructure where CLOUD Act doesn’t apply.

The regulatory pressure is accelerating

  • NIS2 Directive — critical infrastructure operators must demonstrate sovereign data handling and document their cloud security architecture
  • EU AI Act Article 12 — AI audit logs must have documented data residency; US cloud creates a compliance gap
  • CSRD / ESRS — supply chain transparency requirements are increasing scrutiny of cloud vendor selection
  • DORA (finance) — digital operational resilience requirements increasingly favor EU-domiciled providers
  • EUCS (EU Cloud Certification Scheme) — emerging EU cloud certification will formally require EU ownership and jurisdiction for highest sensitivity tier

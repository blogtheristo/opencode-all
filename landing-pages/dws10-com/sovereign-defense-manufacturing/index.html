Why this pattern exists

A modern vehicle line does not scale to hundreds of units a year on spreadsheets. It scales on AI — production planning, supplier-readiness analysis, quality-gate inspection, documentation generation, ramp forecasting. Every one of those touchpoints consumes the most sensitive data the program produces.

On a defense-adjacent line, that data includes, per unit, per supplier, per shift:

  • Vehicle specifications, drawings, and engineering changes (PLM / PDM)
  • Bill of materials and supplier identities
  • Production schedules, throughput, and ramp state
  • Defect, inspection, and conformity data
The gap: for a commercial car line, where the production AI runs is a procurement footnote. For a defense line it is the program's exposure surface. The default tools entering a new line are US-cloud SaaS and shadow copilots — and "security of supply across Europe" and "our production AI runs on US cloud" cannot both be true in front of the customer's security audit.

What goes wrong without governance

Foreign-jurisdiction exposure

Any AI tool in the production loop that touches US-hosted cloud or a US-owned model is, by construction, a foreign-jurisdiction reach into defense production data. The security-of-supply claim and the architecture contradict each other.

Export-control & traceability gaps

An AI action that moves, summarizes, or generates documentation from export-controlled data with no attributable, cleared human behind it is an unbounded control gap — exactly what an end-user certificate and a customer audit will probe.

NIS2 & supply-chain risk

Defense-adjacent manufacturing is in NIS2 scope, and primes push supply-chain security obligations down to the line. Shadow copilots and ungoverned SaaS turn into a remediation project right when the line should be ramping.

Ramp velocity penalty

Without a governed, attributable AI layer designed in, every security review re-opens the "where does your AI run" question by hand. The ramp slows exactly when rate matters most.

Two mechanics behind the headline

The headline names one mechanic with two parts. They are co-equal — neither stands alone.

KYA-governed agent identity

The security function authors data-class allow / redact / aggregate rules. Aegis converts export-control, NIS2, and EU AI Act obligations into machine-enforceable controls. KYA (Know Your Agent) binds every AI-mediated action to a named, trust-scored, cleared human operator. The veto path is cryptographic and auditable.

Sovereign NVIDIA infra

NVIDIA Jetson Orin (air-gapped edge), DGX Spark (on-prem), or EU-sovereign cloud (Aiven Helsinki, Scaleway Paris, UpCloud Helsinki). No US Cloud Act perimeter on defense production data. Program data never trains a foreign model. NVIDIA Connect ISV Partner. EU AI Act aligned.

Operational footer: Lifetime 24/7 EU-staffed support — a security-aware operations team on call when the line is about to act on a borderline data class before a delivery deadline. The veto path stays human, never a US ticket queue. This is what makes the two mechanics defensible in production; it is not a headline axis.

Sovereignty is not solitude — composing Themis on the new Cohere + Aleph Alpha platform

25 April 2026 · by Risto Anton Paarni

The news, in one line

Cohere (Toronto) and Aleph Alpha (Heidelberg) announced they are joining forces to build one sovereign AI platform, anchored in Canada and strengthened by Germany. Available to nations and enterprises that want trusted, non-hyperscaler AI options.

Source: BusinessWire, 24 April 2026.

Good news from a layer below us.

We sell the agent layer and the control plane. Cohere and Aleph Alpha sell the model and serving layer. Two days ago they announced they are merging the two largest non-hyperscaler model platforms in the western world into one. That platform sits directly underneath the work we do, so we read every line of the joint statement carefully.

The line that anchors the piece

“Cohere and Aleph Alpha are joining forces to build a leading sovereign AI platform, anchored in Canada and strengthened by Germany. (…) Sovereignty is not solitude. It means building with trusted partners, from a position of strength.”

From the joint statement, 24 April 2026.

That sentence is doing real work. It says the merged entity is not trying to be the only vendor in your stack. It expects to compose with other trusted partners. And that is exactly the way we sell sovereignty at DWS — one tier map, several trusted partners, each in the seat that fits them.

What the new platform actually is

Once the merger closes, the new entity is a model + serving platform with two named flagship lines:

  • Cohere North. Toronto-anchored. Canada is a trusted democracy with EU adequacy and a clean data-protection track record. Useful as a non-US default for global customers who do not need to clear the strictest EU sovereignty bar.
  • Pharia. Heidelberg-anchored. German-built weights designed for on-prem and EU-resident inference. Useful as the model substrate when the workload has to live inside the EU legal entity perimeter.

One platform, two anchors, three deployment postures. That maps onto our three sovereignty tiers without strain.

The composition, tier by tier

We treat the new platform the same way we treat every cross-tier partner: one named vendor, one capability, three different sovereignty reviews. The platform graduates per tier. Connect (Tier 3) clears first because its sovereignty bar is permissive. Sovereign Infra (Tier 2) and Aegis (Tier 1) graduate after stricter review. We are doing the same thing today with Shufti for identity verification — Shufti’s DPA cleared Connect first, the stricter tiers earn approval next. The pattern is the same.

Frame note (28.05.2026). This three-tier product segmentation is the EU-origin enterprise’s operational expression of the universal three-pillar (Data / Technology / Operational) × three-jurisdiction (EU / US / Singapore) sovereignty model. See Sovereignty Field Note #2 for the taxonomy.

Tier 3 · Connect (Global)

Fast-track to approved

Cohere North as the Canada-anchored non-US default

Connect customers want non-US, not zero-CLOUD-Act-equivalent. The new entity, anchored in Canada under standard data protection, is a clean fit. Standard DPA, no addendum gymnastics. This is where the partnership lights up first — same way Shufti lit up at Connect before the stricter tiers.

Tier 2 · Sovereign Infra (EU)

Exploration · Q3 2026

Cohere North + Pharia on Scaleway, OVH or Red Hat OpenShift

Sovereign Infra customers run regulated EU workloads — CSRD, NIS2, ETS, CBAM. The bar is EU data residency, EU personnel, no US parent in the operational chain. The new platform has to confirm EU-only processing for Pharia weights and Cohere North inference inside the customer’s EU cloud. Exploration is open Q3 2026, mirroring the way the Shufti DPA worked through the Tier 2 amendments.

Tier 1 · Aegis (Full Sovereign)

Exploration · Q3 2026

Pharia weights on Hetzner Helsinki, EU-only sub-processor chain

Aegis is the strictest tier. Defence, government, critical infrastructure. The bar is EU-incorporated everywhere in the data path, full Annex A sub-processor inventory, zero non-EU residency. Pharia weights deployed on Hetzner Helsinki on EU infra, audited end to end. Self-hosted Llama and Mistral remain the Aegis fallback while the Tier 1 sovereignty review runs. We will not flip the Aegis cell to approved until that work is done.

Where Themis fits on top of the platform

The new platform ships models and serving. It does not ship an EU AI Act Article 12 audit ledger. It does not ship an Annex III high-risk systems registry. It does not bind every inference call to a KYC-verified human operator. Those are the pieces most enterprise buyers discover the hard way, six weeks before the auditor arrives.

That is the seat Themis takes. Themis is our control plane, shipping with every Aegis deployment and available as a standalone SKU on Sovereign Infra. It implements the open KYA specification:

  • Article 12 logging. Hash-chained, append-only ledger. Six-month minimum retention, court-defensible tamper evidence.
  • Annex III systems registry. Every high-risk AI system declared, with risk classification, conformity state, and deployer evidence on file.
  • KYA policy proxy. Every inference call bound to a verified human identity, with capability-manifest enforcement and memory-mutation attestation.
  • Policy gate. Allow, deny or escalate at runtime, with a 24-hour human-review SLA when escalation fires.

Same control plane, three deployment postures: bundled with Aegis on Hetzner Helsinki, standalone on Tier 2 EU clouds, available on Connect. Whichever model platform sits underneath — self-hosted Llama, Mistral, or the new Cohere + Aleph Alpha — Themis delivers the same audit-defensible record on top.

That is the trusted-partner story we read in the joint statement, written from the layer above. They build the model platform. We build the control plane. Aegis composes both, deployed on EU infra, with the per-tier graduation tracked in the open.

Why we will not over-claim

The new entity calls itself sovereign. We respect the word and we will not stretch it on their behalf. Cohere is incorporated in Canada, which is a Five Eyes member — that is not a critique, it is a fact a Tier 1 customer reads in their own legal review. Aleph Alpha is incorporated in Germany, which is the cleanest EU posture money can buy at the model layer today.

So we will say the same thing on our pages that we say on our partner map. Connect: fast-track to approved. Sovereign Infra: exploration with a clear Q3 review. Aegis: exploration, with self-hosted weights remaining the fallback until the Tier 1 DPA clears. That is honest, and it is exactly the same shape we use for Shufti, and it will be the same shape we use for the next cross-tier partner after this one.

Nobody — not us, not the platform, not Red Hat — should sell sovereignty in a single flat row when the legal review reads three rows deep.

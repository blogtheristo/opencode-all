## Why him, why now (the wedge)

Petri has publicly argued that most Nordic startup boards optimize the **wrong endpoint** — steering as if every company is heading for an IPO or a large VC round, when in reality IPOs are under ~1% of European exits, the majority of Nordic tech exits are **trade sales without VC money**, growth is **customer-funded**, and **value is created before the money runs out**. His conclusion: boards should optimize the _real_ probable endpoint and protect **acquirability**, not install a venture-scale playbook prematurely.

**Lifetime Oy is the textbook company his thesis describes.** This is not a hyperscale moonshot needing three dilutive rounds — it is a **capital-efficient, EU-compliance deep-tech company with a clean structure and a customer-funded path**. We are not pitching against his thesis; we are pitching _from inside it_.

# Page 1 — The Company

**Lifetime Oy** (Espoo, Finland; EU) builds and sells **sovereign AI for EU regulatory compliance**. Flagship platform **DWS IQ 6** (V1.0 launched 23.02.2026) runs **18+ Skilled Agents** across **16 captain hats** covering **25 EU-regulated industries**, automating Fit for 55, CSRD, ETS, CBAM, EPBD, RED III, NIS2 and EU AI Act reporting. It is **MCP-native, CLI-accessible and EU-hosted** (Google Cloud Run, `europe-north1`), with sovereign-edge options (NVIDIA Jetson, Acer Veriton GN100).

**Why this is a Gorilla-shaped deal:**

- **Clean, simple cap table.** 100% founder-owned (Risto Anton Paarni), **no SAFEs signed, no options, no convertibles, no passive/VC shareholders**. No dilution overhang to underwrite.
- **Capital-efficient by construction.** **No human employees** — the company runs via the founder, AI agents, and partner teams. Zero payroll and zero employment liabilities mean a low, controllable burn.
- **Customer-funded growth motion.** 0 € / 30–60-day pilots as the land motion, converting to subscription. Early commercial traction in logistics at **~€7k–14k MRR per pilot**. Growth is meant to come from customers, not from the next round — exactly the model Petri argues boards should protect.
- **Real product, real storefront.** `dws10.com/buy` — 100+ SKUs priced from a single source of truth (DWS IQ 6 from €4,999; pilots €0).
- **Documented R&D base.** €594,000 of product-development cost capitalized by board decision 31.12.2025 (KPL 5:8 / IAS 38).
- **High acquirability.** Single EU entity, in-house IP (DWS IQ, Firehorse, "Lifetime World Model"), Tier-1 tech partnerships carried forward (IBM, AWS, Google Cloud, RedHat, Confluent). The one open technical item before a term sheet is the **founder→company IP assignment** (templates drafted, counsel review pending) — a known, closable gap, not a structural problem.

**Why now:** the **EU AI Act enforcement wave from 02.08.2026**, plus CSRD/CBAM/EPBD deadlines and NIS2 demand, create a forced-buy compliance market across 25 regulated industries — and a sovereign-by-design, EU-hosted vendor is positioned to win it before larger incumbents adapt.

# Page 2 — The Ask, Risks, and the Board Angle

**The ask.** An initial **~€150k SAFE** (terms in the closing plan). Proceeds fund: (1) executing the founder→company IP assignment and closing the diligence pack, (2) converting the logistics pilot motion into repeatable, referenceable subscription revenue, and (3) the 02.08.2026 EU AI Act go-to-market window. Capital goes to the moat and to acquirability — not to inflating headcount toward an IPO that the company's honest endpoint does not require.

**Honest framing (the part Petri will respect).** This is a **structurally sound, transparently governed, but early-stage** company: young V1.0 product, thin audited financials, a lean (founder-concentrated) team, and a referenceable-customer base still being built. Key-person risk is real and concentrated. We surface this deliberately — the transparency posture is unusually strong (we publish unedited third-party reviews of our own product, caveats included). "More scars than trophies" is a language we speak.

**The board angle.** Lifetime Oy has just codified a **board-work & leadership charter built directly on Petri's thesis** — optimize the real probable endpoint (trade sale / customer-funded), match governance intensity to stage (Search vs Execute), and protect acquirability rather than install premature IPO-grade machinery (`docs/LIFETIME_OY_HALLITUSTYO_JOHTAMINEN_EN_FI.md`). The company is currently founder-led (sole director); **a Gorilla-style institutional angel is exactly the first board voice this company should add** as it moves from Search to Execute. The investment thesis and the governance thesis are the same thesis.

**What we'd want from Gorilla beyond capital:** the scar tissue — disciplined endpoint-setting, trade-sale optionality, and the "don't professionalize too early" judgment that Petri writes about.

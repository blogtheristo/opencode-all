## Why Frankenburg-first (not NIF-first)

The wedge's whole value depends on sequence. Salm is both a target customer and a NIF board director; leading with the fund would invert the leverage and trip the governance wall. So:

- **The commercial relationship is the asset.** A NIF director who has _used_ Aegis at Frankenburg is the strongest possible internal advocate later — that credibility only exists if the customer relationship comes first.
- **The governance wall protects the play.** A NIF director rightly separates his Frankenburg buying decision from any NIF investment decision. Keeping the email purely commercial respects that and keeps both tracks clean.
- **Small, concrete, credible first step.** The ask is entry-path step 1 from the case: a sovereignty + EU AI Act gap assessment on the Riga/Ādaži data estate. Not a platform sell — a scoped diagnostic a scaling defence prime needs anyway.

## Strategy

- **Lead with the FieldFoundry scale problem**, in his own words: replicating Riga → Ādaži → Estonia/UK/Poland means replicating the _data + compliance + agent-governance_ posture per site, per jurisdiction. That is the exact shape Aegis deploys in.
- **Anchor on sovereignty + EU AI Act**, the two non-negotiables for an EU-funded, Commission-visible defence programme: zero CLOUD Act exposure, EDIP Article 9 eligibility, Article 12 audit trails.
- **Attach the worked case, not a brochure.** `CASE_FRANKENBURG_RIGA.md` maps Aegis capability-by-capability onto his own production system — it reads as "we've done the homework on your factory," not a generic pitch.
- **Ask for the assessment, not a purchase.** The 30-minute scoping call is to frame a gap assessment, keeping commitment low and value concrete.
- **Warm intro over cold send.** Use the ~29 mutuals (Keith / Seppo first) to relay the note rather than emailing cold.

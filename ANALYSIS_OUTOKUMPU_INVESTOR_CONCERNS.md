# Analysis of Outokumpu Workflow Investor Concerns

## 1. Specific Investor Concerns Addressed

Based on the DWS6 documents, the investor concerns about Outokumpu workflow can be categorized as follows:

**A. Architecture/Plan vs. Business Case**
The Outokumpu workflow presents a business case approach but is structured more as an architecture demonstration. The partnership offer (v3.0) is a pilot with zero cost to Outokumpu, focusing on CBAM compliance automation rather than direct commercial impact. The Tier-1 sovereignty pilot (documented in `docs/field-notes/2026-05-25-tier1-sovereignty-pilot-outokumpu-dgx-t5000.md`) is presented as an annex to the CBAM partnership rather than a standalone business case, making it seem like architecture/plan rather than business impact.

**B. Template vs. First Attempt**
The approach treats Outokumpu as a unique pilot scenario rather than a replicable template. The workflow is seen as being at a stage where it's not yet ready for replication, as it focuses on a specific use case (CBAM compliance + sovereignty) rather than demonstrating a scalable business model.

**C. Target Customer Specificity**
While the initial Outokumpu offer targets specific CBAM compliance use cases, investors are concerned that the approach is too broad, not focused enough on specific revenue-generating models.

**D. Partner Ecosystem Complexity**
The partnership involves multiple vendors (Microsoft Azure, TCS, Solita, Nortal, Tietoevry) that create a complex ecosystem, raising questions about dependency on US-based vendors that the sovereignty approach is intended to address.

**E. Funds Allocation Clarity**
The €2M investment question raises concerns about how the funding will be specifically allocated to achieve business impact, as current approaches are more architectural than revenue-generating.

**F. Defensibility from Delivery**
Investors want to see tangible business outcomes rather than just architecture. The emphasis is on demonstrating "business case" that generates actual value as opposed to just technical capabilities.

## 2. First Measurable Business Impact

The first measurable business impact from the Outokumpu workflow would be:

**CBAM compliance value generated** - Demonstrated cost savings for fabricators switching from non-EU default to Outokumpu steel (€120,000 saved per 1,000 tonnes in CBAM certificates). This concrete value proposition shows the commercial viability of the platform.

**Platform demonstration** - The ability to automate CBAM declarations as an actual tool rather than a concept, with first pilot customers showing willingness to convert from Navigator to Premium tiers.

## 3. Addressing Investor Concerns About Business Case vs. Architecture

To address investor concerns, the approach should:

**Focus on Clear Value Propositions**: Clearly demonstrate how CBAM compliance tooling directly addresses CFO-level decisions, not just sustainability decisions. The €120k savings example serves as a concrete ROI showing immediate value.

**Build on Success Metrics**: Present pilot success as demonstration of platform capability and customer interest, with clear roadmap showing how to scale beyond CBAM to other compliance areas.

**Address Template Concerns**: Position the Outokumpu approach as first-in-class model for EU sovereign compliance rather than just a test, with sovereignty pilot as foundation for replicable approaches.

## 4. What Makes This Approach Viable from Investor Perspective

Several factors make this approach viable:

**Regulatory Compliance Demand**: CBAM, CSRD, EPBD and other regulatory pressures create a market need that platform addresses directly, with EU regulatory tailwind making compliance automation a necessity for large EU companies.

**EU Sovereign Infrastructure**: The EU sovereignty framework (EUDI Wallet and Digital Euro) provides unique differentiator with "sovereign ownership rule" as structural competitive advantage.

**Platform Scalability**: Platform covers 25 EU-regulated industries providing multiple revenue streams, with multi-industry coverage matrix showing value beyond CBAM.

**Clear Traction**: The investor memo documents 2 contracted enterprise pilots ($59,900 booked) and target of €900k ARR by end of 2026 with projected LTV of €70k-€120k per logo.

**Strategic Timing**: Approach aligns with EU regulatory timeline where compliance becomes mandatory for 2025-2028, making the timing favorable for early adoption.

The approach's vulnerability according to investors is that it's currently more architectural and strategic rather than demonstrating immediate, replicable business outcomes that generate returns. It must transition from architecture demonstration to actual business impact to make investors comfortable with defensibility from delivery.

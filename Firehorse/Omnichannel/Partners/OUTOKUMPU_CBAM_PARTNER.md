## Who They Are

Outokumpu is the world's largest stainless steel producer by capacity — and the only stainless steel
maker operating a chromium mine inside the EU (Kemi, Finland). They are not a typical CBAM sufferer.
They are a **CBAM beneficiary** — EU-based, ultra-low-carbon, 97% recycled material, 75% lower
footprint than industry average.

Their stainless steel is already CBAM-ready from the producer side:

- First stainless steel producer to issue **product-specific carbon footprint certificates** (ISO 14067, TÜV validated, Scope 1–3)
- Committed to 1.5°C pathway, 42% emissions reduction by 2030 vs 2016
- Actively lobbying to expand CBAM scope to all steel-intensive downstream goods by 2027

**2025 financials:** Weak year, Europe business in loss. Margin pressure from non-EU competition is
acute. CBAM enforcement is their strategic ally — they want it to work. EVOLVE strategy 2026–2030
targets cost competitiveness + customer collaboration + advanced materials.

---

## The Core Insight: They Are Upstream, Their Problem Is Downstream

Outokumpu does not need CBAM compliance software for themselves. They are EU-domiciled, low-carbon,
already reporting. Their strategic pain is different:

**Outokumpu's real problem**: Their downstream customers — EU manufacturers, fabricators, construction
companies — are either:

1. Not yet capturing the **CBAM advantage** of buying from Outokumpu vs Asian/non-EU steel
2. Struggling to use Outokumpu's carbon certificates in their own CBAM declarations
3. Still buying from cheaper non-EU suppliers without pricing in the true CBAM cost differential

**Outokumpu's strategic desire**: Every downstream customer should be able to say
_"I buy from Outokumpu, therefore my CBAM liability is near-zero."_ That makes Outokumpu steel
worth a premium. But this only works if the downstream customer can actually do the CBAM math
and present it to their importer/compliance team.

## Competitive Landscape for Outokumpu

Outokumpu is being displaced by:

- **Chinese stainless steel** (lower cost, historically lower carbon accountability)
- **Indian producers** (growing capacity, CBAM scope not yet full)
- **Turkish mills** (large volume, CBAM-exposed after 2027 scope expansion)

Under full CBAM enforcement, an EU manufacturer importing from these sources pays:

- CBAM certificates on the embedded emissions differential
- Administrative compliance burden
- Audit risk

Buying from Outokumpu avoids all three. But buyers currently lack tools to **calculate** and
**document** this differential in a compliant CBAM declaration. That is the DWS IQ 6 gap fill.

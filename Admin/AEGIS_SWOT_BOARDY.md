## Purpose

Honest SWOT on Aegis getting funded. Boardy review style — no sugar-coating, prod-safety mindset applied to the go-to-market and fundraising posture.

---

## Strengths

- **€59,990 one-time + €999/mo is infra margin positive from day one** — rare at this stage.
- **14× cheaper than Palantir** is a real anchor if you can defend it in a demo.
- **Finnish sovereign hosting is structurally differentiated** — no US competitor can claim CLOUD Act-free.
- **EDIP DEF 2.0 timing is real.** Q2/Q3 2026 sub-fund call creates urgency that most defence software pitches don't have.
- **Article 12 + Article 26 evidence infrastructure is a genuine gap in the market** — no EU-native competitor has it.

## Weaknesses

- **Zero defence customers.** KELA and Taksi Helsinki are civilian. Investors backing defence software want at least one government or defence contractor reference.
- **"Design partner stage" for defence procurement reporting is a red flag in your own docs** — that's pre-revenue in the vertical that justifies the thesis.
- **Dual-use export control agent is listed as "live" but no customer has stress-tested it** in a real procurement scenario.
- **€59,990 one-time is a long sales cycle.** Enterprise defence procurement doesn't close in weeks.

## Opportunities

- **NIDR framework is genuinely novel** — if you can get one Finnish government body to acknowledge it, even informally, that's a proof point no one else has.
- **DEFINE Finland accelerator application (March 2026)** — if accepted, that's your defence credibility anchor.
- **BAE Systems Suomi outreach (Jukka letter)** — one warm conversation with a Tier 1 defence prime changes the entire narrative.

## Threats

- **Palantir, Helsing, and Rheinmetall's software division are all moving into EU AI governance.** You have a 12–18 month window before they have a compliant EU-sovereign product.
- **If EDIP DEF 2.0 sub-fund criteria don't align with Aegis positioning, the timing thesis collapses.**
- **Article 99 cleanup is urgent** — if an investor or defence buyer catches the old language before you sweep it, credibility damage is hard to recover.

---

## Boardy's Bottom Line

Aegis has a **real structural wedge** (sovereign hosting + Article 12/26 evidence infra) and **real timing** (EDIP DEF 2.0). It does not yet have a **real defence customer**. Close that gap — even one design-partner LOI from a Finnish government body or BAE Suomi — and the fundraise narrative flips from "compelling thesis" to "de-risked wedge."

**Priority fixes before next investor conversation:**

1. Land one defence / government design partner (even unpaid, time-boxed).
2. Sweep Article 99 references from all public + pitch materials.
3. Convert "live" claims on the dual-use export agent into a logged, dated stress-test with a real scenario.
4. Confirm EDIP DEF 2.0 sub-fund criteria alignment in writing before committing the timing thesis to investors.

# Founder Video Script — Deep SAGE 2026

The Deep SAGE 2026 application asks for a **3-minute** founder video. The reviewer pool
(TIS Inc., NUS Enterprise, regional investors) explicitly scores executive presence,
personality, scope, and cohort-fit reasoning — not just product compression.

This is the **v8 long cut**: 6 beats, ~535 words, lands at ~3:40 with conversational pacing.
Per Risto's direction ("make 3,5 minutes version first then cut corners"), this version is
built for substance density. A 3:00 final cut will be derived from this version, not from
compressing v6/v7 — substance first, length second.

## Script

### [0–38s · Open]

> Hello — my name is Risto Anton Päärni. I'm the founder of Lifetime Oy's DWS Compliance Intelligence ecosystem, based in the Nordics and headquartered in Helsinki, Finland. Twenty years building European industrial software brought us here: shared intelligence across twenty-five industries, powered by our Lifetime World Models.

### [0:38–1:32 · What we discovered — three industries, one sovereign frame]

> What became clear this spring is that we were not just building climate compliance — we were building sovereign compliance infrastructure for three industries that need it most: steel, construction, and dual-use.
>
> Steel: Outokumpu — Europe's stainless-steel anchor at one-point-six tonnes of CO₂ per tonne. CBAM-advantaged, with compliance reporting mandatory, and stainless itself is dual-use — civilian and defence in the same shipment.
>
> Construction: NCC — fifty-five billion in revenue, twelve thousand subcontractors, critical infrastructure for European cities.
>
> And dual-use, where our DWS Aegis line carries the sovereignty perimeter — connected systems, edge sensors, supplier data, all reasoning inside a jurisdiction the auditor and the regulator already sit inside. Not flag-wave sovereignty. The architectural kind.
>
> The DWS Compliance Intelligence ecosystem is built exactly for that.

### [1:32–2:22 · The ecosystem, as a thesis]

> The ecosystem is double-entry bookkeeping for industrial carbon. Every CBAM filing, every compliance report, every carbon payment becomes a journal entry — signed, replayable, regulator-grade. The Lifetime World Model is one source of truth, with every enterprise running its own version. The LLM is a witness in our architecture, not the source of truth. The journal is. That distinction is the whole product.

### [2:22–2:42 · Proof, honestly]

> Four product lines. The DWS IQ compliance ecosystem: Outokumpu pilot under review, NCC in construction. Firehorse: services for DWS IQ compliance work, distributed through Salesforce Agentforce. Lifetime Fleet: Control Rooms on DGX Spark and NVIDIA edge compute — six paying customers, seven thousand monthly recurring, one hundred percent retention since launch, and our path for robotics and sensors to the field. Situation Room: board-level oversight for enterprises keeping tight control of operations and projects. Under all four: fifty production agents doing the work.

### [2:42–3:12 · What moved since May — the agent trust standard]

> And since we applied, one thing accelerated. Autonomous agents are starting to transact — pay, order, commit — and no one regulates the agent. We already run the answer in production: Know Your Agent — identity, a revocable mandate, an audit trail a regulator can replay. This summer we put it on the European funding track as KYA-2, the open conformance standard for AI agents that transact — EIC Accelerator application in motion, patent track underway. The same standard is exactly what agentic commerce in Singapore and Japan will need. That is the asset we bring into this cohort.

### [3:12–3:32 · Cohort fit]

> Why Deep SAGE: TIS Inc. is the route into Japanese manufacturing. We're a founder-led Nordic core with a hundred-plus contracted engineers across two certified partners — deliberately too lean to hire a full-time CRO or COO yet. Twelve weeks of cohort-level senior expertise, in the quarter this market turns mandatory, is leverage we cannot buy any other way.

### [3:32–3:50 · Ask + close]

> We're here because the program's fractional CXO format fits our stage, and the cohort timing lines up with the 2026-to-2028 regulatory window. We want to use the cohort to sharpen the Singapore entry and put the right senior operator next to that market shift.

## Shoot notes

|                |                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Camera         | Laptop webcam or phone on a stack of books, eye-level. Don't go handheld.                                                   |
| Light          | One window in front of you, not behind. No ceiling spotlight on the forehead.                                               |
| Audio          | AirPods or any wired earbud mic beats built-in laptop mic. Quiet room.                                                      |
| Wardrobe       | Plain collar shirt or dark sweater. No logos.                                                                               |
| Tool           | **Loom** (free), record → trim → unlisted link. Don't post to YouTube — Loom gives view analytics and you control deletion. |
| Takes          | Max 3. After take 3 you start performing the script instead of saying it.                                                   |
| Eyes           | Look at the **camera lens**, not at yourself in the preview. Tape a tiny arrow above the lens if needed.                    |
| Energy         | Finnish-direct + steady eye contact reads as senior. American pitch-bro energy at age-and-experience would read off-brand.  |
| Pauses         | Take a small breath between each of the 6 sections — viewers will not feel the silence, you will.                           |
| Gestures       | One small physical gesture per section — hand on table for proof beat, small nod on close. Not theatrical, just human.      |
| If you stumble | Finish the sentence, breathe, continue. Loom lets you trim mid-take.                                                        |

## Lessons from the v1→v6 loop (for future founder-video drafts)

1. **Length is set by the brief, not by the editor's preference.** Boardy is sharp on line-level surgery but biased toward Demo Day tightness. Pin the length to what the application form actually asks for; let editors work inside that frame.
2. **Founder ambition signals outrank external "polish."** When the founder has deliberately put a term in the opener and the editor says "cut it as internal language," default to the founder. The editor doesn't know what the founder is willing to defend in the room; the founder does.
3. **Time budget is substance budget.** "Saving 10 seconds" without saving complexity is corner-cutting, not editing. If a beat is shorter than the previous version, it must be _clearer_, not just _shorter_. v5 failed this test.
4. **Three personality anchors do the heavy lifting:** the customer-voice quote (beat 2), the honest MRR admission (beat 4), and the opinionated technical line (beat 3). Don't let edits sand any of these off.

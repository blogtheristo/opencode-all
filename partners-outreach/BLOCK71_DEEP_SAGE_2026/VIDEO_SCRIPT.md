# Founder Video Script — Deep SAGE 2026

The Deep SAGE 2026 application asks for a **3-minute** founder video. The reviewer pool
(TIS Inc., NUS Enterprise, regional investors) explicitly scores executive presence,
personality, scope, and cohort-fit reasoning — not just product compression.

This is the **v8 long cut**: 6 beats, ~535 words, lands at ~3:40 with conversational pacing.
Per Risto's direction ("make 3,5 minutes version first then cut corners"), this version is
built for substance density. A 3:00 final cut will be derived from this version, not from
compressing v6/v7 — substance first, length second.

## Script

### [0–38s · Open]

> Hello — my name is Risto Anton Päärni. I'm the founder of Lifetime Oy's DWS Compliance Intelligence ecosystem, based in the Nordics and headquartered in Helsinki, Finland. Twenty years building European industrial software brought us here: shared intelligence across twenty-five industries, powered by our Lifetime World Models.

### [0:38–1:32 · What we discovered — three industries, one sovereign frame]

> What became clear this spring is that we were not just building climate compliance — we were building sovereign compliance infrastructure for three industries that need it most: steel, construction, and dual-use.
>
> Steel: Outokumpu — Europe's stainless-steel anchor at one-point-six tonnes of CO₂ per tonne. CBAM-advantaged, with compliance reporting mandatory, and stainless itself is dual-use — civilian and defence in the same shipment.
>
> Construction: NCC — fifty-five billion in revenue, twelve thousand subcontractors, critical infrastructure for European cities.
>
> And dual-use, where our DWS Aegis line carries the sovereignty perimeter — connected systems, edge sensors, supplier data, all reasoning inside a jurisdiction the auditor and the regulator already sit inside. Not flag-wave sovereignty. The architectural kind.
>
> The DWS Compliance Intelligence ecosystem is built exactly for that.

### [1:32–2:22 · The ecosystem, as a thesis]

> The ecosystem is double-entry bookkeeping for industrial carbon. Every CBAM filing, every compliance report, every carbon payment becomes a journal entry — signed, replayable, regulator-grade. The Lifetime World Model is one source of truth, with every enterprise running its own version. The LLM is a witness in our architecture, not the source of truth. The journal is. That distinction is the whole product.

### [2:22–2:36 · Proof, honestly]

> Four product lines. The DWS IQ compliance ecosystem: Outokumpu pilot under review, NCC in construction. Firehorse: services for DWS IQ compliance work, distributed through Salesforce Agentforce. Lifetime Fleet: Control Rooms on DGX Spark and NVIDIA edge compute — seven thousand monthly recurring across Uber Finland, Kela, NCC, Bolt at one hundred percent retention, and our path for robotics and sensors to the field. Situation Room: board-level oversight for enterprises keeping tight control of operations and projects.

### [2:36–2:56 · Cohort fit]

> Why Deep SAGE: TIS Inc. is the route into Japanese manufacturing. We're a six-person Nordic team — too small to hire a full-time CRO or COO yet. Twelve weeks of cohort-level senior expertise, in the quarter this market turns mandatory, is leverage we cannot buy any other way.

### [4:31–4:55 · Ask + close]

> We're applying to Deep SAGE 2026 because the program's fractional CXO format fits our stage, and the cohort timing lines up with the 2026-to-2028 regulatory window. We want to use the cohort to sharpen the Singapore entry and put the right senior operator next to that market shift.

## Where the personality is — don't sand it off in delivery

| Beat | What it signals | Don't smooth out |
|---|---|---|
| "twenty years building software for European industry — its manufacturers, its enterprises, its public sector" | Seniority + breadth, not founder-bro hustle | Stay deliberate; let the three nouns land separately |
| "per-shipment, per-tonne, per-euro — and soon per-transaction" | You see the regulatory arc, not just today's snapshot | Pause one beat before "and soon per-transaction" |
| "shared intelligence across the twenty-five most affected industries through our Lifetime World Models" | This is not a single-vertical product; it's an ecosystem | Don't rush — this is the ambition claim |
| "Steel: Outokumpu… Construction: NCC… And dual-use, where our DWS Aegis line carries the sovereignty perimeter" | Three concrete industries, three named anchors. Not a vague TAM. | Slow the cadence — each industry gets its own breath |
| "Not flag-wave sovereignty. The architectural kind." | Sovereignty as engineering property, not as passport stamp. Differentiates you from defensive EU-vs-US framing. | Land both halves; do not run them together |
| "The Lifetime World Model is one source of truth, with every enterprise running its own version" | Multi-tenant, per-customer instantiation — not a single shared model. | Clear pause between "one source of truth" and "with every enterprise" |
| "the LLM is a witness in our architecture, not the source of truth. The journal is" | Opinionated technical judgment | Eye contact straight into lens; let "The journal is" land alone |
| "I'll say it openly — €7K MRR is small" | Founder honesty, Finnish directness | Pause one beat after "openly" |
| "We cannot absorb a full-time CRO or COO yet" | Self-awareness of stage | Don't apologize, just state it |

## Lessons from the v1→v6 loop (for future founder-video drafts)

1. **Length is set by the brief, not by the editor's preference.** Boardy is sharp on line-level surgery but biased toward Demo Day tightness. Pin the length to what the application form actually asks for; let editors work inside that frame.
2. **Founder ambition signals outrank external "polish."** When the founder has deliberately put a term in the opener and the editor says "cut it as internal language," default to the founder. The editor doesn't know what the founder is willing to defend in the room; the founder does.
3. **Time budget is substance budget.** "Saving 10 seconds" without saving complexity is corner-cutting, not editing. If a beat is shorter than the previous version, it must be *clearer*, not just *shorter*. v5 failed this test.
4. **Three personality anchors do the heavy lifting:** the customer-voice quote (beat 2), the honest MRR admission (beat 4), and the opinionated technical line (beat 3). Don't let edits sand any of these off.

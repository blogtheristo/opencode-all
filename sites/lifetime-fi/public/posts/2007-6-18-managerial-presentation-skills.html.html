Managerial presentation skills

Published on June 18, 2007

Business communication is the most important skill for an entrepreneur. When considering Business Angel meeting, or Customer Sales meeting your presentation skills make the difference to win them to your side.


Designing the presentation as a whole

        A1. Create listing of main ideas on an agenda chart

"preview" chart outlines your presentation. It serves as a presentation's "table of contents". Items in the preview should be structural signposts for the following pages.

        A2. Provide evidence on back-up charts

 Rest of the charts in your presentation are made up of support, or "back-up" visuals.

        A3. Use message titles and stand-alone sense

Use the head line to reinforce the main concept in that visual. Create visual so that someone seeing the chart first time will understand it "stand-alone".

        A4. Provide effective transitions between each visual slide

When designing the visuals for a tell/sell presentation as a whole, think about how you going to provide transitions between each visual.

Transition technique #1: Create consistency in headings and subheadings.

Transition technique #2: Use Repeated use of agenda slide "box" & "highlight / dim".

Transition technique #3: Use of trackers to show attendees presentation structure and where we are going. A tracker is a shortened version of the main point on your agenda chart.

        A5. Use presentation software and necessary equipment for the showcase.

An effective presentation includes use of presentation software.

Also consider using flip charts, LCD  /CRT projectors and overheads to show visuals and further information.

 

The structure of a good sales presentation include 

1. An opening

Open the presentation with a persuasive "grabber". Make positive first impression with the main message for the first minute. Deliver the main message in the opening. Group main messages in descending order. Leave the second most important message to the last. see my entry Audience memory curve from wikipedia.org.

2. A preview of the main points

briefly show "table of contents" for the presentation. 

3. Clearly demarcated main points

In the following charts show main points in descending order. Less charts is many times better. Avoid wordiness.

4. a closing

When reaching end of the presentation mark second most important thing in the ending. Get Feedback with Q & A session. Call to Action. Goodwill ending. Avoid pitfalls. 

 Designing each individual chart

B1. Design and implement graphs to show quantitative data, such as financial information, marketing projections, or operations analyses. Chart types include Pie, Bar, Line, Column, Correlation, etc.

B2. Design and implement diagrams to show nonquantative concepts

Steps in a process , Flows(time, Command, Organisation, Process), Cause and effect, change, interrelation, Barriers/penetration. Chart types include process charts, drawings, arrow-head chart etc. [adapted from G.Zelazny, Say it With Charts]

B3. Use word charts to show main ideas of the business presentation. Word charts are not word-for-word scripts that echo what you are saying throughout the presentation. Instead, reserve word chart for key messages you really want to emphasize and listeners to remember. Avoid overload. Rule of thumb "six by six rule" - six lines of text, six words in a line. Sometimes you may find it necessary to break this rule, but don't do it in every slide.

B4. Use typography effectively

Typography - including font, size, use of boldface, italics, capitals, and so forth-is an important feature of both graphic and word charts. Use standard capitalization. Avoid letter junk.

B5. Editing each chart

Avoid overload. Delete chart junk.

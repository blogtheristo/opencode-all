  <div src="v5"><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">A clip from Exchange the HKEx&nbsp; Newsletter, October 2007</p><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">Foreign Listings on Global Exchanges</p><em><font size="1" face="HelveticaNeue-LightItalic"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">12 <font size="1" face="HelveticaNeue-LightItalic">Companies with a foreign controlling share holding of over 30 per cent.</font></p></font></em><font size="1" face="Galliard-Roman"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">34 October 2007</p></font><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">For HKEx, foreign companies are defined as those incorporated overseas and which have a majority of their businesses outside Hong Kong and Mainland China. Under this definition, HKEx has only eight foreign companies listed at the end of September 2007. However, under the WFE definition, 72 per cent of companies (869 in number) listed in HKEx at the end of September 2007 were foreign companies &mdash; companies incorporated in places outside Hong Kong and Mainland China &mdash; which contributed in aggregate 28 per cent of total equity trading in the first nine months of 2007.</p><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">HKEx does not classify foreign companies by their place of incorporation because a substantial proportion of domestic companies listed in Hong Kong have redomiciled since the 1980s. The most popular places for redomicile have been the tax heavens &mdash; Bermuda (currently 38 per cent of all Hong Kong-listed companies) and Cayman Islands (33 per cent).</p><p>In another dimension, there are over 100 foreign companies listed in Hong Kong if foreign ownership is used as the basis for identification. These include over 50 Taiwanese companies and over 50 others <font size="1" face="HelveticaNeue-Light">&nbsp;</font><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light">from various jurisdictions such as Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, Thailand,</font><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light"> Australia, the US and the UK.</font><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light"> </font></p></font><p><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light">Although domestic companies in Hong Kong include Mainland companies under HKEx's definition, the Mainland dimension of the Hong Kong market in fact constitutes a significant &ldquo;ex-Hong Kong&rdquo;element. Albeit that Hong Kong is part of China, the Hong Kong financial market is separate from the Mainland market under the One-Country-Two-Systems principle. The first Mainland-incorporated enterprise (H-share company) was listed in Hong Kong in 1993. At the end of September 2007, there were 143 H-share companies listed in Hong Kong, constituting 12 per cent by number of listed companies, 27 per cent by market capitalisation and 44 per cent by equity turnover value for the first nine months of 2007. Apart from the H-share companies, there are the red chips &mdash; companies incorporated outside Mainland China but controlled by Mainland government authorities &mdash; and the Mainland private enterprises incorporated outside Mainland China. The entire Mainland dimension of HKEx &mdash; H shares, red chips and non-H-share private enterprises &mdash; constituted in aggregate 32 per cent of listed companies by number, 58 per cent by market capitalisation and 67 per cent by equity turnover value for the first three quarters of 2007.</font></p><font size="2" face="DINCond-Black" color="#869ba9" style="color: rgb(134, 155, 169);"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;"><font size="1" face="Galliard-Roman"></font></p></font><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">The Hong Kong market has been successful in establishing itself as an international listing and trading platform for Mainland enterprises. Now, it has gone beyond that to be well regarded as an &ldquo;international home market for Mainland businesses&rdquo; &mdash; attracting the listing of other ex-Hong Kong/ Mainland enterprises with businesses in the Mainland, eg Taiwan-controlled companies operating in the Mainland. Ranked 7th globally by market capitalisation at the end of September 2007, the&nbsp; Hong Kong market is an international financial centre well recognised by the world. Its attractiveness to Mainland enterprises, and potentially other overseas enterprises, lies in its substantial capital raising capacity stemming from a good combination of local and international market participants, a sound legal and regulatory framework that provides a high level of investor protection, a broad and balanced retail and institutional investor base, efficient information dissemination and communication&nbsp; channels and strong intermediary service support. With its current scale and international stature, the Hong Kong market may be able to attract more foreign issuers.</p></font><font size="5" face="HelveticaNeue-Bold" color="#00849a" style="color: rgb(0, 132, 154);"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">Conclusion</p></font><font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light"><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">Statistics show that most exchanges mainly list and trade their domestic companies. </p><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">London and New York are the main exceptions in attracting significant listing and trading of foreign companies. Nevertheless, the home market effect tends to dominate in the listing and trading of stocks.</p><p align="left" style="text-align: left;">HKEx has successfully established itself as the home market and capital formation centre, with substantial international participation, for Hong Kong and Mainland enterprises. With its current market scale, international investor base, sound regulatory regime and professional intermediary <font size="3" face="HelveticaNeue-Light">service support, the Hong Kong market may be able to attract a wider range of foreign issuers.</font></p></font></div>

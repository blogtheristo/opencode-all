<h2><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">&nbsp;</span>&nbsp;</h2>
<h2></h2>
<h2>The Learning Organization</h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">Learning organizations have continued to remain in the centre of management studies since 90&rsquo;s. The dynamics of operational environment require organizations to pay all the time growing attention to maintaining their competitive advantage. Global competition, differing customer needs and technical development all make it harder for the companies to keep going in the phase.</span></p>
<h2><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">The Semantic Web Promise</span></h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;"><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">How can Semantic Web and Web 2.0 then assist companies in maintaining their status &ndash; and even to grow? There are numerous examples of companies succeeded in for example engaging customers with the help of Web 2.0 services. This is not, however, even near the whole spectrum of possibilities that Web 2.0 offers for companies in data management, training, product development and so on.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">It is not enough for the companies to establish a fan page in Facebook. It is of course a good start <span class="full-image-float-right ssNonEditable"><span><img src="http://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/54c48881e4b0d7a7fa7eddf4/1425316459196-YSOBFZFP91UL3IZI4WB2/siltakatu_2010_023.jpg" alt="" /></span><span class="thumbnail-caption" style="width: 175px;">Pictures Copyright Kalle P&auml;&auml;rni Productions 2010.</span></span>but, according to Sirkka J&auml;rvenp&auml;&auml; from Texas University (link to: </span><a href="http://www.gemilo.com/yritysblogi/sosiaalisen-median-trendeja-tyomaailmasta/" target="_blank"><span style="font-family: Calibri; color: #0000ff; font-size: small;"><span style="text-decoration: underline;">http://www.gemilo.com/yritysblogi/sosiaalisen-median-trendeja-tyomaailmasta/</span></span></a><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">), the whole process behind Web 2.0 should be planned and implemented well. Usage of Web 2.0 tools and services should, of course, be aligned with corporate strategy and not to be built as a separate part, neither.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">Why I mentioned learning organizations in the first chapter?</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">This is because learning and adoption of new ways to work are needed in order to grasp the idea and possibilities that Web 2.0 offers for companies. On the other hand Web 2.0 can serve as a great catalyst for learning.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-family: Calibri; font-size: small;">To take an example, Wikis can ease flow of information, function as a place where employees can share their ideas and thoughts that company can then take advantage of. And this all can lead to increased employee engagement and thus greater productivity.</span>&nbsp;</p>

<div><span style="font-size: small;"><br /></span></div>
<div><em><span style="font-size: small;">In the previous entry I made a notion that adoption of new ways to work is needed in order to exploit Web 2.0 services. Implementing Web 2.0 in any company means shifting from traditional top-down design to more collaborative ways of working and thinking, a shift that many companies have already seen to be fundamental for their success. By this I mean that in order for companies to survive in the postmodern business world they need to take a step forward to more open communication towards their customers and employees. Web 2.0 cannot be, however, considered to be an easy step to take.</span></em></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><br /></span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">Web 2.0 brings both, customers and employees, closer to the point where decisions are made and goods are produced. It makes the company more transparent.&nbsp;</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><br /></span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">Global competition has made it extremely difficult to gain competitive advantage. Nowadays most of the customers find information about products and services in the Internet. Your company may have Internet site and may be found in Google, but so do millions of companies worldwide. Every company has to think how they can be differentiated from all the others.</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><br /></span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">As you represent your company in the social networks of the Internet your professional identity cannot be separated from your private and personal ones. This can be, however, considered to be an advantage. It brings humanity to the organization as an entity.&nbsp;</span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;"><br /></span></div>
<div><span style="font-size: small;">By participating in the everyday</span><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="font-size: small;">&nbsp;practices of the organization and in the creation of perceived social&nbsp;</span></span><span style="font-size: small;"><span style="font-size: small;">reality, managers could bring themselves closer to the everyday level of the company, and thus encourage employees to share new ideas. No-one else could be able to enhance the everyday processes better than the ones who are involved in them.</span></span></div>

# Exit Playbook: A Founder's True Story

## What Actually Happens When a Founder "Exits"

We see headlines like "Company X acquired by Company Y", but behind that is a structured and always demanding process. This playbook documents the real experience of exiting Lyyti through acquisition by SuperOffice, providing a practical guide for founders considering their own exit.

---

## Executive Summary

Most deals follow roughly this path, but the reality is more nuanced than the headlines suggest. This document provides the true story, timeline, and practical guidance based on real M&A experience.

**Key Insight**: An exit isn't the end. For many founders, it's the next chapter of growth with better resources and market reach.

---

## Prerequisites: Building a Sellable Business

Before you can exit, you need to build a business worth acquiring. In SaaS, this typically means:

### Financial Requirements
- **Rule of 40**: Growth rate + profit margin should equal or exceed 40%
- **Revenue threshold**: Approaching €10M+ ARR (varies by industry)
- **Unit economics**: Clear path to profitability or already profitable
- **Predictable revenue**: Strong retention metrics and recurring revenue

### Business Fundamentals
- Product-market fit with demonstrated traction
- Scalable go-to-market motion
- Clean cap table and legal structure
- Strong team and culture
- Clear competitive differentiation

**Reality Check**: Don't start the exit process until these fundamentals are solid. Buyers will discover any weaknesses during due diligence.

## The 7 Phases of an Exit

### Phase 1: Preparation & Advisors (Month -2 to 0)

#### Decision Framework
Before engaging advisors, answer these questions honestly:
- **Why are you selling?** (Scale? Burnout? Strategic opportunity?)
- **What's your desired outcome?** (Maximum price? Right cultural fit? Future role?)
- **Are all owners aligned?** (Resolve any disagreements before starting)

#### Assembling Your Advisory Team

You'll need 4 core advisors:

**Investment Bank / M&A Advisor**
- Role: Runs the process, manages buyer outreach, negotiates deal terms
- Selection criteria: Industry experience, buyer network, track record
- Cost: 3-5% success fee (typically)
- Example: Stifel Europe for Lyyti exit

**Financial Advisor**
- Role: Financial due diligence preparation, data room setup, QoE (Quality of Earnings)
- Selection criteria: Experience with SaaS metrics, familiarity with due diligence
- Cost: €20K-€100K depending on complexity
- Example: EY for Lyyti exit

**Legal Advisor**
- Role: SPA negotiation, legal due diligence, regulatory compliance
- Selection criteria: M&A experience, jurisdiction expertise
- Cost: €30K-€150K depending on deal complexity
- Example: Borenius for Lyyti exit

**Commercial Due Diligence Advisor** (Optional but valuable)
- Role: Market analysis, competitive landscape, growth validation
- Selection criteria: Industry expertise, buyer credibility
- Cost: €15K-€50K
- Example: Adepto Partners for Lyyti exit

**Budget Planning**: Expect €100K-€300K in total advisor costs for a mid-market SaaS exit.

---

### Phase 2: Story & Materials (Month 1)

#### The Teaser (2-4 pages)
Non-confidential overview to generate interest:
- Company overview (without revealing name)
- Market opportunity and positioning
- High-level financial metrics
- Transaction overview
- Contact process

**Purpose**: Sent to long list before NDAs to gauge interest

#### The CIM: Confidential Information Memorandum (30-60 pages)

Think of the CIM as "the owner's manual" for your company. This is the most important document you'll create.

**Critical sections**:
1. **Executive Summary** (2-3 pages)
   - Investment highlights
   - Transaction summary
   - Key selling points

2. **Company Overview** (5-10 pages)
   - History and milestones
   - Product/service offering
   - Value proposition
   - Competitive advantages

3. **Market Analysis** (5-8 pages)
   - Market size and growth
   - Competitive landscape
   - Market positioning
   - Growth trends and opportunities

4. **Business Model** (5-10 pages)
   - Revenue model
   - Customer acquisition
   - Sales process and funnel
   - Pricing strategy

5. **Operations** (5-8 pages)
   - Organizational structure
   - Key personnel
   - Technology stack
   - Infrastructure

6. **Financial Performance** (10-15 pages)
   - Historical financials (3-5 years)
   - Revenue breakdown and trends
   - Key metrics (MRR, ARR, CAC, LTV, churn)
   - EBITDA and margins
   - Working capital

7. **Growth Strategy** (3-5 pages)
   - Product roadmap
   - Market expansion plans
   - Strategic initiatives

8. **Transaction Overview** (2-3 pages)
   - Deal structure
   - Timeline
   - Management's future involvement

**Pro Tip**: Simultaneously conduct seller-facilitated commercial due diligence. Get ahead of questions buyers will ask about market size, competitors, and growth potential.

**Timeline**: Expect 4-6 weeks to build a high-quality CIM with your advisors.

---

### Phase 3: Market Outreach (Month 2-3)

#### Building the Long List

Work with your investment bank to identify 30-50 potential buyers across categories:

**Strategic Buyers** (Companies in your industry)
- Pros: Highest valuations, synergy premiums, market consolidation
- Cons: Slower process, integration risk, potential culture clash
- Examples: Competitors, adjacent market players, enterprise customers

**Financial Buyers** (Private Equity, Growth Equity)
- Pros: Faster decisions, operational expertise, platform build strategy
- Cons: Lower valuations, financial engineering focus, exit pressure
- Examples: Industry-focused PE funds, growth equity funds

**International Players** (Foreign market expansion)
- Pros: Geographic synergies, market entry strategy, cross-border premiums
- Cons: Complex negotiations, regulatory hurdles, cultural differences

#### The Outreach Process

1. **Week 1-2**: Send teaser to long list
2. **Week 3-4**: NDA signatures (expect 50-70% NDA rate)
3. **Week 5-6**: CIM distribution to signed NDAs
4. **Week 7-8**: Schedule "coffee chats"

**Real Numbers from Lyyti Exit**:
- Long list: 30+ companies
- NDAs signed: ~20-25 (estimated)
- CIM shared: 20-25 companies
- Coffee chats: ~15 companies
- Indicative offers: 5-8 (typical)

---

### Phase 4: First Meetings - "Coffee Chats" (Month 3-4)

#### What Buyers Are Really Evaluating

These aren't casual coffee meetings. Buyers are assessing:

**The Founder Story**
- Passion and credibility
- Market insight
- Vision alignment
- Future commitment

**Culture & Team**
- Cultural fit with acquirer
- Team retention risk
- Organizational health
- Leadership depth

**Product & Technology**
- Product differentiation
- Technology moat
- Scalability
- Roadmap alignment

**Chemistry Check**
- Personal rapport
- Communication style
- Trust building
- Partnership potential

#### Your Meeting Strategy

**Preparation**:
- Perfect your 15-minute story
- Prepare for tough questions
- Research each buyer deeply
- Understand their strategic rationale

**During the Meeting**:
- Be authentic and passionate
- Listen more than you talk
- Assess cultural fit (you're interviewing them too)
- Note their strategic vision for your company

**Red Flags to Watch**:
- Lack of strategic vision
- Focus only on financial metrics
- Poor cultural alignment
- Unclear decision-making process

**Timeline**: Expect 4-6 weeks for 10-15 meetings

---

### Phase 5: Indicative Offers (Month 4-5)

#### Non-Binding Offers (IOIs)

Expect 30-50% of coffee chat participants to submit offers.

**What's in an IOI**:
- Proposed valuation range
- Deal structure (cash, stock, earnout)
- Key assumptions and conditions
- Preliminary timeline
- Next steps and exclusivity expectations

#### Evaluation Framework

**Don't just compare prices**. Evaluate holistically:

**Financial Terms** (40% weight)
- Enterprise value
- Payment structure (cash vs. stock)
- Earnout terms and achievability
- Working capital adjustments
- Debt assumptions

**Strategic Fit** (30% weight)
- Product vision alignment
- Market strategy
- Investment commitment
- Growth resources

**Deal Structure** (20% weight)
- Certainty of close
- Timing and speed
- Regulatory risks
- Financing contingencies

**Cultural Alignment** (10% weight)
- Team retention plans
- Your future role
- Decision-making autonomy
- Values alignment

**Reality Check**: The highest price isn't always the best deal. A slightly lower offer with better terms and cultural fit often wins.

---

### Phase 6: Due Diligence & Negotiations (Month 5-7)

This is the most intense phase. Expect to live and breathe the deal.

#### Round 1 Due Diligence (4-6 weeks)

Buyers will examine everything:

**Financial DD**
- Revenue recognition
- Customer contracts
- Margin analysis
- Working capital needs
- Historical accuracy

**Legal DD**
- Corporate structure
- IP ownership
- Customer contracts
- Employment agreements
- Litigation risks
- Regulatory compliance

**Technical DD**
- Code quality and documentation
- Architecture scalability
- Security and compliance
- Technical debt
- Dependencies and licenses

**Commercial DD**
- Customer interviews
- Market validation
- Competitive position
- Pipeline quality
- Churn analysis

**Operational DD**
- Organizational structure
- Key person dependencies
- Infrastructure and tools
- Vendor relationships

#### The Auction Process

Most M&A advisors run a controlled auction to drive valuation:

1. **Week 1-2**: Simultaneous DD with 3-5 top bidders
2. **Week 3-4**: Management presentations
3. **Week 5**: Best and final offers (BFOs)
4. **Week 6**: Selection and LOI

**Pro Tip**: Your M&A advisor orchestrates competitive tension. Trust the process.

#### Letter of Intent (LOI) & Exclusivity

The winning bidder submits an LOI covering:
- Final valuation and structure
- Key terms and conditions
- Exclusivity period (30-90 days)
- Breakup fee provisions
- Timeline to close

**Critical Decision Point**: Once you sign the LOI with exclusivity, you cannot negotiate with other buyers. Make sure you're comfortable with the terms.

**Lyyti Experience**: Selected SuperOffice after auction-type negotiations in October

---

### Phase 7: Final DD, SPA, and Closing (Month 7-9)

#### Round 2 Due Diligence (4-6 weeks)

Even deeper dive after exclusivity:
- Detailed financial audits
- Customer reference calls
- Employee interviews
- Partner validation
- Competitive intelligence

**Your Job**: Respond quickly and completely. Delays kill deals.

**Managing Your Team**: By now, you'll likely need to inform key employees. Plan the communication carefully.

#### SPA Negotiation (3-4 weeks)

The Share Purchase Agreement is the final, binding contract. Key negotiation points:

**Purchase Price Adjustments**
- Working capital targets
- Cash vs. debt assumptions
- Earnout calculations
- Escrow provisions

**Representations & Warranties (R&Ws)**
- Scope and coverage
- Disclosure schedules
- Indemnification caps and baskets
- Survival periods
- R&W insurance

**Employment & Transition**
- Founder employment terms
- Key employee retention
- Non-compete provisions
- Handover responsibilities

**Conditions to Closing**
- Regulatory approvals
- Third-party consents
- Financing conditions
- Material adverse change (MAC) clauses

**Timeline**: Expect 3-4 weeks of intense legal negotiations. Your legal counsel earns their fee here.

#### The Rigorous Finalization

Between SPA signing and closing (often same day, sometimes weeks apart):

**Closing Checklist**:
- Obtain regulatory approvals
- Customer consent letters
- Employee notifications
- Vendor notifications
- Banking arrangements
- Insurance policies
- Share transfer logistics
- Final deliverables completion

**Lyyti Timeline**:
- SPA signed: December 8
- Closing: December 11 (3 days later)

**Note**: The period between signing and closing involves rigorous finalization of everything required for transfer of ownership.

#### Closing Day

The moment arrives. Typical closing sequence:

1. **Morning**: Final document review
2. **Midday**: Signature execution
3. **Afternoon**: Funds transfer
4. **Evening**: Celebration (you've earned it!)

**Emotional Reality**: It's intense. You're selling something you've built for years while simultaneously committing to its next chapter.

## Key Jargon Decoder

### Essential M&A Terms

**NDA - Non-Disclosure Agreement**
- Confidentiality contract allowing open discussion
- Required before sharing CIM
- Typically includes non-solicitation provisions

**CIM - Confidential Information Memorandum**
- Detailed company story, financials, and data
- "The owner's manual" for your business
- Shared only after NDA signature

**IOI - Indication of Interest**
- Non-binding preliminary offer
- Includes valuation range and key terms
- Used to narrow the field

**DD - Due Diligence**
- Deep dive investigation into financial, legal, technical, and commercial aspects
- Occurs in multiple rounds
- Aim: Validate CIM claims and uncover risks

**LOI - Letter of Intent**
- Document outlining main terms of the deal
- Usually includes exclusivity period
- Mostly non-binding except exclusivity and confidentiality

**Exclusivity Period**
- Time frame where seller cannot negotiate with other buyers
- Typically 30-90 days
- Allows buyer to complete DD without competition

**SPA - Share Purchase Agreement**
- Final binding contract for the sale
- Detailed legal document (100+ pages)
- Covers all terms, conditions, and protections

**R&W - Representations & Warranties**
- Seller's promises about the business
- Breach can trigger indemnification
- Often backed by insurance (R&W insurance)

**Earnout**
- Portion of purchase price paid based on future performance
- Aligns incentives but adds complexity
- Typical periods: 1-3 years

**Escrow**
- Portion of purchase price held back temporarily
- Protects buyer against breaches of R&Ws
- Typically 10-20% for 12-24 months

**MAC/MAE - Material Adverse Change/Effect**
- Significant negative event that allows buyer to walk away
- Heavily negotiated clause
- High bar to trigger

**Working Capital**
- Current assets minus current liabilities
- Purchase price often adjusted based on working capital at close
- Target is typically normalized historical average

**QoE - Quality of Earnings**
- Financial DD report validating revenue and EBITDA
- Identifies one-time items and accounting issues
- Critical for buyer's valuation confidence

## Deal Structure Deep Dive

### Common Deal Structures

#### 1. All-Cash Deal
**Structure**: 100% cash at close
**Pros**:
- Immediate liquidity
- Clean exit
- No future risk
**Cons**:
- Potentially lower valuation
- Tax implications
**Best For**: Founders seeking clean break

#### 2. Cash + Earnout
**Structure**: Partial cash upfront + performance-based payments
**Pros**:
- Higher total valuation
- Aligns incentives
- Buyer shares risk
**Cons**:
- Delayed payout
- Performance risk
- Potential disputes
**Best For**: High-growth companies with optimistic projections

#### 3. Cash + Stock
**Structure**: Mix of cash and acquirer equity
**Pros**:
- Upside participation
- Lower tax on stock portion (sometimes)
- Alignment with buyer
**Cons**:
- Illiquidity risk
- Valuation risk
- Lock-up periods
**Best For**: Strategic acquisitions where you believe in combined entity

#### 4. Stock Swap
**Structure**: Pure equity exchange
**Pros**:
- Tax deferral
- Full upside participation
**Cons**:
- High risk
- No liquidity
**Best For**: Mergers of equals, public acquirers

### Understanding Valuation

#### Key Valuation Metrics in SaaS

**ARR Multiple**
- Most common: 4x-10x ARR for SaaS
- Factors: Growth rate, retention, margins, market position
- Lyyti context: Approaching €10M revenue

**EBITDA Multiple**
- Typical: 8x-15x EBITDA
- More common for profitable businesses
- Less relevant for high-growth SaaS

**Revenue Multiple Drivers**:
- **Growth rate**: >40% YoY = premium multiple
- **Retention**: >95% NRR = strong multiple
- **Margins**: >70% gross margin expected
- **CAC payback**: <12 months ideal
- **Market position**: Category leaders get premiums

## Emotional Journey: What They Don't Tell You

### The Founder's Emotional Arc

#### Pre-Process: The Decision
- Self-doubt: "Am I giving up too soon?"
- Excitement: "This could be transformative"
- Fear: "What if it falls apart?"

#### During Process: The Grind
- **Month 1-2**: Energized by possibilities
- **Month 3-4**: Exhausted by meetings
- **Month 5-6**: Stressed by due diligence
- **Month 7-8**: Anxious about closing

#### Post-Close: The Adjustment
- Relief: "We made it"
- Loss: "It's not mine anymore"
- Optimism: "New chapter begins"
- Uncertainty: "What's my role now?"

### Managing the Emotional Load

**During the Process**:
1. Maintain a confidant outside the company
2. Keep a journal
3. Exercise and sleep
4. Set boundaries (no email after 10pm)
5. Trust your advisors

**After Closing**:
1. Take time to process
2. Reflect on accomplishments
3. Clarify your new role
4. Set personal goals
5. Stay connected to the mission

### The Lyyti Perspective

> "Emotionally, it's intense: you sell something you've built for years – and at the same time you commit to its next chapter."

> "For me, this isn't the end. It feels much more like the third time I get to be truly excited about Lyyti's future: when I founded it, when Vaaka joined, and now as we start a new chapter together with SuperOffice."

**Key Insight**: Frame the exit as a new beginning, not an ending.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### 1. Starting Too Early
**Mistake**: Running a process before the business is ready
**Result**: Low valuation or failed process that damages reputation
**Solution**: Build to Rule of 40 and meaningful revenue first

### 2. Choosing the Wrong Advisors
**Mistake**: Hiring advisors without M&A experience or industry knowledge
**Result**: Poorly run process, missed opportunities, bad terms
**Solution**: Check references, evaluate track record, ensure chemistry

### 3. Over-Optimizing for Price
**Mistake**: Taking the highest offer without considering other factors
**Result**: Deal falls apart in DD or post-close integration fails
**Solution**: Use the evaluation framework (financial 40%, strategic 30%, structure 20%, cultural 10%)

### 4. Poor Due Diligence Preparation
**Mistake**: Starting DD unprepared with messy data room
**Result**: Deal delays, valuation reductions, lost credibility
**Solution**: Prepare data room before going to market, run seller DD

### 5. Ignoring Cultural Fit
**Mistake**: Focusing only on financial terms
**Result**: Miserable post-acquisition experience, team attrition
**Solution**: Spend time with the buyer's team, assess values alignment

### 6. Inadequate Legal Counsel
**Mistake**: Using generalist lawyers instead of M&A specialists
**Result**: Unfavorable SPA terms, excessive risk retention
**Solution**: Hire experienced M&A counsel, invest in R&W insurance

### 7. Mismanaging Team Communication
**Mistake**: Telling team too early or too late
**Result**: Leaks and disruption or loss of trust
**Solution**: Plan communication carefully, inform key people at LOI stage

### 8. Neglecting the Business
**Mistake**: Taking eye off operations during M&A process
**Result**: Declining metrics that reduce valuation or kill deal
**Solution**: Delegate daily operations, maintain normal business cadence

### 9. Rushing Through SPA
**Mistake**: Accepting unfavorable terms to speed up closing
**Result**: Long-term financial and legal exposure
**Solution**: Take time to negotiate properly, understand every clause

### 10. No Post-Close Plan
**Mistake**: Not defining your role and goals post-acquisition
**Result**: Frustration, conflict, potential departure
**Solution**: Negotiate clear role, responsibilities, and autonomy upfront

## Checklist: Are You Ready for an Exit Process?

### Business Fundamentals
- [ ] Revenue approaching €10M ARR (for SaaS)
- [ ] Rule of 40 achieved or close
- [ ] Positive unit economics (LTV/CAC > 3x)
- [ ] Strong retention metrics (>90% gross, >100% net)
- [ ] Clean financial statements (audited or audit-ready)
- [ ] Predictable, recurring revenue model

### Legal & Governance
- [ ] Clean cap table (no complicated structures)
- [ ] All IP properly assigned to company
- [ ] Customer contracts are assignable
- [ ] No pending litigation or major legal issues
- [ ] Board and shareholder alignment on exit
- [ ] Employment agreements up to date

### Operations
- [ ] Management team extends beyond founder
- [ ] Documented processes and playbooks
- [ ] Minimal single points of failure
- [ ] Scalable infrastructure
- [ ] Strong security and compliance posture

### Market Position
- [ ] Clear competitive differentiation
- [ ] Defensible market position
- [ ] Growth potential articulated
- [ ] Customer satisfaction demonstrated
- [ ] Industry recognition or awards

### Personal Readiness
- [ ] Clarity on why you want to exit
- [ ] Alignment among co-founders and key shareholders
- [ ] Prepared for 6-9 month intensive process
- [ ] Open to post-acquisition role (if required)
- [ ] Emotional and mental readiness

### Data Room Preparation
- [ ] Financial statements (3-5 years)
- [ ] Cap table and option pool details
- [ ] Customer contracts and data
- [ ] Employee agreements
- [ ] Technology documentation
- [ ] IP registrations and assignments
- [ ] Key vendor contracts
- [ ] Board minutes and consents
- [ ] Marketing and sales materials
- [ ] Product roadmap
- [ ] Organizational charts

**If you can check 80%+ of these boxes, you're likely ready to explore an exit.**

## Post-Acquisition Success

### The First 100 Days

**Week 1-2: Integration Kickoff**
- Meet the extended team
- Understand reporting structure
- Clarify decision rights
- Set communication cadence

**Week 3-6: Cultural Integration**
- Learn the acquirer's culture
- Identify quick wins
- Build cross-functional relationships
- Address team concerns

**Week 7-12: Strategic Alignment**
- Align on product roadmap
- Integrate go-to-market
- Establish shared metrics
- Launch joint initiatives

**Month 4-6: Momentum Building**
- Deliver early wins
- Expand integration
- Leverage combined resources
- Demonstrate synergy value

### Keys to Post-Acquisition Success

1. **Be Open-Minded**
   - Your way isn't always the best way
   - Learn from the acquirer
   - Embrace new resources

2. **Communicate Relentlessly**
   - Over-communicate with your team
   - Build bridges across organizations
   - Raise issues early

3. **Deliver Results**
   - Hit your targets
   - Demonstrate the deal thesis
   - Build credibility

4. **Stay True to Your Values**
   - Don't compromise core principles
   - Advocate for your team
   - Protect the product vision

5. **Think Long-Term**
   - This is a marathon, not a sprint
   - Build relationships
   - Create lasting value

## Conclusion: Exit as New Beginning

An exit isn't an ending—it's a new chapter. The companies that view acquisition as a fresh start with better resources, broader reach, and stronger support tend to achieve the most success post-close.

### Key Takeaways

1. **Build First, Exit Second**: Create a business worth acquiring before starting the process
2. **Advisor Selection Matters**: Choose experienced M&A advisors with relevant industry expertise
3. **Process is Intense**: Expect 6-9 months of demanding work
4. **Price Isn't Everything**: Evaluate strategic fit, culture, and structure alongside valuation
5. **Due Diligence is Critical**: Prepare early, respond quickly, maintain business momentum
6. **Emotions Run High**: Acknowledge the journey, support your team, trust your advisors
7. **New Chapter Begins**: Approach post-acquisition with optimism and openness

### Final Thoughts from the Lyyti Experience

This latest process demonstrated that with the right advisors, clear preparation, and genuine buyer interest, a well-run M&A process can move from initial conversations to closing in approximately 6 months.

The key success factors:
- Strong business fundamentals (Rule of 40, approaching €10M revenue)
- Excellent advisor team (Stifel, EY, Borenius, Adepto)
- Well-prepared materials (CIM, commercial DD)
- Broad market outreach (30+ NDAs)
- Competitive process (auction dynamics)
- Right strategic fit (SuperOffice alignment)

**Remember**: For founders who love their business, an acquisition isn't about exiting—it's about unlocking the next level of growth with the right partner.

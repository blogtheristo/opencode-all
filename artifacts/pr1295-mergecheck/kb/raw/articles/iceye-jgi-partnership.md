## Summary

ICEYE, the Finnish SAR (synthetic aperture radar) satellite constellation operator, announced a partnership with the Jane Goodall Institute USA (JGI) to deploy ICEYE's **Deforestation Solution** across conservation corridors in the Congo Basin. Initial focus: eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC). Expansion: Gombe National Park, western Tanzania.

## Key facts

- **Who:** ICEYE (Helsinki, Finland; ~1,000 employees; SAR constellation) + Jane Goodall Institute USA (founded 1977; 30 global offices; Roots & Shoots in 75 countries).
- **What:** Deployment of ICEYE's **Deforestation Solution** for near-real-time monitoring of illegal mining, illegal logging, settlement expansion, and forest disturbance.
- **Where:** Eastern DRC (primary); Gombe National Park, Tanzania (expansion).
- **Why SAR:** Persistent cloud cover and dense tropical canopy make optical satellites unreliable in the Congo Basin for weeks–months at a time. SAR sees through cloud cover and works day and night.
- **Cadence:** Intelligence delivered in **days**, not weeks/months (explicit quote from ICEYE VP Andy Read).
- **Stakes:** Eastern DRC rainforests host an estimated **70,000–100,000 chimpanzees**; Congo Basin holds the most diverse ape population on Earth. Gombe is the site of Jane Goodall's original 1960 chimpanzee research.

- **Dual-use sensing** — same SAR capability serves defence/insurance and environmental/biodiversity monitoring; maps to the DWS Aegis dual-use thesis.

## The Problem in Numbers

Without a path from Outokumpu's ISO 14067 certificates to a CBAM annual declaration,
fabricators default to the EU punitive default emission factors. Those defaults reflect
worst-case non-EU production — not Outokumpu's 75%-below-average footprint.

**What this costs a fabricator (illustrative, 1,000 tonnes steel import):**

| Scenario                                       | Emission factor                 | Embedded CO₂      | CBAM cost @ €65/tCO₂e |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| Non-EU supplier, no verified data (EU default) | 1.852 tCO₂e/t (Reg. 2023/1773)  | 1,852 tCO₂e       | **€120,380**          |
| Non-EU supplier, verified low-carbon data      | ~0.5–0.9 tCO₂e/t (actual)       | ~500–900 tCO₂e    | **€32,500–€58,500**   |
| Outokumpu EU-origin steel                      | EU-produced — no CBAM triggered | 0 tCO₂e liability | **€0**                |

Sources: EU default from Commission Implementing Regulation 2023/1773; ETS price EU average 2024.

The fabricator switching from non-EU default to Outokumpu EU steel saves up to €120,000 per
1,000 tonnes in CBAM certificates. That number belongs in their procurement meeting, not just
their sustainability report. The 60-day pilot puts it there.

fabricator procurement teams cannot put a euro figure on them without compliance tooling.
Once they can, Outokumpu steel has a quantified price advantage in every competitive bid against
non-EU alternatives — and that advantage grows as ETS prices rise.

The pilot converts an intangible sustainability claim into a line-item CFO decision before
the first account manager conversation.

## 1. The Concept

The **Reindustrialize Summit (Detroit, 16–17 June 2026)** works because it pairs the conference with a _physical drive_ through the metro area's raw industrial capacity: Ecorse (US Steel), Auburn Hills (GM/Ford and OEM tech campuses), Fraser (mid-tier suppliers), and the 8 Mile corridor (distributed factories). Seeing idle blast furnaces and humming EV lines side by side manufactures conviction in a way a slide deck cannot.

Europe has the same raw capacity — and the same reindustrialization wave (Capgemini projects **€4.7T of reindustrialization investment by 2028**; 66% of large EU firms are actively reindustrializing) — but no equivalent drive-the-loop convening. **Reindustrialize EU Field Tour** closes that gap with a four-city backbone, each city the direct analogue of one Detroit stop.

The tour is not a sightseeing trip. It is a **structured enterprise-outreach motion**: drive the capacity, then sit down with the people who run it — plant managers, sustainability officers, and procurement heads now under CBAM, CSRD, and the EU Industrial Accelerator Act ("Made in EU"). DWS IQ 6 is positioned as the _intelligence layer_ over that capacity, not as a vendor pitch.

The tour runs on **two complementary backbones**: an **industrial Capacity Backbone** (§2 — the daytime drive through real plants) and an **investor/policy Capital Backbone** (§7 — where pilots get funded and policy doors open). Together they mirror the Detroit Summit's fusion of raw capacity _and_ capital/government.

## 2. The Four-City Backbone

| #   | EU City            | Detroit Analogue  | What it proves                                       | Anchor industry                       |
| --- | ------------------ | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Duisburg, DE**   | Ecorse / US Steel | Europe's largest steel concentration, mid-transition | Integrated steel, Rhine logistics     |
| 2   | **Gothenburg, SE** | Auburn Hills      | OEM + tech-campus reinvention                        | Automotive, trucks, bearings          |
| 3   | **Tampere, FI**    | Fraser            | Mid-tier heavy machinery & process equipment         | Mining/forestry machinery, automation |
| 4   | **Liège, BE**      | 8 Mile corridor   | Distributed factories pivoting to green steel        | Green steel, metals, logistics        |

### Stop 1 — Duisburg, Germany _(= Ecorse / US Steel)_

- **The capacity:** Duisburg produces more steel than any other city in Europe and is home to thyssenkrupp Steel's largest works. In 2026 it is the visible front line of heavy-industry transition — thyssenkrupp has announced deep workforce cuts (27,000 → 16,000 over six years) while commissioning new high-tech rolling facilities. This _is_ the European Ecorse: world-scale steel, mid-restructuring.
- **What to see:** thyssenkrupp Steel Duisburg works; the Rhine inland port (duisport, Europe's largest inland port); Landschaftspark Duisburg-Nord (decommissioned ironworks) as the visual "before."
- **Target accounts:** thyssenkrupp Steel Europe, duisport, surrounding Rhine-Ruhr Tier-1 metals processors.
- **DWS hook:** CBAM exposure mapping + EN 15978 embodied-carbon tracking for steel; CSRD-grade emissions data over a multi-site footprint.

### Stop 2 — Gothenburg, Sweden _(= Auburn Hills)_

- **The capacity:** Gothenburg is Northern Europe's automotive and engineering campus — Volvo Cars, Volvo Group (trucks), and SKF. Like Auburn Hills, it is the OEM brain-trust where the EV/automation transition is being engineered, not just assembled.
- **What to see:** Volvo Cars Torslanda plant; Volvo Group truck operations; SKF's manufacturing and reliability-tech footprint; the Lindholmen Science Park cluster.
- **Target accounts:** Volvo Cars, Volvo Group, SKF, plus the Stegra green-steel offtake chain feeding Nordic OEMs.
- **DWS hook:** supply-chain emissions intelligence and supplier scoring; sustainability-officer reporting under CSRD; procurement-side "Made in EU" content tracking.

### Stop 3 — Tampere, Finland _(= Fraser)_

- **The capacity:** Tampere is Finland's industrial heart — the mid-tier, high-value machinery layer (Metso, Valmet, AGCO/Valtra, Sandvik operations, Nokia legacy). This is the European Fraser: not the marquee OEM, but the deep supplier base where capacity is broad and distributed.
- **What to see:** Metso and Valmet process-equipment plants; the Tampere red-brick industrial heritage quarter (Finlayson) as the reinvention story; local automation/robotics SMEs.
- **Target accounts:** Metso, Valmet, AGCO Power, regional precision-machinery suppliers.
- **DWS hook:** plant-manager operations intelligence; energy optimization; sovereignty-aware (EU-hosted) data for mid-cap manufacturers wary of US-cloud exposure.

### Stop 4 — Liège, Belgium _(= 8 Mile corridor)_

- **The capacity:** The Liège basin is a classic distributed industrial corridor — legacy steel pivoting to green steel and clean tech, anchored by John Cockerill (green-steel and hydrogen technology) plus aerospace and logistics. Like 8 Mile, capacity is spread across many sites rather than one campus, and the green-transition contrast is sharpest here.
- **What to see:** John Cockerill green-steel / hydrogen technology sites; ArcelorMittal Liège operations; Liège Airport cargo hub (logistics backbone).
- **Target accounts:** John Cockerill, ArcelorMittal Belgium, Liège logistics and metals SMEs.
- **DWS hook:** green-steel lifecycle documentation; CBAM importer/exporter exposure; distributed multi-site carbon accounting.

## 3. The Route

Three drivable legs link the backbone into one tour. Each leg is a half/full day of road that itself passes through the EU industrial heartland — the "drive the capacity" principle.

- **Leg A — Rhine-Ruhr to the Nordics:** Duisburg → (fly Düsseldorf–Gothenburg) → Gothenburg. Anchors the steel-to-OEM transition.
- **Leg B — Nordic crossing:** Gothenburg → (fly Gothenburg–Tampere via Helsinki) → Tampere. Anchors OEM-to-supplier depth.
- **Leg C — Return south:** Tampere → (fly Helsinki–Liège/Brussels) → Liège. Anchors supplier-to-green-steel pivot, closing the loop near the Brussels policy center.

A compressed **3-day version** runs Duisburg (Day 1) → Gothenburg (Day 2) → Liège (Day 3), holding Tampere as a dedicated Nordic satellite leg. The **full 4-day version** includes all four.

## 4. The Convening Format (per city)

Mirroring Reindustrialize's "dozens of curated dinners," each stop runs the same repeatable block:

1. **Morning — Capacity drive (2–3 hrs):** the anchor site(s) above, with a short DWS field briefing en route.
2. **Afternoon — Working session (90 min):** 6–10 invited operators. DWS frames the regulatory horizon (CBAM, CSRD, Industrial Accelerator Act) against their specific site, not a generic deck.
3. **Evening — Curated dinner (8–14 seats):** plant managers, sustainability officers, procurement heads, plus one regional capital or policy guest. This is where partnerships, pilots, and references are made.

**DWS positioning across all stops:** the intelligence layer over industrial capacity — predictive regulation, lifecycle/embodied-carbon tracking, EU-sovereign data hosting. Sell the _Situation Room_, not a feature.

## 7. Capital & Policy Track (Capital Backbone)

The industrial backbone in §2 answers _"show me the capacity."_ It does not, by itself, answer _"who funds and governs the transition?"_ — which is half of why the Detroit Summit works (its roster is heavy with capital and government: JP Morgan, BlackRock, Founders Fund, plus White House / DoD / State officials). To mirror that fully, the tour runs a **second backbone of investor and policy hubs**, grounded in Lifetime's InvestEU portal network history.

The two backbones are **complementary axes, not alternatives**: the Capacity Backbone is the daytime drive; the Capital Backbone is where pilots get funded and policy doors open.

| #   | Capital Hub       | When          | Why it anchors                                                                   | Convening vehicle                                                                                     |
| --- | ----------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Brussels, BE**  | Q1 2027       | InvestEU, EU industrial policy, DG GROW/CLIMA orbit; pairs with Liège (~100 km)  | EAIS; policy workshops; Rhineland-Meuse Belgian anchor (§8)                                           |
| 2   | **Athens, GR**    | (pull to BRU) | Southern-Europe angel ecosystem; EBAN Congress — pull to Brussels (see §9)       | EBAN; Big Pi; HDB/RRF officers                                                                        |
| 3   | **Budapest, HU**  | (pull to BRU) | CEE finance; Lead Ventures / MOL-backed industrial fund — pull to Brussels       | Lead Ventures; Day One Capital; DG REGIO Just Transition                                              |
| 4   | **Dublin, IE**    | 2027 H2       | W-EU finance, cross-border tech; Atlantic Bridge / Elkstone industrial scale-up  | EU Finance Days lineage; Enterprise Ireland HPSU                                                      |
| 5   | **Stockholm, SE** | November 2026 | Nordic PE/LP capital cluster; bridges Gothenburg+Tampere industrial leg to money | PE Insights Nordics (Nov 11); Nordics Institutional Forum (Nov 17–18); Real Deals Nordic Summit (Oct) |

**Natural synthesis — Belgium pairs itself.** Liège (Stop 4, Capacity Backbone) and Brussels (Capital Hub 1) are ~100 km apart. Run them as a single **2-day Belgian anchor** (full agenda in §8): Liège green-steel capacity morning → Brussels EAIS/InvestEU capital-and-policy evening. This is the tightest, lowest-cost proof of the two-axis format before scaling.

**Stockholm is the Nordic capital bridge.** The Gothenburg/Tampere industrial leg (Capacity Backbone) has no capital anchor without Stockholm. Three confirmed November 2026 events make it the natural close of the Nordic circuit: **PE Insights Nordics (Nov 11, 500+ attendees, 150+ LPs)**, **Nordics Sweden Institutional Forum (Nov 17–18, pension funds / sovereign wealth)**, and **Real Deals Nordic Summit (Oct, inaugural one-day LP/GP event)**. The PE Summit cadence means late-October to mid-November is when Nordic industrial LP capital is in the room. DWS attends or presents; warm the list from the Gothenburg/Tampere plant visits made earlier in the year.

**Format per capital hub (mirrors Reindustrialize 3.0):** two-day events with local manufacturing showcases, policy workshops, and e-pitching — cross-border investment attraction as the explicit objective. DWS IQ 6 is the industrial climate OS being demonstrated (predictive regulation, lifecycle/embodied-carbon tracking, EU-sovereign data).

## 9. Gemini Strategic Consolidation Recommendation

_Gemini's assessment of where the tour over-stretches and what to cut. Adopted with one addition: Stockholm November as the Nordic capital bridge._

### The Problem: Logistical Whiplash and Diluted Focus

DWS IQ 6 is a specialized industrial climate OS. Going to hubs that lack heavy industrial nexus tanks ROI. Two specific weaknesses:

1. **Athens and Dublin as physical stops** — Athens lacks the heavy concentration of automotive/steel enterprise buyers that Axis 1 requires. Dublin is an excellent SaaS and financial hub but largely detached from the continental heavy manufacturing ecosystem.
2. **Attention splintering** — enterprise buyers in Duisburg or Gothenburg do not care about a policy briefing in Budapest. CEE investors in Budapest are rarely looking to fund enterprise buyers in Tampere.

### The Consolidation: Three Moves

**Move 1 — Cut Athens and Budapest as physical tour stops.**
Pull their funds to Brussels instead. Lead Ventures (Budapest) and Big Pi (Athens) routinely travel to Brussels for EU-level LP events and EIF meetings anyway. The capital arrives at the Belgian anchor without the travel overhead.

**Move 2 — Tighten Axis 1 to the Rhineland-Meuse Triangle.**
The pilot loop is **Duisburg → Liège → Brussels**. Drivable in a single day. Contains Germany's steel heart, Belgium's green industrial pilot capacity, and the EU's policy capital. Prove the format here before spending on the broader circuit.

**Move 3 — Separate the Nordic leg cleanly.**
Keep Gothenburg and Tampere as a distinct **Q3/autumn 2027 industrial leg**. Do not mix Nordic auto/machinery giants with Southern/Eastern European funding ecosystems in one tour — the narrative fractures. Run the data from the Belgian anchor to justify the Nordic spend.

**Addition: Stockholm November as Nordic capital bridge.**
Once the Nordic industrial leg (Gothenburg → Tampere) has produced pilots and proof points, Stockholm in November closes the loop on the capital side. The PE Insights Nordics (Nov 11), Nordics Institutional Forum (Nov 17–18), and Real Deals Nordic Summit (Oct) give DWS three consecutive venues to convert industrial evidence from the summer leg into LP/VC relationships before year-end. This is the natural cadence: **spring/summer — drive the capacity; autumn/November Stockholm — raise the capital that funds the next leg.**

### Revised Tour Sequence (Recommended)

```
Q1 2027  Belgian Anchor (pilot)    Duisburg → Liège → Brussels (2 days)
         Prove two-axis format; pull Athens/Budapest funds to Brussels

Q3 2027  Nordic Industrial Leg     Gothenburg → Tampere (2 days)
         Volvo/SKF + Metso/Valmet; build enterprise pilot pipeline

Nov 2027 Stockholm PE/LP Cluster   PE Insights Nordics + Institutional Forum
         Convert Nordic industrial pipeline into capital commitments

2028     Full Circuit               Add Dublin; revisit Belgian anchor at scale
```

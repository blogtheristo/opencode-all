## Purpose

This sheet audits the five EU digital-sovereignty claims used in DWS IQ 6 outreach. Each claim is rated **CONFIRMED**, **CONFIRMED (with caveat)**, or **OUTDATED**, with the source and the phrasing approved for external use. The rule: do not propagate a number into a sales deck, landing page, or investor note unless it appears here as CONFIRMED. One claim ("zero payments alternatives") is **OUTDATED** as of 2026 and must not be used in its original form.

## 1. Defense Technology Dependency — ✅ CONFIRMED (with caveat)

**Claim:** EU defense procurement is ~78% from non-EU suppliers, ~63% from the US.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED, but the figure is **window-specific**. The 78% non-EU / 63% US figure refers to EU member-state defense acquisitions during **February 2022 – June 2023** (the first 16 months after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine), per IRIS / European Commission analysis cited in the 2024 European Defence Industrial Strategy (EDIS).

**Caveat for use:** Always attach the date window. This is a surge-period figure, not a steady-state average. EU defense procurement diversification efforts (EDIRPA, EDIP) post-date the measurement window.

**Approved phrasing:**

> "In the 16 months after February 2022, EU member states sourced roughly 78% of their defense acquisitions outside the EU — about 63% from the United States." (Source: EDIS 2024 / IRIS)

## 2. Payments Independence — ❌ OUTDATED

**Claim (original):** "Zero major independent EU alternatives to Visa / Mastercard / SWIFT."

**Verdict:** OUTDATED as of 2026. **Do not use in this form.**

**Why it fails:** The European Payments Initiative (EPI) launched **Wero**, a pan-European wallet and instant-payments scheme, now live for P2P and rolling out for e-commerce across Germany, France, Belgium, and the Netherlands, with a reported user base in the tens of millions (~43M cited in 2026 coverage). Wero is explicitly positioned as the European sovereign alternative to US card networks. Separately, the digital euro (ECB) is in its preparation phase. The "zero alternatives" framing was defensible ~2020 but is factually wrong in 2026.

**Approved corrected phrasing:**

> "Europe historically lacked a homegrown card network and still routes most card payments through Visa and Mastercard — but that is now changing: the European Payments Initiative's Wero wallet is live and scaling as a sovereign alternative, and the ECB's digital euro is in preparation. The dependency is real but no longer total."

**Sales implication:** Use the _direction of travel_ (Europe is building sovereign payments rails) as supporting context for "Europe is reasserting digital sovereignty across the stack" — do not assert a vacuum that no longer exists.

## 3. Cybersecurity Reliance — ✅ CONFIRMED (qualitative)

**Claim:** EU has heavy reliance on non-EU cybersecurity vendors and tooling.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED as a **qualitative** statement. The EU's cybersecurity tooling, threat intelligence, and endpoint/SIEM markets are dominated by US vendors (Microsoft, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, etc.). ENISA and the EU Cyber Resilience Act context both acknowledge supply-side dependency.

**Caveat for use:** Avoid attaching a single hard percentage — the market is fragmented and no single authoritative "% non-EU" figure is clean enough to quote. Keep it qualitative.

**Approved phrasing:**

> "Europe's cybersecurity stack — endpoint, SIEM, threat intelligence — is dominated by non-EU (largely US) vendors, a dependency ENISA and the Cyber Resilience Act both acknowledge."

---

## 4. Cloud Market Share — ✅ CONFIRMED

**Claim:** US firms hold ~65–70% of the EU cloud market.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED. Synergy Research Group data puts the combined AWS + Microsoft Azure + Google Cloud share of the European cloud infrastructure market at ~70%, while European providers hold roughly 13–15%. This has been stable-to-rising for several years despite EU sovereign-cloud initiatives (Gaia-X, EU sovereign cloud offerings).

**Approved phrasing:**

> "The three US hyperscalers — AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud — hold roughly 70% of Europe's cloud infrastructure market; European providers hold around 13–15%." (Source: Synergy Research Group)

**CLOUD Act wedge:** This is the strongest sovereignty hook for DWS IQ 6. Data on a US hyperscaler is subject to the US CLOUD Act regardless of where the data physically sits in the EU. DWS IQ 6's EU-sovereign hosting removes that exposure — the core differentiator in the Brussels sovereign data briefing.

---

## 5. Aggregate Digital Dependency — ✅ CONFIRMED

**Claim:** The EU depends on non-EU providers for over 80% of its digital technologies and services.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED. This is the canonical figure from the **Draghi Report on EU Competitiveness** (Mario Draghi, September 2024), which states the EU depends on non-EU providers for over 80% of its digital products, services, infrastructure, and intellectual property.

**Approved phrasing:**

> "The EU depends on non-EU providers for more than 80% of its digital technologies, services, and infrastructure." (Source: Draghi Report on EU Competitiveness, September 2024)

**This is the headline number.** It is authoritative, recent, and from the single most-cited European competitiveness document of the decade. Lead with it.

## Usage Rules

1. **Lead with claim #5** (Draghi 80%) — most authoritative, sets the frame.
2. **Use claim #4** (cloud 70%) as the concrete, quotable hook into the CLOUD Act wedge.
3. **Attach the date window** to claim #1 (defense) every time.
4. **Never use the original claim #2** (payments "zero alternatives") — use the corrected phrasing only.
5. **Keep claim #3** (cybersecurity) qualitative; do not invent a percentage.

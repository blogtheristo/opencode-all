## Executive decision

DWS IQ should become an **enterprise research-and-decision system**, not a broad catalog of AI services.

“Doctorate-level” and “post-doctorate-level” must describe observable behavior, not model branding:

- **Doctorate-level intelligence:** answers a difficult, bounded enterprise question through an explicit method; uses traceable evidence; states assumptions and uncertainty; and survives independent reproduction, expert review, and audit.
- **Post-doctorate-level intelligence:** identifies a material question the enterprise has not framed; proposes competing hypotheses; runs governed tests, simulations, or counterfactual analyses; produces new validated industrial knowledge; and converts it into a governed decision or customer-owned capability.

> **Growth thesis:** DWS IQ grows when each customer decision improves the governed intelligence system, and each validated learning becomes reusable Customer-Retained Capital without leaking customer-confidential knowledge.

The product-line test is: **Does the offer produce an audit-surviving decision, improve a governed knowledge asset, or directly enable those outcomes in a way the enterprise can inspect?**

## 1. Research basis

- Stanford HAI reported 78% organizational AI use in 2024 and 71% generative-AI use in at least one function, while financial gains were usually modest and formal responsible-AI evaluation remained uncommon.
- McKinsey's 2025 survey reported 88% AI use, but only 23% scaling agentic AI somewhere in the enterprise and 39% reporting measurable enterprise-level EBIT impact.
- NIST AI RMF organizes trustworthy deployment around **Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage**. DWS IQ must expose all four as product behavior.
- EU AI Act Articles 12 and 14 make logging and effective human oversight central for high-risk systems.
- OECD work on AI-supported evaluation emphasizes evidence quality, methodological rigor, governance, and the limits of automated causal interpretation.

Enterprise buyers will not believe a doctorate-level claim because DWS has many agents, skills, models, or benchmarks. They will believe a repeatable chain from question to evidence to governed decision to measured outcome to retained knowledge.

## 2. Primary Research Question

> **RQ0: How can DWS IQ transform enterprise data, regulatory evidence, domain ontologies, and human expertise into reproducible, audit-grade decisions and novel validated industrial knowledge that measurably increases Monthly Governed Decisions and Customer-Retained Capital across regulated industries while preserving sovereignty, confidentiality, and effective human oversight?**

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Enterprises using the DWS IQ intelligence loop will produce more audit-surviving governed decisions, in less decision-cycle time and with better calibrated confidence, while retaining more reusable knowledge capital than comparable expert-only, generic-copilot, or ungoverned-agent workflows.

The hypothesis fails if DWS IQ cannot outperform an agreed baseline on decision quality, cycle time, auditability, or retained knowledge after accounting for cost and human-review effort.

## 3. Research questions DWS must answer

### Enterprise value and buyer

1. Which recurring decisions create the greatest regulatory, operational, safety, revenue, or capital-allocation value in each industry?
2. Which role owns each decision, supplies evidence, can veto it, and signs the economic case?
3. What proof must R03/R04 research leaders, R13/R15 technical approvers, and R22/R23 executive sponsors see before they call DWS IQ intelligent?
4. Which decisions are repeated and data-rich enough to become products rather than consulting projects?

### Knowledge and evidence

5. What minimum ontology, evidence, temporal context, and customer knowledge does each decision require?
6. How will every material claim link to a source, observation, rule, calculation, or expert judgment?
7. How will DWS distinguish fact, inference, hypothesis, simulation, and recommendation?
8. Which learning may generalize across customers, and which must remain tenant-private?

### Reasoning and discovery

9. Can DWS compare competing hypotheses rather than produce one plausible narrative?
10. Can it perform scenario, sensitivity, causal, and counterfactual analysis where needed?
11. Can it identify missing evidence and design the next cheapest useful observation or experiment?
12. Can it discover and prospectively validate a previously unknown industrial relationship?

### Evaluation, governance, and economics

13. What expert-created gold cases represent real enterprise difficulty rather than demo difficulty?
14. How reproducible is an answer across models, runs, data perturbations, and reviewers?
15. Are confidence estimates calibrated against observed correctness and outcomes?
16. What are the unsupported-claim, evidence-omission, tool-failure, planning-failure, and human-override rates?
17. Can an auditor reconstruct each claim, its evidence, model, tool, and human action?
18. Can the enterprise set autonomy limits, stop conditions, data boundaries, and escalation rules?
19. How are model, regulation, data, and ontology drift detected before they change a decision?
20. Which outcomes increase Monthly Governed Decisions (MGD), and which create Customer-Retained Capital (CRC)?
21. How does approved customer learning become a reusable evaluation, ontology pattern, skill, or workflow without exposing confidential data?
22. Which offers cannot accumulate intelligence and should be separated, bundled, or retired?

## 4. Intelligence maturity model

| Level | Name                              | Observable behavior                                                                    | Enterprise proof                                              |
| ----: | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
|     0 | Utility                           | Executes a fixed task                                                                  | Successful completion                                         |
|     1 | Assistant                         | Retrieves, drafts, or summarizes                                                       | Sources and human acceptance                                  |
|     2 | Specialist                        | Applies bounded domain rules and tools                                                 | Domain test set and expert review                             |
|     3 | Governed decision intelligence    | Recommends with evidence, uncertainty, lineage, controls, and HIC approval             | Audit replay, calibrated evaluation, measured outcome         |
|     4 | Doctorate-level intelligence      | Answers a difficult bounded question through a reproducible method                     | Independent reproduction, adversarial review, expert baseline |
|     5 | Post-doctorate-level intelligence | Generates and validates a novel hypothesis that changes enterprise action or knowledge | Prospective validation and reusable customer-owned knowledge  |

Only Levels 3–5 belong in the flagship DWS IQ Intelligence line. Levels 0–2 may remain components or onboarding services but must not headline the intelligence claim.

## 5. Intelligence Product Gate

Every flagship product must prove:

1. a named high-value **decision**;
2. an accountable **owner and HIC intervention point**;
3. claim-level **evidence and provenance**;
4. an explicit **method, assumptions, calculations, and alternatives**;
5. calibrated **uncertainty, abstention, and escalation**;
6. expert gold cases, adversarial cases, repeatability, and a comparative **evaluation**;
7. Article 12 lineage, audit replay, access control, and Article 14 **governance**;
8. drift detection and a governed **learning loop**;
9. measured cycle time, risk removed, financial effect, MGD, and CRC;
10. an inspectable evidence graph, decision replay, evaluation card, and outcome dashboard.

Score each gate 0–3: absent, claimed, demonstrated, independently validated. Passing requires no zero, production proof for Gates 1, 3, 6, 7, and 9, and at least 24/30.

## 6. Strategy loop

1. **Observe:** capture a real decision, outcome, failure, regulation, signal, or expert correction.
2. **Question:** formulate the decision question and competing hypotheses.
3. **Map:** bind roles, ontology, evidence, controls, and economic value.
4. **Evaluate first:** define gold cases, failure cases, baseline, threshold, and stop conditions.
5. **Run:** execute with claim-level evidence and uncertainty.
6. **HIC decision:** approve, override, request evidence, or stop.
7. **Measure:** record quality, cycle time, review effort, economic effect, MGD, and CRC.
8. **Learn:** convert approved learning into an evaluation case, ontology update, skill, policy, or threshold.
9. **Productize:** expose the capability through product, API/CLI, MCP, dashboard, and audit pack.
10. **Scale:** sell the proven decision to the next role, site, business unit, or industry.

Review failures weekly, prioritize by MGD/CRC monthly, gate every SKU quarterly, and independently reproduce Level 4–5 claims annually.

### Quarterly strategic review layer (adopted from external review, 05.08.2026 — see §13)

The ten steps above are the operational loop. Once per quarter, before the SKU
gating pass, answer five positioning questions in order; each answer must cite
Evidence Room artifacts, not intentions:

1. **Purpose** — why does an enterprise buyer pay for DWS IQ instead of a generic LLM wrapper or legacy GRC tool this quarter?
2. **Landscape** — where are incumbent tools currently failing on hallucination, reproducibility, or passive data collection, and which of those failures do we exploit?
3. **Climate** — which regulatory forces (EU AI Act enforcement wave, CSRD double-materiality audits, CBAM tightening) are creating demand for defensible evidence right now?
4. **Doctrine** — is the non-negotiable still holding: no ungrounded assertion reaches a customer without a verifiable audit chain (TRUTH row 35; Gate 3)?
5. **Execution** — how fast did customer feedback change an evaluation case, ontology, or threshold last quarter (loop steps 7–8), and what blocked it?

## 7. Enterprise-visible proof

Create one **DWS IQ Intelligence Evidence Room** with decision maps, claim-to-source graphs, governed-decision replays, model/tool lineage, human interventions, confidence calibration, known failure modes, evaluation results, baseline comparisons, MGD/CRC trends, ontology drift history, validated discoveries, and a redacted customer case.

This replaces fleet-size, capability-inventory, compute-throughput, and frontier-model comparison claims as the primary proof of intelligence.

## 9. Recommended architecture

1. **DWS IQ Intelligence Core** — governed decision engine, evidence graph, evaluation system, HIC controls, MGD/CRC.
2. **Intelligence Functions** — Compliance, ESG, HSE, Construction, Sovereignty, Commercial/GTM, and Themis.
3. **Industry Research Pods** — ontology, evaluations, regulatory models, and validated discoveries.
4. **Deployment and Adoption** — sovereign cloud/edge, integrations, training, implementation, and signed evidence; never presented as the intelligence itself.

> One intelligence core, multiple enterprise functions, industry-specific research depth, and optional sovereign deployment.

## 10. First 90-day programme

- **Days 1–30:** select one compliance, one operational/safety, and one commercial decision; assign owners; build 30–50 expert gold cases each; measure current baselines.
- **Days 31–60:** implement evidence, assumptions, competing hypotheses, calibrated confidence, and audit replay; run repeatability, perturbation, cross-model, and expert-review tests.
- **Days 61–90:** identify one high-value unknown per workflow; design a test or prospective prediction; validate at least one hypothesis; convert it into customer-owned ontology/evaluation capital; publish a redacted Evidence Room.

## 11. Success criteria

The strategy works when MGD and CRC grow; evidence coverage and calibration improve; unsupported claims and overrides decline; cycle time and review effort fall; at least one validated discovery per quarter changes an enterprise decision; and core recurring revenue grows faster than stand-alone services.

It is not working if growth is explained mainly by more agents, SKUs, pilots, model calls, or consulting hours.

## 14. Academic interlock — the four-year LUT doctorate as an external validation pathway

The doctorate-level claim in this strategy must not be self-certified. The LUT PhD
programme (4 years, 2027–2030, supervision discussions with Prof. Kari
Smolander in progress) can independently test and publish evidence relevant to it. Each DWS IQ
version supplies the production testbed that makes the research questions
falsifiable (DWS 7 customer evidence → Article I–II; DWS 8 carbon ledger →
Article II–III; the §5 Product Gate scores → Article III's measurement
instrument), and each peer-reviewed article can become an Evidence Room artifact —
public, citable evidence relevant to Level 4 behavior. Product-level claims still
require the independent reproduction and evaluation defined by the Product Gate. Full plan, year-by-year map, shared success scoreboard, and risk
controls: `docs/lut/dws-iq-phd-interlock-roadmap-2026-2030.md`.

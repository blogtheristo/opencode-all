## Campaign thesis (one line)

> **Better steel wins under CBAM — if you can prove it, price it, and publish it safely. DWS IQ is that machinery.**

CBAM is the EU's carbon tariff, and Outokumpu is on the _winning_ side of it: low-carbon EAF stainless made on recycled scrap and low-CO₂ Nordic electricity, competing against high-carbon imports that now carry an embedded-emissions cost at the EU border. Every tightening of CBAM widens Outokumpu's price wedge. But the premium is only captured where three things hold in the tender room:

1. The buyer **trusts Outokumpu's carbon numbers** (verifier-grade proof, not marketing claims).
2. The seller **knows the CBAM-adjusted price of the competing import** (the cost-of-alternative math, per CN code, per origin, at the current certificate price).
3. The proof **doesn't leak process IP** while doing 1 and 2 (the existing IP-governance wedge).

DWS IQ is the only stack that does all three on EU-sovereign infrastructure — one dedicated CBAM steel-sector agent (full Annex I coverage), KYA-governed disclosure logic, and per-tender carbon-price intelligence.

## Market signal — Outokumpu is already running this play (2026-07-06)

Observed by Risto on X/Twitter (screenshot on file, session upload 2026-07-06): a **paid Outokumpu ad** ("Mainos") — _"How can stainless steel importers reduce CBAM complexity? Watch our webinar for practical insights on how CBAM impacts your business and supply chain."_ — promoting a **"CBAM and Stainless Steel" webinar** hosted on outokumpu.com, showing ~**4.9M impressions**.

What this changes:

1. **Thesis confirmed externally.** Outokumpu's marketing is already positioning CBAM as a commercial weapon and paying to distribute it at scale. The campaign is not proposing a new idea to Outokumpu — it is selling the machinery behind a motion they have already budgeted.
2. **Their angle is sharper than "we're greener": it's "buy EU-made, skip CBAM entirely."** The ad targets **importers** — buyers currently sourcing non-EU stainless who carry the CBAM declarant burden (registration, quarterly data, certificates). EU-produced Outokumpu steel removes that burden completely, not just the certificate cost. Fold this into pillar 2: the cost-of-alternative math is certificate cost **plus** the buyer's compliance overhead.
3. **A webinar funnel needs fulfilment machinery.** 4.9M impressions of "we reduce your CBAM complexity" generates inbound buyer questions their sales engineering must answer — per-CN-code numbers, verifier-grade proof, supplier data forms. That is exactly the manual-fulfilment-desk pain in the existing INTRO (R10 Head of Customer Engineering) — now amplified by their own marketing spend. The DWS line: _"Your marketing just promised CBAM simplicity to 4.9 million people. We're the machinery that keeps that promise at quote time."_
4. **Recon before outreach.** Watch the webinar; note what math they show (do they quote certificate prices? per-origin intensities?), who presents (names = campaign-lane contacts), and what they _can't_ show (live per-tender math, verifier-grade per-quote proof, IP-governed disclosure) — that gap list is the first-meeting agenda.

## The market logic (why now, post-Omnibus)

Canonical facts per `DWS_TRUTH_STANDARD.md` §2C — use these figures externally, nothing older:

- CBAM definitive period is live since **1 Jan 2026**. The ~50-tonne de-minimis exempts most small importers while keeping **~99% of embedded emissions covered** — the price signal on industrial-scale stainless imports is fully intact.
- Omnibus asymmetry (established messaging, `Sales/Myyntikirje_3_Omnibus_Pivot.md`): **Brussels cuts paperwork, not the carbon price.** CSRD scope shrank ~90%; the ETS price, CBAM trajectory, and Fit for 55 targets did not move. For a low-carbon producer this is the _best_ regulatory mix: competitors' cost stays, reporting drag falls.
- Free-allocation phase-out under ETS transfers more of the carbon cost onto imports each year through 2034 — the CBAM wedge **widens on a published schedule**. The premium conversation gets easier every year; the producer who builds the proof machinery first compounds the advantage.

The steel-specific math (confirm current figures before quoting externally): high-carbon stainless routes (e.g. NPI-based Asian production) carry an embedded intensity several multiples of Outokumpu's scrap-EAF route. At industrial tonnages and current EU carbon prices, the certificate cost per tonne of imported high-carbon stainless is a **material, quotable number** — and that number belongs in every Outokumpu tender as the cost-of-alternative line.

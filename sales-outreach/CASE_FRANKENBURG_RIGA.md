# Case Study (Illustrative Target) — DWS IQ Aegis written in Frankenburg Riga FieldFoundry case

> **Framing note:** This is an _illustrative target case_. It maps the DWS IQ Aegis sovereign dual-use manufacturing layer onto Frankenburg Technologies to show fit and value — it does **not** claim an existing contract, pilot, or engagement with Frankenburg. Frankenburg facts are drawn only from the public opening statement; DWS capability is sourced to `Store/PRODUCT_AEGIS.md`. Use as a worked example in dual-use sales conversations.

## 1. Why Frankenburg

In June 2026 Frankenburg Technologies opened the **Riga Weapon System & Missile Assembly Factory** — described as the world's first affordable air-defence missile mass-production facility — supporting production of **Mark I**, its affordable guided air-defence missile built to counter mass drone and aerial threats. The opening was attended by **Andrius Kubilius, European Commissioner for Defence and Space**, signalling EU-institutional weight behind the programme.

Riga is the first operational step toward annual capacity for **1,000,000 affordable guided air-defence missiles**. Together with the **Ādaži** final-assembly site now under construction, Riga forms Frankenburg's first Latvian **FieldFoundry** production system, planned to reach **100 missiles per day by the end of 2026**. Next FieldFoundry sites are planned in **Estonia, the United Kingdom and Poland**.

That profile — a rapidly scaling EU defence prime, building a _replicable multi-country manufacturing system_ for dual-use critical production, under direct EU policy attention — is exactly the customer DWS IQ Aegis is built to serve. Aegis "optimizes and secures physical production facilities, not the tactical frontline" (`Store/PRODUCT_AEGIS.md`): the factory and its supply chain, not the missile in flight.

## 3. The dual-use / sovereignty angle

Frankenburg is not only a missile manufacturer — it is a **dual-use critical-manufacturing base** whose supply chain is a national- and EU-resilience asset. The same EU gap that DWS IQ Aegis was built to close applies directly:

- **No European sovereign baseline.** A defence prime that adopts US-owned data infrastructure inherits CLOUD Act exposure, an EU AI Act Article 12 gap, and no agent-governance layer (`partners-outreach/EU_SOVEREIGN_C2_DATA_LAYER_BRIEF.md`). Aegis is the EU-sovereign counterpart — single-tenant, EU-only deployment, **zero CLOUD Act exposure**.
- **Buy-European credibility.** Aegis is positioned as **EDIP Article 9 eligible** (EU establishment, no third-country control), three-pillar sovereign (data / technology / operational), and PiTuKri-audited — the exact procurement posture an EU-funded defence programme with Commission-level visibility must defend.
- **Scale is the thesis.** Frankenburg's value is the _FieldFoundry pattern_ — a production system replicated across Latvia, Estonia, the UK and Poland. A data + agent-governance layer that is itself replicable per site, sovereign per jurisdiction, and compliant by construction is the natural companion to that pattern.

## 4. Business model & economics

- **Aegis platform:** from **€59,990** onboarding + integration plus **€999/mo** subscription, across three tiers — Standard / **Defence** / **Sovereign** (`Store/PRODUCT_AEGIS.md`). The Defence and Sovereign tiers map to a programme of Frankenburg's classification.
- **Priced to the work it replaces:** the compliance, export-control, sovereignty-audit and AI-governance effort a mass-production defence facility must staff anyway — delivered as agents, at SaaS margin.
- **Scales with the FieldFoundry footprint:** per-site deployment of the sovereign data + agent layer turns each new FieldFoundry (Estonia, UK, Poland) into incremental recurring revenue, each single-tenant and jurisdiction-sovereign.

## 5. Entry path

1. **Sovereignty + compliance assessment:** run the SovereigntyMonitor over the Riga/Ādaži data estate; deliver a CLOUD Act exposure score, EU data-residency map, and NIS2 / EU AI Act gap report.
2. **Article 12 audit pilot:** instrument one production/quality decision flow with Article12Logger; produce a court-defensible AI-decision audit trail under the KYA Standard.
3. **Supply-chain intelligence:** light up CrossVerticalIntel + ComplianceAgent on the Mark I bill-of-materials for dual-use export-control and CRM/CBAM/CSRD coverage.
4. **Replicate per FieldFoundry:** package the sovereign data + agent layer as a per-site deployment for Ādaži, then Estonia, the UK and Poland — sovereign per jurisdiction, EDIP Article 9 eligible.

## 6. Related targets — the FieldFoundry expansion

The same playbook extends along Frankenburg's own announced roadmap:

- **Ādaži (LV)** — the final-assembly site now under construction completes the first Latvian FieldFoundry; the SiteSenseAgent + sovereignty layer apply during build-out and from day one of operation.
- **Estonia, United Kingdom, Poland** — each planned FieldFoundry is a fresh single-tenant, jurisdiction-sovereign Aegis deployment. Estonia anchors the **Lifetime OÜ** leg of the group; the UK and Poland extend the Buy-European, EDIP-eligible footprint into two more NATO/EU defence-industrial markets.

Together, the Latvian anchor plus the Estonia/UK/Poland expansion give the pitch a **multi-country, single-prime dual-use case** — one sovereign manufacturing-intelligence deployment per FieldFoundry, matching Frankenburg's replicate-and-scale model. All remain illustrative target cases, not claimed engagements.

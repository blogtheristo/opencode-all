## The wedge — "Your water and fibre chemicals are critical-infrastructure inputs — so your AI has to be sovereign"

Kemira's products sit at two regulated intersections at once:

1. **Critical infrastructure.** Water-treatment and process chemicals feed municipal water utilities and energy/industrial sites — squarely inside the **CER Directive** (critical-entity resilience) and **NIS2** perimeter. Customers in these segments increasingly require their suppliers to attest to sovereign, auditable data handling.
2. **Dual-use supply chain.** Speciality and fibre chemicals are part of the manufacturing base that supplies Europe's defence and critical-raw-material sector — bringing **export control (Reg. 2021/821)**, **CRM Act**, and **EDIP** (EU Defence Industrial Programme) eligibility into scope, on top of the climate stack every chemicals producer already carries (**CBAM**, **ETS Phase 4**, **CSRD**).

Today, the AI tooling Kemira's teams reach for to handle these disclosures and analyses typically runs on US hyperscalers. For a critical-infrastructure and dual-use supplier, that is a **CLOUD Act exposure surface** over process data and customer data, and an **EU AI Act compliance gap**. The wedge: the compliance burden Kemira already carries can be turned into an _attestable advantage_ — but only on a sovereign stack. That is exactly what Aegis Defence is.

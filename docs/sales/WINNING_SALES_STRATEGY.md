## The Core Question

> "Why do people actually say yes?"
> — Noah (sales practitioner, LinkedIn, June 2026)

The answer is the same whether you are driving an Uber in Helsinki, selling tech in a retail store, or closing a €250k Fleet Admiral seat:

**People say yes when they feel understood — not when they feel sold to.**

This strategy codifies that insight into a repeatable system for DWS IQ 6 and Lifetime Oy.

## 2. The Silence Principle

> "Sometimes silence is the best ask. Listen until the customer is ready to give yes."
> — Risto Paarni

This is the most counterintuitive sales rule and the most Nordic one:

**Do not fill silence. The customer is thinking. Thinking is buying.**

### How Silence Works in Practice

| Context            | Wrong Move                           | Right Move                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Uber ride          | Talk non-stop                        | Play music, drive well, let the passenger choose the conversation      |
| LinkedIn DM        | 3-paragraph pitch in connection note | Connect with no note. Let your content speak.                          |
| Enterprise meeting | Slide 47 of your deck                | Ask one question. Wait. The answer tells you more than 47 slides.      |
| Investor call      | Fill every pause with metrics        | State the number. Stop. Let them do the math.                          |
| Procurement        | Daily follow-ups                     | Submit the proposal. One follow-up. Then wait. The price is the price. |

### The Nordic Sales Cadence

```
Day 0:  Connection (no note, or max 2 sentences)
Day 2:  If accepted — short bilingual DM (FI/EN)
Day 7:  If no response — share a relevant article
Day 30: Silence. Content does the work.
        No further direct outreach.
```

**Maximum 2 touches.** After that, your work, your content, and your reputation do the selling. If the answer is no, silence tells you. If the answer is "not yet," silence preserves the door.

## 5. The Three Doors — Adapted from Captain Madonna Funnel

Every enterprise deal has exactly three doors. You pick one. You knock once.

### Door #1: The Pain Owner (Strategic Sponsor)

The person whose daily work gets measurably worse if they do nothing.

- **Steel:** IP Director — CBAM disclosures are IP filings in disguise
- **Chemicals:** VP Product Strategy — dual-use compliance is a revenue blocker
- **Construction:** Head of Sustainability — EN 15978 embodied carbon is coming in 2028
- **Financial services:** CISO — EU AI Act high-risk AI decisions need audit trails

**Rule:** Lead with the role that has the strongest revenue or cost lever, not the role with the most authority.

### Door #2: The Diagonal Win

Once Door #1 opens, every other role must see their own win in the same headline:

> "DWS helps [Company] sell more by removing compliance friction before the sale."

- **IP Director** wins: trade-secret governance automated
- **CSO** wins: CSRD reporting is a byproduct, not a project
- **CIO** wins: one vendor replaces five point solutions
- **CRO** wins: faster tender response, higher win rate
- **CEO** wins: board narrative on sovereignty and compliance readiness

### Door #3: The Close That Is Not a Close

> "We are not looking for fast money. We are looking for partners who understand that regulated markets reward patience and precision. If that is your style, this vineyard has a plot for you."

No ultimatums. No discount deadlines. No "limited slots remaining."

The close is an invitation, not a trap.

## 6. Signal-Based Timing — When to Knock

From signaalimyynti: reach out only when there is a real signal. Noah's retail insight — "figuring out who's who, fast" — is the same skill applied at enterprise scale.

| Signal Type     | Example                                             | Action                                                                       |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Regulatory**  | CBAM reporting deadline approaches                  | Outreach to CBAM-exposed importers 90 days before                            |
| **Hiring**      | Company posts for "Sustainability Manager"          | They are building capacity — offer the platform that multiplies the new hire |
| **Event**       | Prospect speaks at FinnBuild / Bauma / AI Stockholm | Attend, listen to their talk, reference it in connection                     |
| **Pain public** | CSRD audit finding reported in annual report        | "We saw your 2025 report. Here is how three peers automated that finding."   |
| **Referral**    | Existing customer mentions a peer's name            | Warm intro via customer — strongest signal                                   |

**Rule:** If there is no signal, there is no outreach. Cold spray is a brand tax.

## 7. The Five-Star Framework

Risto's Uber insight: **every interaction is rated, whether formally or not.**

In enterprise sales, the "stars" are invisible but real:

| Star | What It Means           | How You Earn It                                                                 |
| ---- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | They remember your name | Professional, clean first impression (VIP service model)                        |
| 2    | They open your message  | Signal-based timing — your outreach is relevant                                 |
| 3    | They respond            | You asked one good question instead of pitching                                 |
| 4    | They take a meeting     | Your content / case study answered a question they already had                  |
| 5    | They say yes            | You removed their anxiety, matched their pace, and waited until they were ready |

**You cannot jump from 1 to 5.** Each star is earned sequentially. The Silence Principle protects the progression — pushing too fast resets to zero.

## 8. Tactical Playbook — Weekly Rhythm

| Day           | Activity                                                                      | Volume         | Metaphor            |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| **Monday**    | Signal scan — regulatory updates, hiring signals, event calendars             | 30 min         | Reading People      |
| **Tuesday**   | LinkedIn connections — signal-matched prospects only                          | 20 connections | Short Windows       |
| **Wednesday** | Content creation — one technical article or case insight                      | 1 post         | Consistency         |
| **Thursday**  | Follow-ups — only Day 2 responses to Monday/Tuesday connections               | As needed      | Silence Principle   |
| **Friday**    | Reflection — what worked, what signals emerged, what to prepare for next week | 30 min         | Attention to Detail |

**Monthly:** 80 new connections, 4 content pieces, 2-4 meetings from inbound responses.

**Rule:** No outreach on Friday. Friday is for preparation and thinking. Nordic buyers do not make Friday decisions.

## 9. What Noah Got Right

Noah's progression from kennel attendant to B2B outreach at National Black Car is the same journey every good salesperson makes:

1. **Service first** — you earn the right to sell by caring about the small things
2. **Read the room** — scripts get you started, understanding gets you the close
3. **Adjust naturally** — upselling that feels like helping is the only upselling that works
4. **Short interactions, real conversations** — time is not the variable; relevance is
5. **Figure out the real reason** — connect their reason to what you offer, and the yes follows

This is exactly what DWS does at platform level. The AI agents do not pitch. They listen (ingest data), understand (industry ontology), adjust (per-customer World Model), and deliver (scored, audit-ready output in minutes). The platform sells itself the same way a five-star driver earns the rating: by being excellent before the customer asks.

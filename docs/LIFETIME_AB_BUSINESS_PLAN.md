## 1. Why Lifetime AB, why Stockholm, why now

### 1.1 The market is in Sweden
Sweden hosts the EU's strongest **fossil-free / low-CO₂ steel and CBAM-exposed industrial
cluster**: SSAB (fossil-free leader), Stegra (ex-H2 Green Steel, Boden), LKAB (HYBRIT
iron), Sandvik, Alleima, Boliden, plus Outokumpu's Avesta site. Every one of them faces
the same forcing functions our wedge addresses: CBAM definitive declarations (Q3 2026),
EU AI Act Article 12 logging (Aug 2026), CSRD assurance, and the disclosure-as-IP risk.

### 1.2 A Swedish entity removes friction
- **Contracting:** Swedish industrials and the public sector prefer a Swedish counterparty
  (org.nr, F-skatt, SEK invoicing option, Swedish governing law).
- **Presence & language:** Stockholm presence + Swedish-language motion signals commitment
  (the Nordic strategy's "evidence over energy" rule).
- **Investors:** Swedish climate/deeptech capital (e.g. 2150, EQT Ventures, Norrsken,
  Industrifonden) reads a Swedish AB as in-market.

### 1.3 Why now (clustered deadline window)
SSAB's 2026 commercial fossil-free launch across all product groups + CBAM Q3 2026 + AI
Act Art. 12 = the moment disclosures spike and the wedge bites. First-mover on the Swedish
steel cluster before US/legacy vendors retrofit.

### 1.4 Income model — the Uber cash engine

Near-term revenue is **Uber driving in Stockholm**, operated through Lifetime AB. This is
deliberate, not a fallback:

- **Self-funds the entity + presence:** Uber cash covers the AB's running costs and puts the
  founder physically in Stockholm — where SSAB, Stegra, Sandvik and the investors are.
- **Non-dilutive bootstrap:** cash flow without burning the raise or giving up equity.
- **Two engines:** (1) **Uber cash** — immediate, recurring; (2) **SSAB / enterprise** — the
  CBAM-wedge upside. Engine 1 buys the runway and the in-market presence to land engine 2.

The vehicle (§2.5) is the AB's productive asset for engine 1.

### 2.4 Uber operating setup (Sweden taxi licensing)

Uber in Sweden is regulated taxi work. Requirements:

- **Taxiförarlegitimation** (taxi driver licence) — medical certificate + driving exam + 3
  knowledge exams; ~2 months; **≈ 4,425 kr** (Transportstyrelsen).
- **Taxitrafiktillstånd** (taxi traffic permit) — held by the operator (the AB), **or** drive
  under a **fleet partner (åkare)** to avoid running your own permit (ongoing fee instead).
- **F-skatt + VAT** (Skatteverket), **commercial taxi insurance**, taxi vehicle registration
  (verify current taximeter / särskild utrustning rules with Transportstyrelsen).
- **Vehicle eligibility:** max **8 years** from registration (10 for EV), 4 doors, 5 seats;
  **Uber Comfort** needs a newer, roomier car.

Decision: run the permit **in the AB** (keeps margin, builds the asset) unless speed-to-income
favours starting under a fleet partner for month 1.

### 2.5 Vehicle — Corolla Hybrid floor + Ford study

**Minimum spec: Toyota Corolla Hybrid** — the ride-hailing benchmark (full hybrid ~4.5
L/100 km, proven high-mileage reliability, strong resale, UberX + Comfort eligible). New
campaign price **from ≈ 299,900 kr** (Touring Sports 1.8 Hybrid Active; ord. 334,900 kr).

**Ford alternative** evaluated against the **Ford 100** anniversary financing (2.95% loan;
25% service discount through 2026; Arval leasing). Closest comparables: **Kuga FHEV/PHEV**
(roomy, Comfort-tier) and **Puma** (cheaper, but mild-hybrid + small). Full analysis,
buy-vs-lease, and the **taxi-use caveat on privatleasing** in
`docs/LIFETIME_AB_VEHICLE_STUDY_FORD_VS_COROLLA.md`.

**Buy from Sweden (Sverige)** so registration, taxi-eligibility, warranty and service sit in
the AB's jurisdiction.

### 2.6 Corporate language — Finnish & English

**Lifetime AB's corporate and working languages are Finnish and English** (not Swedish).
English is primary for governance, contracts, board materials, technical documentation, and
investor/enterprise communication; Finnish is the parallel working language in lockstep with
parent **Lifetime Oy** and the **DWS Document Standard** (FI/EN language-pair rule). Swedish
is used as a courtesy to Swedish customers/authorities on request, not as an internal working
language. The registered **bolagsordning** is filed per Bolagsverket statutory requirements
(verify language requirement); this policy governs operating and commercial language, not the
statutory filing. Board-adoptable policy text maintained in the company handbook.

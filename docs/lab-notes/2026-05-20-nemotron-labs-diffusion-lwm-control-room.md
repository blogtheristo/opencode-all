## TL;DR (90 seconds)

NVIDIA released **Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion**, a family of **diffusion language models** (3B–14B, including vision-language variants). Unlike autoregressive LMs that emit one token at a time and commit irreversibly, diffusion LMs **generate many tokens in parallel within a single forward pass** and can **revise tokens during decoding**. Net effect: higher GPU utilization on Blackwell-class tensor cores and lower wall-clock latency for the kind of structured, multi-step outputs our agents produce.

For DWS this maps to three concrete places where the autoregressive bottleneck currently hurts:

1. **Control Room (per-industry Godagent)** — long structured compliance reports (CSRD, CBAM, EPBD) generated by `firehorse-report-*` skills. Today these stream token-by-token; diffusion decoding could collapse a multi-second narrative into a single parallel decode step on a Spark.
2. **Situation Room (Atlas orchestrator)** — executive summaries that fuse N Control-Room outputs. Latency is dominated by sequential drafting; diffusion is a natural fit for "draft the whole summary, then iterate".
3. **Vision-language variant** — direct candidate for SiteSense (construction safety, EN 13001) and any CCTV / drone-feed path on the V2 omnimodal LWM.

**Action this fortnight:** stand up a 15-minute spike with the 3B (or 8B if VRAM allows) Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion checkpoint against an offline copy of one compliance prompt and one SiteSense prompt. Measure tok/s, time-to-first-useful-output, and revision count. Compare against our current FP8 vLLM lane on the same prompt set. **No** V1 commit before V1 ships on June 12 — this is V1.x / V2 territory.

### Why parallel-token decoding is interesting (not just a curiosity)

Autoregressive decoding has a hard floor: latency ≈ `tokens × per-token forward pass`. Even at 200 tok/s, a 2,000-token CSRD compliance section is ~10 s of pure decode time, and **every token is committed** — if the model drifts at token 50, the only fix is to keep going or restart.

Diffusion decoding inverts this:

- The decoder emits **a large block of tokens at once**, then runs **K refinement steps** that *revise* individual tokens.
- GPU utilization on Blackwell is much higher because each forward pass is a wide matmul, not a thin one (closer to what tensor cores were designed for).
- Quality at fixed wall-clock is empirically competitive with autoregressive at 1B–14B scale; the open question for industrial work is **factual stability under revision** (see §Risks).

The published Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion family is the **first NVIDIA-supported** diffusion LM in this size class, which matters for us because the tensor-core, NeMo, TensorRT-LLM, and vLLM paths are the same paths we already use for the autoregressive LWM. Adoption cost is much lower than e.g. an academic diffusion LM with no inference engine.

## Mapping to the LWM and the two rooms

### 1. Control Room — per-industry Godagent

The Control Room is the **industry-facing** dashboard (`dws6.com/dashboard/control-room/`) backed by one Godagent per industry (Vulcan for Steel, Aegis for Construction, etc. — per `docs/Deputy-Agents-Architecture.md` §9.1). Today, the Godagent drives a chain of `firehorse-report-*` skills that produce CSRD board packs, CBAM exposure analyses, EN 15978 carbon reports, EPBD building reports.

These outputs share three properties:

- **Long** (1k–5k tokens of structured prose + tables).
- **Templated** (heading skeleton is known; the model fills in factual claims).
- **Bursty under deadline** (a customer with a CSRD reporting deadline does not want a 10-second drip).

Diffusion decoding fits all three. Parallel emission means the templated skeleton can be filled in essentially in one step; the revision passes then converge on factual content. **Expected control-room win:** time-to-first-useful-report drops from "tens of seconds" to "seconds" on a Spark. This is the kind of latency change customers feel.

**Caveat:** the V1 LWM ships on **June 12 2026** and is locked. Diffusion enters as a **V1.x inference path** (target: July–August dot-release) or as the **V2 default decoder** (November 2026). It is NOT a V1 ship blocker.

### 2. Situation Room — Atlas orchestrator

The Situation Room is the **executive surface** (`dws6.com/dashboard/situation-room/`) where Atlas fuses N Control-Room outputs into one cross-industry executive summary (per `docs/Deputy-Agents-Architecture.md` §9.2). Atlas is the single global orchestrator; only one Situation Room exists.

The Situation Room workload is **even more diffusion-friendly** than the Control Room because:

- The summary is **shorter** (300–800 tokens) — well within a single diffusion decode block.
- The structure is **highly templated** ("Top risks, top opportunities, top decisions needed").
- The factual content is **already grounded** in upstream Godagent outputs — diffusion's revision passes can lean on these as conditioning, reducing the hallucination surface.

**Expected situation-room win:** the executive summary becomes a **near-interactive surface** rather than a delayed read. CEO opens the Situation Room → summary is ready by the time the page paints.

### 3. Vision-language variant — SiteSense and V2 omnimodal

The vision-language Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion variant is the most strategically interesting piece for the **V2 LWM roadmap** (omnimodal, November 2026). Today our SiteSense skill (construction safety, EN 13001 + ISO 45001 + VNa 205/2009) processes still images and short clips through an external VLM call. A V2 LWM with a native diffusion VLM core means:

- **Frame-by-frame analysis** of CCTV / drone feeds at 8 FPS becomes economical on a single Spark (each frame is a short generation).
- **Parallel structured output** — bounding boxes + hazard categories + EN 13001 wind-speed call-outs emitted in one step instead of N autoregressive passes per frame.
- **Alignment with the NVFP4 thesis** in `docs/field-notes/2026-05-15-nvfp4-pretraining-for-control-room-lwm.md`: if the diffusion VLM also has an NVFP4-quantized checkpoint, we get **both** numerical-precision and decoding-algorithm wins on the same hardware.

This is the cleanest argument to make to the V2 design doc: **"V2 = omnimodal + NVFP4 + diffusion decoder"** as the three-axis upgrade over V1.

## Easiest path to actually try Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion (15-minute spike)

No internal training. No checkpoint surgery. Pick the smallest model that fits the GPU in front of you, run an inference engine that supports diffusion decoding, point an OpenAI-compat client at it.

| Model size | Min VRAM (rough) | Suggested host | Best for |
|-----------:|-----------------:|----------------|----------|
| **3B (text)** | ~6–8 GB | Laptop / 4060 / RTX 50-series | Smoke test, latency comparison |
| **8B (text)** | ~16 GB | RTX 4090 / RTX PRO 6000 / GN-100 / Spark | Control-room-class draft |
| **14B (text)** | ~28 GB | DGX Spark / GN-100 unified memory | Closest to LWM-class output |
| **VL variants** | depends on vision tower | Spark for the bigger one | SiteSense / V2 omnimodal spike |

**What the spike measures:**

- **tok/s wall-clock** vs our current FP8 vLLM lane on the same prompt.
- **Time-to-first-useful-output** — diffusion's "draft then refine" inverts the curve; first-token latency may be **higher** but full-output latency much lower. We want both numbers.
- **Revision count to stable output** — does it converge in 4 steps? 16? This decides whether the diffusion lane is interactive or batch.
- **Factual stability** — does revision *fix* hallucinations or *introduce* new ones? Run against one CSRD prompt with a known correct answer.

**What the spike does NOT validate:**

- LWM-specific industry evals (25 industries, EU regulatory corpora) — those are LWM-fine-tune-specific, not Nemotron-base.
- KV-cache behaviour at very long context — diffusion KV cache patterns are different from autoregressive; assume nothing.
- Customer-deploy licensing — confirm the license file on download before committing.

Owner for the spike: whoever's first to a GN-100 / Spark / 4090+ with an idle hour. **Default model: 8B text variant** for the Control Room latency comparison, **plus the smallest VL variant** for the SiteSense smoke test. Result: a one-page comparison appended to this note as Appendix A.

## Risks & honest limits

1. **Diffusion LMs are new at this size.** The published quality numbers for 3B–14B diffusion LMs are competitive with autoregressive peers on benchmarks, but the **industrial-prompt distribution is not on those benchmarks**. We will discover edge cases.
2. **Revision can re-introduce errors.** Autoregressive LMs commit and move on; diffusion LMs can *un-fix* a correct token during a later refinement step. For regulatory text where one wrong CN code is a compliance defect, this is a real risk. Mitigation: hard structural constraints (regex, JSON schema) on the decode loop, not just a prompt.
3. **Tooling maturity.** vLLM and TensorRT-LLM support for diffusion decoding is **less mature** than for autoregressive. Expect rougher edges than the FP8 lane.
4. **Latency profile is inverted.** First-token latency may be **worse** than autoregressive. For streaming UI (chat surface), this is a UX regression unless we either (a) batch and show the whole answer at once, or (b) stream from refinement step 2 onwards. The Situation Room "near-interactive summary" framing assumes (a).
5. **Vision-language variant size on GN-100.** Confirm the VL variant footprint before any V2 design lock-in. If it does not fit in 128 GB Spark unified memory alongside the LWM core, the V2 design needs a second box or a smaller VL variant.
6. **Licensing.** Standard NVIDIA Open Model License terms are usually fine for our customer-deploy posture, but **read the file on first download** — diffusion releases sometimes carry research-only restrictions.

## One-line thesis

> Parallel-token diffusion LMs collapse the dominant latency cost in templated, structured industrial outputs. The Control Room and Situation Room are the two surfaces where customers will feel the win first. Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion makes this evaluable on our existing NVIDIA stack today — but V1 stays autoregressive; diffusion is a V1.x / V2 lane.

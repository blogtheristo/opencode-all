## Page 1 — The Shrimp Boat Captain

There's a line in Forrest Gump that most people remember for the wrong reason. Bubba doesn't sell Forrest on the economics of shrimping. He doesn't pitch the TAM. He makes Forrest feel like he belongs on that boat. He gives him a role, a purpose, and a deck to stand on.

Risto Paarni has spent 35 years in industrial IT. Factory floors in Finland. ERP migrations across Scandinavia. Cloud architecture for manufacturers still running systems from the 1990s. He holds an M.Sc. in Industrial Management — the degree that sits at the intersection of engineering and business, exactly where compliance problems live.

Two years ago he looked at the EU's Fit for 55 package — ETS, CBAM, CSRD, EPBD, and a dozen more regulations hitting 16 industries simultaneously — and saw what no one was building: a single platform that treats every regulation as a data contract, maps them into one system, and automates compliance end-to-end.

Every regulation tells you what to measure. CBAM tells you what to report at the border. CSRD tells you how to disclose. They aren't separate problems. They're 15,000 rules in one system. And systems are what engineers build.

So he built it.

He still writes code daily. He manages the GitHub repository. He reviews architecture decisions. When a demo breaks, he fixes it himself. His philosophy: the CEO of an AI company should understand the models, the infrastructure, and the deployment constraints — not just the market opportunity.

That's the origin. Not a pivot from fintech. Not an MBA chasing a trend. An industrial engineer who spent three decades watching factories struggle with compliance — and finally built the tool they needed.

## Page 1B — The Long Road Nobody Saw

The story investors don't hear in a pitch meeting — because it doesn't fit on a slide.

Risto Paarni started the AI path in December 2018. He had completed a Data Specialist diploma and moved into Microsoft Cloud Architecture certification — a year of training before the word "AI" meant anything to the startup world. He followed up with IBM's full AI curriculum on Coursera: machine learning, neural networks, applied AI. Not weekend YouTube courses. Structured, certified programmes completed while working industrial IT jobs that the Finnish labour market considered his ceiling.

Then GPT-2 arrived in 2019 — and everything changed with transformers. The attention mechanism wasn't an incremental improvement. It was a new paradigm. Paarni recognised this immediately because he had already spent a year studying the underlying mathematics. This was his third engineering hat: after industrial management and industrial IT, he now carried *"what is new in AI, Edge, Cloud"* — the ability to evaluate emerging technology not as a spectator, but as a builder who understood the stack from silicon to API.

From 2015 to 2025 he built cloud-native applications in the Microsoft Founders Hub programme — ten years of shipping software inside Microsoft's ecosystem, with production deployments, not hackathon demos. The stack evolved with the platform: Azure AI Foundry for model orchestration, .NET Aspire for cloud-native distributed applications. He wasn't just a user of Microsoft's tools — he was a Founders Hub co-founder building production systems while others were still running tutorials.

Then came November 2022. ChatGPT went public and the world caught up to what Paarni had been studying for four years. He didn't need to "learn AI." He needed to apply it. The next three years became the most intense learning and building phase of his career: DWS versions 1 through 6, each version a complete rebuild, each one closer to what industrial compliance actually required.

DWS IQ 6 is now an InvestEU project (INPO-20254383) — validated by the European Commission's investment platform. Not an accelerator. Not an incubator. A project deemed eligible for EU-level institutional investment support.

**Here's the part the Finnish market never processed.**

In all these years — the IBM certifications, the Microsoft programme, the master's thesis, the six platform versions, the InvestEU validation — not a single Finnish employer saw value in hiring Risto Paarni. His CV is a 50-page technical narrative covering three decades of industrial IT, cloud architecture, AI systems, and EU regulation expertise. It interests nobody in Finland's hiring process.

Abroad, it's different. International accelerators accepted him. NVIDIA's Inception Program took him in. Google, AWS, and Groq handed over six-figure cloud credits. Construction companies in the Nordics signed pilot contracts. The European Commission listed his project.

Finland's system looked at the same person and saw unemployment statistics.

In November, he delivered a whitepaper and seminar presentation for the Metropolia Smart Factory M.Sc. programme — the kind of academic contribution that demonstrates deep domain expertise but earns no labour market recognition in Finland.

Today, Kela — the Finnish social security institution — acknowledged his thesis progress at Metropolia University of Applied Sciences (YAMK, Master's level) by denying his student support. Three years of work on a 30-credit Smart Factory thesis, and the system counted credits, not capability. The thesis will be completed. He won't need student support after that. If LUT University accepts him for doctoral studies, the work continues at a higher level.

This is not a complaint. It's context.

When an investor asks "why should I back this founder?" — the answer isn't the platform architecture or the regulatory wave. It's this: Paarni kept building when nobody was watching, nobody was paying, and nobody in his home country thought the work mattered. He has been coding, studying, certifying, and shipping for seven years straight with zero institutional support from Finland.

The platform exists because he refused to stop.

That's the background a magazine can verify. Every certification has a credential. Every programme has an enrolment record. Every version of DWS has commits on GitHub. The InvestEU listing is public. The Kela decision is on file. The Finnish labour market's indifference is documented in three decades of applications.

Here's the number that makes this make sense: deep tech startups take on average **11 years** to reach funding. Paarni is in year eight. He's not behind schedule — he's ahead of the curve, with a platform already generating revenue and validated by the European Commission. Most deep tech founders are still in the lab at this stage. Paarni has paying customers and a fleet division bringing in EUR 7,000 MRR — enough to sustain operations without worrying about runway. The Lifetime Fleet isn't a side hustle. It's a strategic asset that lets the founder build on his own timeline, without the desperation capital that kills deep tech companies.

A founder who builds through that doesn't quit at a down round.

## Page 2 — The Problem: 15,000 Rules, Zero Tools

The EU's Fit for 55 package is the most ambitious climate regulation in history. It covers 16 industries and spans regulations arriving on a compressed timeline:

| Regulation | What it demands | Deadline |
|------------|----------------|----------|
| **ETS** | Emissions allowance tracking, trading, surrender | Active now |
| **CBAM** | Carbon calculations on every import at the EU border | Full implementation 2026 |
| **CSRD** | ESRS E1 climate disclosures, audit-ready data | Expanding 2026 |
| **ETS2** | Road transport + buildings emissions trading | 2027 |
| **EPBD** | Embodied carbon reporting, buildings >1,000 sqm | 2028 |
| **ESR** | National target tracking, non-ETS sectors | Active now |
| **ReFuelEU** | Sustainable aviation fuel mandates | Active now |
| **NIS2** | Cybersecurity compliance for critical infrastructure | Active now |
| **EU AI Act** | Automatic logging of all AI system events (Article 12) | 2026 |

What do companies use today? Spreadsheets. Consultants. Manual data entry. Time to produce a compliance report: three to six weeks.

Why existing tools fail:

- General-purpose ESG dashboards don't handle regulation-specific logic
- Consulting firms sell hours, not automation — they don't scale
- Cloud-only platforms can't serve air-gapped factories or mid-ocean vessels
- No existing platform covers all 16 industries under one regulatory stack

The gap is real, the deadline is fixed, and the penalty for non-compliance is market exclusion.

## Page 4 — The Platform

DWS IQ 6 is a multi-agent AI platform that automates climate compliance across 16 EU industries. It operates in four layers:

**Brain — Multi-Agent Orchestration.**
Coordinates real-time decision-making across compliance domains. Built on Google Cloud Run with Groq LPU acceleration. Processes regulatory signals, emissions data, and supply chain inputs simultaneously.

**Memory — Lifetime World Model.**
Climate-specialized knowledge base indexed across 15,000+ EU regulatory documents and 16 industry verticals. Purpose-built for regulatory intelligence — not a general-purpose language model repurposed for compliance.

**Body — Firehorse Product Suite.**
The operational layer: report generation, workflow automation, document management, omnichannel outreach. The Firehorse V2 module for construction uses Physical AI to reconstruct site events and generate audit-ready compliance documentation.

**Hands — Lifetime Fleet.**
Physical execution layer: vehicles, IoT devices, and drones extending the platform into the real world. Already operational and generating revenue through Uber, Taksi Helsinki, and Kela National Care transportation partnerships.

The full infrastructure stack:

- **Edge:** NVIDIA Jetson + AWS IoT Greengrass
- **Core:** Google Cloud Run + Groq API + LlamaStack orchestration
- **Client:** Chromebook Plus with Progressive Web App
- **Data:** Supabase (hot data) + AWS S3 (cold archive)
- **AI models:** Claude (Anthropic) + Google Gemini + Groq, orchestrated via Model Context Protocol (MCP)
- **Monthly infrastructure cost:** EUR 15-50

## Page 1 — The Shrimp Boat Captain

There's a line in Forrest Gump that most people remember for the wrong reason. Bubba doesn't sell Forrest on the economics of shrimping. He doesn't pitch the TAM. He makes Forrest feel like he belongs on that boat. He gives him a role, a purpose, and a deck to stand on.

Risto Paarni has spent 35 years in industrial IT. Factory floors in Finland. ERP migrations across Scandinavia. Cloud architecture for manufacturers still running systems from the 1990s. He holds an M.Sc. in Industrial Management — the degree that sits at the intersection of engineering and business, exactly where compliance problems live.

Two years ago he looked at the EU's Fit for 55 package — ETS, CBAM, CSRD, EPBD, and a dozen more regulations hitting 25+ industries simultaneously — and saw what no one was building: a single platform that treats every regulation as a data contract, maps them into one system, and automates compliance end-to-end.

Every regulation tells you what to measure. CBAM tells you what to report at the border. CSRD tells you how to disclose. They aren't separate problems. They're 15,000 rules in one system. And systems are what engineers build.

So he built it.

He still writes code daily. He manages the GitHub repository. He reviews architecture decisions. When a demo breaks, he fixes it himself. His philosophy: the CEO of an AI company should understand the models, the infrastructure, and the deployment constraints — not just the market opportunity.

That's the origin. Not a pivot from fintech. Not an MBA chasing a trend. An industrial engineer who spent three decades watching factories struggle with compliance — and finally built the tool they needed.

## Page 1B — Intuition, Innovation, and the Joy of Building

Most founders point to a single moment of inspiration. For Risto Paarni, the moment lasted seven years.

In late 2018 he started building the systems that would become DWS — drawn not by a business plan but by a question he could not put down. He spent a year inside the mathematics of machine learning before the rest of the industry understood what transformers would become.

In 2019, OpenAI released GPT-2, and the transformer architecture announced itself as the defining paradigm of a generation. Paarni grasped the shift at once. What the rest of the industry would discover over the following three years, he was already building on. It gave him a third engineering discipline — after industrial management (with process improvement roots stretching back to 2007) and industrial IT, he now carried what he calls _"what is new in AI, Edge, Cloud"_: the intuition to recognise emerging technology and the craft to turn it into working systems.

When ChatGPT launched in November 2022, the world rushed to understand what Paarni had been quietly exploring for four years. He did not need to learn AI. He needed to deploy it. What followed was the most creative stretch of his career: six successive versions of DWS, each a complete rebuild, each closer to what industrial compliance actually demanded.

The European Commission took notice. Three versions of the platform — DWS 3, DWS 4, and DWS IQ 6 — earned listings on the InvestEU Portal (INPO-20254383). Not a single fortunate submission. Three consecutive iterations, validated at the Commission level. Progressive recognition of work that kept getting better.

**This is how a Captain is made.**

Not through adversity alone — through intuition, innovation, and the plain joy of doing the work. Then the industry started coming to him. NVIDIA opened the door to its hardware programme. The evidence is public. More than 500 pull requests on GitHub — a living codebase, not a slide deck. Three InvestEU listings verifiable on the European Commission's portal.

The DWS IQ platform and the onelifetime.world community exist for a single reason: to fight the climate crisis with the best available technology in each of 25+ industries.

When a business angel asks, "Why this founder?" — the answer is the energy behind the work. Seven years of learning, building, and shipping because the problem matters and the craft is worth mastering. A platform validated three times by the European Commission. Revenue from day one through the Fleet division. And a Captain's intuition: Paarni sees where the technology is heading, and he takes genuine pleasure in arriving there first.

## Page 2 — The Problem: 15,000 Rules, Zero Tools

The EU's Fit for 55 package is the most ambitious climate regulation in history. It covers 25+ industries and spans regulations arriving on a compressed timeline:

| Regulation    | What it demands                                        | Deadline                 |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| **ETS**       | Emissions allowance tracking, trading, surrender       | Active now               |
| **CBAM**      | Carbon calculations on every import at the EU border   | Full implementation 2026 |
| **CSRD**      | ESRS E1 climate disclosures, audit-ready data          | Expanding 2026           |
| **ETS2**      | Road transport + buildings emissions trading           | 2027                     |
| **EPBD**      | Embodied carbon reporting, buildings >1,000 sqm        | 2028                     |
| **ESR**       | National target tracking, non-ETS sectors              | Active now               |
| **ReFuelEU**  | Sustainable aviation fuel mandates                     | Active now               |
| **NIS2**      | Cybersecurity compliance for critical infrastructure   | Active now               |
| **EU AI Act** | Automatic logging of all AI system events (Article 12) | 2026                     |

What do companies use today? Spreadsheets. Consultants. Manual data entry. Time to produce a compliance report: three to six weeks.

Why existing tools fail:

- General-purpose ESG dashboards don't handle regulation-specific logic
- Consulting firms sell hours, not automation — they don't scale
- Cloud-only platforms can't serve air-gapped factories or mid-ocean vessels
- No existing platform covers all 25+ industries under one regulatory stack

The gap is real, the deadline is fixed, and the penalty for non-compliance is market exclusion.

## Page 4 — The Platform

DWS IQ 6 is a multi-agent AI platform that automates climate compliance across **16 captain hats covering 25+ EU industries**. The short version: DWS IQ 6 is the orchestration platform. The Lifetime World Model is the proprietary intelligence layer. Together they turn commodity AI into a deep tech compliance engine that runs where cloud can't reach.

It operates in four layers:

**Brain — Multi-Agent Orchestration.**
Coordinates real-time decision-making across compliance domains. Built on Google Cloud Run with Groq LPU acceleration. Processes regulatory signals, emissions data, and supply chain inputs simultaneously.

**Memory — Lifetime World Model.**
Climate-specialized knowledge base indexed across 15,000+ EU regulatory documents and 16 industry verticals. Purpose-built for regulatory intelligence — not a general-purpose language model repurposed for compliance.

**Body — Firehorse Product Suite.**
The operational layer: report generation, workflow automation, document management, omnichannel outreach. The Firehorse V2 module for construction uses Physical AI to reconstruct site events and generate audit-ready compliance documentation.

**Hands — Lifetime Fleet.**
Physical execution layer: vehicles, IoT devices, and drones extending the platform into the real world. Already operational and generating revenue through Uber, Taksi Helsinki, and Kela National Care transportation partnerships.

The full infrastructure stack:

- **Edge:** NVIDIA Jetson + AWS IoT Greengrass
- **Core:** Google Cloud Run + Groq API + LlamaStack orchestration
- **Client:** Chromebook Plus with Progressive Web App
- **Data:** Supabase (hot data) + AWS S3 (cold archive)
- **AI models:** Claude (Anthropic) + Google Gemini + Groq, orchestrated via Model Context Protocol (MCP)
- **Monthly infrastructure cost:** EUR 15-50

## Page 4B — The Forward Deployed Founder

There is a role the AI industry rediscovered in 2026. Palantir invented it twenty years ago and called its practitioners "Deltas." OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are now racing to hire them. The title is **Forward Deployed Engineer** — the engineer who leaves the office, embeds in the customer's environment, and makes the software actually work in production.

The reason the role matters is a single statistic. MIT found that **95% of enterprise AI pilots produce no measurable return.** The model performs in the demo, then dies when it meets the customer's messy data, undocumented workflows, and the unglamorous reality of their ERP, data warehouse, identity provider, and approval chain. The demo is the easy 5%. The last mile is where deals are won or lost.

Risto Paarni has worked this way his entire career — long before the role had a fashionable name. Thirty-five years of ERP migrations meant sitting on Finnish factory floors, inside the customer's systems, integrating against data that never matched the brochure. The DWS motion is the same: discovery with the people doing the work, a paid pilot proven on the customer's own data, then production rollout owned after launch. When a demo breaks, he fixes it himself.

The BIM pipeline on Page 3B is the proof. Real NCC project data, real EN 15978 lifecycle stages, real Finnish emission factors — a complete production pipeline built in a five-hour session on a Saturday morning. That is not a founder reviewing pull requests from a distance. That is a forward deployed engineer closing the last mile himself.

Two things make this model defensible for DWS specifically:

- **Sovereignty as reach.** A US-cloud FDE is legally barred from the air-gapped factory, the maritime vessel, the critical-infrastructure account. DWS deploys forward into exactly those rooms — EU data residency, on-device inference, KYA identity binding — where the data cannot leave and the cloud cannot follow.
- **One captain per industry.** The 16 captain hats are the forward-deployed model at portfolio scale: an embedded compliance engineer who knows building sites, or mid-ocean shipping, or steel — not a generalist demoing a dashboard.

The CEO who still writes code is not a quirk of this company. It is the go-to-market strategy. The founder is the first Forward Deployed Engineer, and the platform is built so the next fifteen can follow him into the field.

# HIC Learning Outcome — 2026-07-29

## 1. What the session was, and what it became

It started as a two-line request: confirm the blog lives only at
`onelifetime.world/blog/`, and find the missing LSJ link in the dws10.com menu.

Both were real. The blog is single-homed, and the link genuinely did not exist — not
in the dws10.com nav, and not in onelifetime.world's own nav either, so the blog's
own site did not link to it from the menu.

The HIC then asked for a content audit of every blog text. That surfaced a class of
defect nobody had been looking for, and the HIC caught a second one from memory:
**the price list had been claiming five languages, in its own document header and in
the 2026-07-26 field note, while only the English page existed.**

**The arc:** missing menu link → single-home confirmed → audit 115 posts → find 22
Finnish pages with the orthography destroyed → find six false factual claims → HIC
catches the five-language price-list claim → build the four missing pages → correct
the field note that carried the false claim rather than editing it away.

## 3. What was actually wrong

### The Finnish batch (the real find)

`posts/fi/day-001` … `day-021` plus `markkinasignaali-visman-myynti-2026` — 22 public
pages — were published with **every ä and ö flattened to a and o**: `tekoäly` →
`tekoaly`, `järjestelmä` → `jarjestelma`, `päättelyyn` → `paattelyyn`. Titles, `<title>`
tags, meta descriptions, body text. In the company's home-market language, on the
posts that carry the 800-day campaign.

Every other Finnish post in the repo is clean, which is what makes this diagnosable:
it is not house style, it is one generator run in March–April 2026 that lost UTF-8.

The repair is `scripts/fix-fi-diacritics.mjs` — stem rules, vowel-harmony rules and
whole-word forms, applied to text nodes and prose attributes only so slugs and
canonical URLs keep the ASCII form they are already published under. The part worth
keeping is `--check`: **every rule is validated against corpora that are already
spelled correctly** (the clean Finnish posts and all the English posts). A rule that
fires there would corrupt correct text, and the script reports it as unsafe and exits
non-zero. That is what made a 300-rule blind rewrite safe to run.

Diacritic density went from ~0.2 per 100 words to a mean of 29, against a 32–38
reference from the clean Finnish posts.

The Article 12 one is the one that could have cost money: the buyer's note turned it
into a procurement question — *"can you show me the audit trail from the last 7
years?"* — inviting a customer to test us against a requirement that does not exist.

### The price list

Claimed five languages in its own `Language:` header since v1.21 and in the
2026-07-26 field note. Only English existed. Now generated by
`scripts/generate-price-list-i18n.mjs`, with prices still fetched from
`/config/products.json` in every language — nothing to drift.

## 4. What compounds

**The lint was clean the whole time.** `scripts/lint-truth-claims.mjs` reported 0
fail / 0 warn across all 115 posts, before and after. It never saw the "18+ AI agents"
drift because its deprecated-phrase list contains only `18+ Industry Agents`. A
word-list lint catches the phrasing someone thought of, not the claim. Every finding
in this session came from reconciling against the canonical file, not from the gate.

**Nobody reads the home-market language.** 22 broken Finnish pages sat public for four
months. The English posts they were translated from were fine, so every English-first
review passed.

**A false claim in a field note outlives the thing it described.** The five-language
claim was written down, then cited, and would have kept being cited. It is corrected
in place, in a quote block, with the date — not edited away.

## 6. The Article 14 question for the HIC

**The Finnish day-series posts are half the length of their English originals — ~475
words against ~980. The orthography is now fixed, but they are still summaries
published as translations. Do we (a) leave them as short-form Finnish and label them
as summaries, (b) rewrite the 21 posts to full parity with the English, or (c) retire
the Finnish day-series and point `fi` readers at the English originals?**

Each is defensible. (a) is free and honest. (b) is roughly 10,000 words of Finnish and
makes the 800-day campaign whole in both languages. (c) admits the series was never
really bilingual. This is a positioning call about the Finnish market, not an
engineering one — which is why it is yours.

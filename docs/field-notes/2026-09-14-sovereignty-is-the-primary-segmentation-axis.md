## 3. Why *primary* holds

**Official procurement already segments on sovereignty.** The European Commission's Cloud Sovereignty Framework (presented October 2025 for Cloud III DPS; implementation guidance published after the tender) scores providers on eight Sovereignty Objectives (SOV-1…8) and five Sovereignty Effectiveness Assurance Levels (SEAL-0 *No Sovereignty* through SEAL-4 *Full Digital Sovereignty*). SOV-2 Legal & Jurisdictional names extraterritorial reach (US CLOUD Act, Chinese Cybersecurity Law) as a scored criterion. That is a public-sector segmentation engine, not a slogan.

DWS-tier mapping (indicative, not a SEAL self-claim). **C = Connect = non-EU sales (J2 + J3).**

## 4. Why *not the only one* is the load-bearing fence

Classic marketing (Porter / Kotler) already segments on geography, industry, size, behaviour, and benefit. Sovereignty does not erase those bases. It became the **first** cut for EU-regulated AI and critical workloads.

Evidence that the market is **not** binary:

- Deloitte: 58% primarily local vendors means 42% still mix. Origin is a criterion, not a monopoly.
- Gartner “geopatriation”: ~20% of current workloads estimated to shift from global to local providers — not 100%.
- DACH commentary on DIHK Digitalisierungsumfrage 2026 (secondary: Remote Native citing DIHK, ~5,000 firms): 53% distrust non-European AI providers **and** 78% already use generative AI. The predicted architecture is **workload-based segmentation** — sensitive on sovereign substrate, the rest on hyperscalers. That is *primary axis, not the only one* in operational form.
- DWS itself sells Connect into UAE / Singapore / USA (AZ, TX). If sovereignty were the only axis, Connect would not exist.

Formerly used on the coaching pages, now deprecated as a headline: “The market splits on one axis” / “Markkina jakautuu yhdellä akselilla.”

## 5. Academic warrant — sovereignty *is* boundary-drawing

Pohle & Thiel (*Internet Policy Review* 2020; later Policy & Internet 2024) treat digital sovereignty as a **discursive and policy practice of drawing or reinforcing jurisdictional boundaries** around infrastructure, data flows, and digital markets — not as a legal status you stamp on an EU region. They distinguish (at least) state-autonomy claims from economic-self-determination claims (nurture local competitors against US/CN dominance). Both are segmentation: who is inside the perimeter, who is not.

That is the process reading of the Finnish partitive *suvereeniteetti on segmentointia*: sovereignty is the act of cutting. Mueller and others warn that the same cut can fragment the open internet; DWS does not need that debate to use the cut as a **product and buying axis**. The academic point that matters here is narrower: the literature already describes sovereignty as segmentation-by-boundary, which is why a vendor that treats it as a checkbox cannot price against Aegis / Infra / Connect.

## 8. SEAL-0…4 — what the Commission actually built

SEAL = **Sovereignty Effectiveness Assurance Level**. Source of the names below: Cloud Sovereignty Framework **v1.2.1, October 2025**, DG DIGIT, used as the Cloud III DPS sovereignty gate (€180M, awarded 17.04.2026). Implementation guidance (post-tender) is a derivative explainer, not a second scale.

**SEAL is a gate, not a rank.** Each of eight Sovereignty Objectives (SOV-1…8) receives a SEAL. A tender that misses the stated minimum on **any** objective is rejected. The offer’s overall SEAL is the **lowest** SEAL among the objectives (weakest link). A separate **Sovereignty Score** (weighted: supply chain 20%, strategic/operational/technology 15%, legal/data-AI/security 10%, environment 5%) ranks only the offers that passed the gate.

| SEAL | Official name (v1.2.1) | Control | Cloud III |
| ---- | ---------------------- | ------- | --------- |
| 0 | No Sovereignty | Exclusive non-EU control, non-EU law | Fail |
| 1 | Jurisdictional Sovereignty | EU law on paper, exclusive non-EU control | Fail |
| 2 | Data Sovereignty | EU law enforceable; **indirect** non-EU control; material non-EU deps remain | **Minimum**. Customer needs no extra tech to protect data. Proximus/S3NS (Thales+Google) landed here. |
| 3 | Digital Resilience | Meaningful EU influence; **marginal** non-EU control; cannot be cut by a non-EU supplier | Post+OVH+CleverCloud, STACKIT, Scaleway |
| 4 | Full Digital Sovereignty | Complete EU control; no critical non-EU deps; chips-to-software | **No awarded provider.** Commission lessons-learnt: not relevant today because of chip/hardware dependence. |

**Name fork on SEAL-3 (Rule 12).** v1.2.1 and the 17.04.2026 award news say **Digital Resilience**. The implementation guidance says **Technological Sovereignty**. The 01.06.2026 explainer says **technological autonomy**. Same rung, three labels — do not invent a sixth level.

**DWS implication.** This row’s Aegis ≈ SEAL-3 mapping is **indicative, not a certificate** — DWS was not in Cloud III. SEAL-4 is structurally closed while inference sits on NVIDIA GN100/DGX (the same chip-dependence the Commission named). US-parent “EU region” is SEAL-1, not SEAL-2. Do not add a fake SEAL badge to `/sovereignty-assess` until the 43–48-question evidence pack exists. Catalogue and tier tables now name the indicative nearest SEAL on Aegis / Sovereign Infra / Connect SKUs (`docs/PRODUCT_TIERS.md`, `dws10.com/config/products.json`).

Primary URLs: Framework v1.2.1 via [achatpublic.info](https://www.achatpublic.info/sites/default/files/document/documents/cloud-sovereignty-framework.pdf); award [17.04.2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/commission-advances-cloud-sovereignty-through-strategic-procurement-2026-04-17_en); explainer [01.06.2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/sovereign-cloud-framework-explained-2026-06-01_en); [implementation guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/2ad80a48-166f-4c77-a513-80c53ca2a128_en).

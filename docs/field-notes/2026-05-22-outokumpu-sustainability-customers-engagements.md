## 3. The product / proof points Outokumpu is selling

1. **Circle Green®** — Outokumpu's lowest-carbon stainless variant, launched late 2022. Cradle-to-gate footprint claimed at <0.5 tCO₂e/t; uses ~100 % renewable electricity at Tornio, biocoke for the EAF reduction step, and ~94 % scrap. EPDs are third-party verified.
2. **Tornio Works** — flagship asset. ~1.6 Mt annual stainless capacity, fully integrated (ferrochrome smelter + EAF + AOD + hot strip mill + cold mill + finishing) on one site. The energy footprint is uniquely low because it sits on the Finnish grid and runs EAF, not BF-BOF.
3. **Kemi mine** — Europe's only chromium mine; chromium is on the EU Critical Raw Materials Act strategic list. CRMA-aligned narrative: domestic, traceable, EU-sovereign chromium supply for the EU's stainless industry.
4. **Calvert (Alabama)** — same low-carbon EAF playbook on the US grid; positioned for Inflation Reduction Act / 45X eligibility.
5. **Permascand / hydrogen** — Outokumpu has done green-hydrogen pilots ("H2Future" cluster work at Tornio) — directionally aligned with HYBRIT-style narratives, but Outokumpu is already EAF, so the hydrogen story is narrower (reduction agent for the chromium ferroalloy step, not a primary-iron play).

## 4. Regulatory context — why this campaign exists in 2026

1. **CBAM is live as of 01.01.2026.** Stainless flat products (CN codes 7218–7222, 7223, 7225–7229) are in CBAM scope. Outokumpu is **CBAM-advantaged**: an EU producer with ETS coverage is automatically inside the CBAM perimeter, while non-EU competitors (Tsingshan / Baosteel / Jindal / Acerinox-US) must surrender CBAM certificates pegged to the ETS price. The customer-engagement campaign is, partly, demand-side recruitment — "lock in EU stainless before CBAM bites your imports."
2. **CSRD second wave** — Outokumpu is a long-time mandatory reporter (CSRD wave 1, fiscal 2024 → reported 2025). Their Scope 1+2+3 disclosure depth is one of the deepest in EU heavy industry; this campaign is partly to attract CSRD-mandated buyers who need their suppliers' verified numbers.
3. **EU Taxonomy** — Outokumpu's revenue alignment with the EU Taxonomy Climate Delegated Act is non-trivial for stainless (the activity codes for steel are tight on emission intensity thresholds). Outokumpu argues they meet the technical screening criteria for "manufacture of iron and steel" under §3.9 of the Delegated Act on a substantial share of revenue.
4. **Critical Raw Materials Act** — Kemi mine = chromium = Annex II strategic raw material. Outokumpu is the EU's flag-carrier on this row of the CRMA table.
5. **EU ETS Phase 4 free-allocation phase-out** — runs in lockstep with CBAM ramp; Outokumpu wants buyers to internalize that imported stainless's carbon cost is rising every year through 2034.

## 5. Why this matters for DWS IQ / Lifetime Oy

1. **Material Oracle calibration anchor.** Outokumpu's published Scope 1+2 number (~1.6 tCO₂e/t group, <0.5 tCO₂e/t Circle Green) is the EU-best-case datapoint our `material-oracle` agent must reproduce when a user inputs "stainless 304, EU-produced." If our default EF for EU stainless is materially above 1.6 t/t, the agent is overestimating embodied carbon and under-rewarding EU producers — directly contrary to the CBAM-aligned market.
2. **Customer prospect — direct.** Outokumpu is a textbook DWS IQ customer profile: CSRD-mandatory, CBAM-affected, CRMA-strategic, Espoo-HQ'd, German/Swedish/UK/US plants subject to multiple regulatory regimes. Their internal carbon-accounting + compliance load is exactly the workflow our `compliance-check`, `regulatory-radar`, `carbon-analysis`, and `emissions-data-analyst` agents are built for. The named contacts to approach are typically the Director Sustainability / Head of Climate (the role currently publicly profiled on outokumpu.com/sustainability) — and through Aalto/TKK alumni networks in Espoo.
3. **Customer prospect — indirect, through their value chain.** Every customer logo in the "Customers-animation-1" reel is itself a CSRD reporter who needs verified Scope 3 numbers from Outokumpu. That dependency is a DWS IQ opportunity — we can sit between Outokumpu's supplier data and the customer's CSRD line item. Fiskars and Stora Enso are particularly tractable (Finnish, listed, CSRD-mandatory, DWS IQ already touches their adjacent sectors).
4. **Competitive intelligence on the stainless EPD narrative.** Circle Green's <0.5 tCO₂e/t is the public ceiling-buster claim. Any competing low-carbon stainless story (Acerinox, Aperam, Acerinox Columbus, ThyssenKrupp Hohenlimburg) will be benchmarked against this number. DWS IQ's competitor-surveillance agent should track Aperam's "infinite" recycled-content positioning vs Outokumpu's recycled-content + renewable-electricity positioning — they are not identical claims.
5. **Sovereignty / CRMA narrative reinforcement.** Outokumpu + Kemi is the textbook EU sovereignty case for chromium. The `sovereignty-assess` skill should treat Outokumpu/Kemi as a positive sovereignty anchor for any EU industrial customer whose stainless supply chain is asked about under CRMA.
6. **Local DSO and grid overlap.** Outokumpu HQ in Espoo (Salmisaarenranta 11) is in Helen Sähköverkko, not Caruna Espoo, footprint — so the Caruna 2026 plan does **not** apply to their Espoo HQ. But Tornio Works' grid connection (Tornion Energia / Fingrid trunk at the Petäjäskoski–Keminmaa corridor) is **the** Finnish industrial baseload story; any DWS IQ analysis of green-hydrogen siting or large industrial electrification has to take Tornio's ~1.6 TWh/yr footprint as a fixed planning constraint.

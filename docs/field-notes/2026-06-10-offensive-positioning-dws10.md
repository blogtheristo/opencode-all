## The Shift in One Line

| Frame | Pitch | Buyer | Budget type |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----------- |
| **Defensive (clean)** | "Buy us so you don't get fined." | Risk / Legal / Compliance | Cost-center / insurance |
| **Offensive (sharp)** | "Buy us so you win tenders faster, ship compliant artifacts sooner, and turn regulatory readiness into a commercial edge." | Commercial / Sales / CEO | Growth / revenue |

Fines-avoidance gets us through the door of the risk, finance, and legal offices —
no C-suite wants the 4% EU AI Act / CSRD turnover headline. But that is a
cost-center conversation: boring, insurance, easy to defer. The offensive frame
moves the same product into a **growth budget**, which is faster to unlock and
larger.

This is consistent with the canonical homepage sentence (`DWS_BRAND_STANDARD.md`
§22): *"compliance can freeze or release money."* The offensive frame is just the
"release money" half said out loud.

## Three Promises (reframed to keep teeth, drop the recklessness)

The raw "dirty" framing tested too hot for exec rooms — "starve your competitors
out of the market" makes the buyer sound like a procurement bully and invites
antitrust/PR blowback. Each promise below keeps the commercial edge but is
phrased so it survives a legal review and a CEO repeating it on stage.

### 1. Operational Velocity — "Outrun the market, don't just avoid the fire"

- **Claim:** While rivals spend six months and a standing committee compiling
  data, a DWS10 customer is artifact-ready in a fraction of the time.
- **Why it's defensible:** maps to the canonical "removing compliance friction is
  the entry point" and the *System of Action operator* frame — *carries regulated
  work to completion with a full audit trail*. The "10,000 years of regulatory
  edge cases simulated before your first audit" pitch line is the proof point.
- **Guardrail:** state speed as **"days, not quarters"** rather than a hard "48
  hours" SLA unless Product signs off on a contractual number. An unmet hero
  number is a refund conversation.

### 2. Tender / Procurement Edge — (was "Procurement Weaponization")

- **Claim:** EU enterprise and public-sector buyers are increasingly mandated to
  drop suppliers who can't produce ironclad compliance artifacts (green-washing
  rules, sovereign-data guarantees). A DWS10 customer walks in *already* holding
  those credentials and qualifies for tenders rivals are excluded from.
- **Reframe:** sell the customer's **upside** ("you qualify, you win the tender
  sooner"), not the rival's **downfall** ("we starve them out"). Same mechanic,
  no bully optics.
- **Why it's defensible:** maps to "compliance can freeze or release money" — for
  the buyer's *own* sales motion. This is the Boardy buyer-line already in §22:
  *"helps [company] sell more by removing compliance friction before the sale."*

### 3. Deterministic Accountability — (was "Total Accountability Arbitrage")

- **Claim:** Software generates the math, locks the ledger, and delivers a
  deterministic, auditable artifact — replacing human error, internal politics,
  and individual career risk with a hard-coded, traceable record.
- **DO NOT promise "shift 100% of execution risk off your balance sheet."** That
  claim is not deliverable: software vendors cap liability exactly like advisory
  firms do, and a no-cap guarantee dies in our *own* legal review. Promise what
  is real and still strong: **a defensible audit trail and deterministic output
  that withstands scrutiny** — Article 12 logging, KYA-governed, reproducible.

## Two governance catches (must-fix before any of this ships)

1. **"Zero human resource cost" contradicts our own footer.** Every page carries
   the standing Ethics Principle band (`DWS_BRAND_STANDARD.md` §6, v1.3): *"DWS
   products augment people, never replacing junior talent or the
   human-in-command."* The master tagline is *"We amplify human intelligence."*
   So the pitch is **"frees your experts from the grunt work"** / **"no army of
   consultants"** — never **"replaces your people."** Off-message and
   self-contradicting otherwise.
2. **Competitors implicit, never named.** Brand rule (§0): reference rivals as
   *"Unlike US-hosted platforms…"* — never by name on public surfaces. Keeps the
   teeth, drops the libel/antitrust surface.

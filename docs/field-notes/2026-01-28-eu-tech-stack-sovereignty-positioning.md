## Strategic Analysis: The Transatlantic Tension Trade-off

### My Take (Requested Assessment)

The push for a fully independent "European tech stack" presents a **genuine strategic dilemma**:

**Benefits of EU Sovereignty Push:**
- Reduces extraterritorial legal exposure (CLOUD Act)
- Accelerates EU cloud provider innovation
- Creates protected market for EU tech companies
- Aligns with regulatory trajectory (AI Act, DMA, GAIA-X)

**Risks of Aggressive Decoupling:**
- Higher costs (EU providers ~15-30% more expensive at scale)
- Capability gaps in frontier AI (LLMs, chips)
- Trade retaliation cycle escalation
- Innovation fragmentation vs. global collaboration

**Realistic Outlook (2026-2030):**
- Full independence unlikely and perhaps undesirable
- **Hybrid sovereignty** more probable: Critical systems on EU infrastructure, non-sensitive workloads on global hyperscalers
- EU dependency likely to decline from ~80% to ~50-60% by 2030 (more conservative than ECFR's 40% target)

**DWS6 Strategic Implication:** Position for the **hybrid sovereignty** market—enterprises that need EU compliance for regulated workloads but maintain flexibility. Don't require customers to go "fully European," but make EU-sovereign the default, compliant choice.

## The Quote

Palantir CEO Alex Karp explains why people think we're in an AI bubble and how to actually create value with AI:

> **"If you just buy LLMs off the shelf and try to do any of this, it won't work."**
>
> **"It's not precise enough. You can't do underwriting. You can't do these things that are regulated."**
>
> **"People have tried things that just can never work. You buy a LLM, put it on your stack, and wonder why it's not working."**
>
> **"What you're going to see, especially in America, is people trying to do something like Ontology by hand."**
>
> **"Once you build a software layer to orchestrate and manage the LLMs in a language your enterprise understands, you actually can create value."**
>
> **"There's a lot of discussion on if we're in an AI bubble. What is the meaning of this bubble? If anything, we're just in a lag. There's a lot of AI, some of it works."**
>
> **"Go back to the battlefield context: everybody in the world assumed this would not work. But now it does work. Now the question is, 'How can I get it to work for my country?'"**
>
> **"Palantir barely has a sales force. In fact, it seems to be getting smaller and smaller every time I go see them."**

---

## Core Themes

### 1. The "Bubble" vs. The "Lag"

Karp argues we aren't in a bubble, but rather a **functional lag**.

**The Issue:**

- Companies buy LLMs off the shelf
- Expect them to work for regulated or precise tasks (underwriting, compliance)
- Face failure because basic LLMs lack precision and enterprise-specific "language"

**The Result:** Failure and disillusionment

### 2. The Solution: The "Software Layer" (Ontology)

Karp emphasizes that LLMs alone are insufficient. To create value, an enterprise needs:

- **Orchestration:** A software layer to manage the models
- **Ontology:** A way to translate LLM outputs into the specific "language" and logic of the business
- **Manual Pitfall:** Many companies are trying to build this "Ontology" by hand, which is inefficient

### 3. Proof of Concept: The Battlefield

- The transition from skepticism ("it would not work") to adoption ("how can I get it for my country?") happened first on the battlefield
- This serves as the blueprint for enterprise adoption
- Move from theoretical "bubble" talk to practical, mission-critical application

### 4. Interesting Internal Metric: Shrinking Sales Force

- Palantir's sales force is reportedly **shrinking**
- This suggests the product is "pulling" itself into the market via results
- NOT being "pushed" by traditional sales tactics

## Why This Matters for DWS

### Direct Application to DWS7

Karp's insights validate our approach:

1. **We are NOT building generic AI tools**
   - DWS-IQ6/7 must have enterprise-specific ontology
   - Can't rely on off-the-shelf LLMs for regulated tasks

2. **We MUST build the orchestration layer**
   - Route queries through validated artifacts first
   - Fall back to LLMs only with enterprise context
   - Never use raw LLM output for compliance-critical decisions

3. **The "Battlefield" is our customers**
   - Field notes from customer interactions are our training data
   - Production incidents are learning opportunities
   - Each edge case improves the world model

4. **Sales should be results-driven**
   - If we build the right ontology and orchestration
   - Customers will "pull" the product based on demonstrated value
   - Not pushy sales tactics

**Palantir's Approach:** Build custom ontology for each customer (expensive, time-consuming)

**DWS Opportunity:**

- Build **reusable ontologies** for specific industries (construction, finance, manufacturing)
- Faster deployment, lower cost
- Still enterprise-specific, but templatized

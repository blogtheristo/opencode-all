# Letter of Intent — working draft v0.1

**Between:** KTH Royal Institute of Technology / TECoSA — Trustworthy Edge Computing Systems and Applications, and Lifetime Oy (Business ID 0772407-9), Espoo, Finland.

> **Note to the reader.** This is a **v0.1 working draft circulated for comment, not a proposal to sign.** Three items — the **applicant entity**, the **coordinating party**, and the **co-funding model** — are deliberately left as `[TO BE AGREED]`. They depend on how TECoSA's competence-centre model actually works, and we would rather have KTH's answer than our assumption. Everything else is drafted so it can be edited rather than rebuilt.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Parties intend to explore a research collaboration on **assurance for sovereign edge AI systems**: how an autonomous decision taken at the edge can be shown, on evidence, to be safe — and what that assurance costs in system performance.

The collaboration is envisaged within **Vinnova's competence-centre model**, with TECoSA as the research environment and Lifetime as an industrial partner contributing a regulated operating environment.

## 2. Background

**KTH / TECoSA** is a Vinnova competence centre working on methods, tools and theory for safe, secure and predictable systems relying on edge computing.

**Lifetime Oy** builds DWS IQ, a governed outcome layer for AI agents in EU-regulated industries. Its KYA engine (identity binding, session trust scoring, containment, EU AI Act Article 12 reasoning-lineage capture) is at TRL 5 completed, entering TRL 6. Lifetime also operates a **licensed passenger-transport business** under Traficom operator licence 00010089 (valid to 24 October 2034) — a regulated, insured, operating edge environment rather than a demonstrator.

## 3. Intended scope of collaboration

1. **Assurance cases for sovereign edge AI** — what claim structure is appropriate; what the KYA evidence is and is not sufficient to support; how such an argument is validated rather than asserted.
2. **Safety–performance trade-offs** — what containment, trust scoring and lineage capture cost in latency, throughput and availability at the edge, and where a defensible operating point lies.
3. **Validation methodology** applied to an industrial use case.

Trustworthiness properties in and out of scope to be agreed; the Parties intend to state exclusions explicitly rather than rely on a broad inclusion.

## 4. Intended contributions

**KTH / TECoSA:** research leadership on assurance argumentation and validation methodology; supervision of any researcher engaged under §6; academic quality assurance.

**Lifetime:** the industrial use case; the DWS IQ platform and KYA engine as the system under study; and **scoped access to a licensed operating environment**, subject to §7.

The Parties record that Lifetime is a small company. Its contribution is the operating environment and the platform, not headcount.

## 5. Funding route, applicant entity and co-funding

`[TO BE AGREED]` — **this section is intentionally unfilled.**

The Parties will agree:

- **(a) the funding route** — TECoSA partnership, a joint call, or a separate application;
- **(b) the coordinating party**;
- **(c) the applicant and funded entity**, noting that Lifetime Oy is a Finnish company and that a Swedish construction (subsidiary, `filial`, or participation alongside a Swedish funded party) may be required. **Lifetime makes no claim to be eligible today; no Swedish entity is currently registered.** A Swedish entity can be established on an accelerated schedule if the track becomes concrete;
- **(d) the form and valuation of Lifetime's co-funding contribution** — cash, engineering time, or scoped in-kind access to the licensed operating environment and platform — and what evidence or valuation method would be expected.

## 6. Researcher pathway

`[TO BE AGREED]` — one of: an **industrial doctoral student** employed by the Lifetime entity and enrolled at KTH; a **licentiate** route; or a **postdoc** hosted at KTH and part-funded by the project. Supervision, admission requirements and start date to follow from the choice.

## 7. Instrumentation, data access and data protection

No commitment on data access is made by this Letter. **Instrumentation and data access will be defined separately and subsequently**, within safety, GDPR and operator-licence-holder boundaries. Operational fleet data involves personal data of passengers and drivers; the Parties will define GDPR roles (controller / processor), what is anonymised or aggregated before leaving the operational system, and what does not leave it at all. Limits are set before instrumentation begins, not negotiated afterwards.

## 8. Intellectual property

Background IP remains with its owner. **DWS IQ, the KYA engine and DWS IQ Aegis are Lifetime background IP.** Foreground IP to be allocated per category — research results, software, data — in the definitive agreement.

## 9. Publication

KTH shall be free to publish. Lifetime shall have a review period of **[30] days** before submission to identify confidential material and to file where appropriate. Publication shall not be unreasonably withheld or delayed.

## 10. Confidentiality

Each Party shall keep confidential the other's non-public information disclosed under this Letter and use it only for the purposes described here.

## 11. Status of this Letter

This Letter expresses intent and **commits no funds**. It is **non-binding except as to §7 (data protection), §9 (publication review), §10 (confidentiality), and any IP provisions expressly agreed**, which bind on signature.

It is superseded by a consortium agreement or a Vinnova project agreement, and lapses on `[date]` if no such agreement is concluded.

## 12. Next steps

1. KTH comment on §5 (route, coordinator, entity, co-funding) and §6 (researcher pathway).
2. Parties agree the scope exclusions under §3.
3. Definitive agreement drafted against a specific call deadline.

> Purpose: give the accountant a structured starting point for how Lifetime Oy (Finland) and
> Lifetime AB (Sweden) transact, so the arrangement is arm's-length and clean from day one.
> **This is not a contract and not advice.** The accountant/tax adviser formalises terms,

## 0. Hard rule (non-negotiable, top of the page)

**No Lifetime Oy debt into the AB. No negative-equity exposure into the AB.**

- Lifetime Oy does **not** lend to Lifetime AB and does **not** guarantee AB liabilities.
- The AB funds itself from **share capital (25 000 SEK) + Vinnova grant + its own revenue**.
- Intercompany services are **invoiced and settled in cash**, not accrued as a growing
  intercompany payable that could push the AB toward negative equity.
- If the AB cannot pay for a service, the service is **not rendered / scaled back** — it is
  **not** converted into a loan from Oy.
- Watch the Swedish **kontrollbalansräkning** trigger: if AB equity falls below half the
  registered share capital, ABL 25 kap. duties bite. The structure must keep AB equity safe.

## 2. Direction of services — SELECTED: Option 1 (Stockholm/Vinnova lane)

**Option 1 (SELECTED) — Oy licenses IP/platform to AB; AB does Sweden-establishment work.**

- Oy → AB: software licence + platform/dev support (Oy owns the DWS IP).
- AB → Oy: Sweden-market development / local delivery (if any) billed back.
- Fits the Vinnova lane: the AB performs **real Stockholm-establishment work** (EV/Uber ops,
  KTH/TECoSA, campus) with **project costs borne by the AB's Swedish driftställe** — the
  cost-bearing Vinnova requires. IP ownership stays cleanly with Oy.
- Confirm with the accountant: the licence fee Oy charges the AB must **not** be sized so it
  starves the AB's equity or converts into an intercompany debt (see hard rule, §0).

**Option 2 (not selected) — AB as pure market-entry/reseller vehicle.** Simpler, but a thin
reseller weakens the driftställe/cost-bearing story Vinnova assesses. Rejected for this lane.

> Recommendation to raise with the accountant: **Option 1**, because Vinnova needs real
> project work and cost-bearing at the Swedish establishment, and IP ownership stays cleanly
> with Oy.

## 3. IP ownership

- **DWS IP stays with Lifetime Oy.** The AB receives a **licence** (scope, territory, term to
  be set). No IP is transferred to the AB. Any AB-created improvements: assign-back or
  joint-ownership terms to be defined.

## 4. Pricing (arm's length)

- Services priced at **arm's length** (both FI and SE follow OECD transfer-pricing principles).
- Typical method for intra-group services: **cost-plus** (mark-up on cost base) — accountant
  to set the mark-up and cost base.
- Licence fee: royalty or fixed-fee basis — accountant to choose and document.

## 5. Invoicing, currency, VAT

- Invoicing cadence: [monthly/quarterly]; currency [EUR or SEK — decide].
- **B2B cross-border EU services → VAT reverse charge** (recipient accounts for VAT); confirm
  registrations and that both entities have valid VAT numbers.
- Settlement in cash within [30] days — reinforces the "no accruing intercompany debt" rule.

## 6. Transfer-pricing documentation

- Keep contemporaneous documentation on both sides (FI + SE). Even for small volumes, a short
  intercompany agreement + pricing memo protects both companies in an audit.

## 7. Governance / conflicts

- Risto is on both sides (Oy CEO; AB director). Document related-party terms and ensure
  arm's-length pricing to avoid förtäckt värdeöverföring (disguised distribution) issues under
  ABL, and to keep the Vinnova cost-bearing clean.

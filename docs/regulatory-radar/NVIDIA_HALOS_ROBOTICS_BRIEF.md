# Regulatory Radar Brief — NVIDIA Halos for Robotics vs EU Machinery Regulation + AI Act

> Canonical DWS position on NVIDIA Halos & the physical-AI safety conformity pipeline. Registered in `DWS_TRUTH_STANDARD.md` §2 row 20. **Derived market-signal analysis — not a fact source:** the external instruments are canonical in `DWS_STANDARDS_LIBRARY.md` (Row 3) and the regulatory nodes are modelled in code at `src/compliance/regulatory-timeline.ts`.

**Mode:** market-signal (not a per-customer pipeline scan). Per `/regulatory-radar` use cases 4 (board/investor briefing) and 5 (DWS product planning).

**Signal:** On 2026-06-22 NVIDIA announced **Halos for Robotics** — not a new AI/foundation model, but the industry's first full-stack functional-safety system for physical AI, built on **18,600+ engineering years of NVIDIA autonomous-vehicle safety data**: IGX Thor hardware with a dedicated Functional Safety Island (FSI, SIL-3), Holoscan Sensor Bridge, Halos OS/Core (safety-certified layer running on Linux and QNX OS for Safety 8.0 for real-time control), an open "Outside-In Safety Blueprint" (external infrastructure-mounted cameras that monitor blind spots/dynamic zones so robots adjust speed and trajectory proactively instead of hard-stopping), and an **ANAB-accredited AI Systems Inspection Lab** — the first such program — that pre-assesses integrations against IEC 61508 and ISO 13849 before third-party certification by TÜV Rheinland, UL Solutions and TÜV SÜD. Agility Robotics is the first humanoid maker to integrate it (5th-gen Digit, targeted for late 2026), enabling warehouse/factory navigation for customers including **Amazon and Toyota**. This is a **standardisation/normalisation signal**: the controls the EU will require of safety-relevant AI in machinery are moving from bespoke safety-engineering projects to a shared, citable, pre-certification pipeline. For a physical-AI deployer this raises the _expected baseline_ a Notified Body and an auditor will measure against — exactly as the EU Machinery Regulation and AI Act conformity regimes come into force.

## 1. Halos layer → EU regulatory obligation → DWS IQ primitive

The relevant EU lens is the **dual conformity regime for safety-relevant AI in machinery**: the Machinery Regulation is the paramount sector law, and the AI Act treats AI safety components of machinery as high-risk through its harmonisation-legislation annex (Notified Body assessment under the Machinery Regulation discharges the overlapping AI Act conformity step).

| Halos layer                                                                                         | What it delivers                                                                | EU obligation it serves                                                                                                 | DWS IQ primitive (where DWS sits)                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **IGX Thor + Functional Safety Island (FSI)** (IEC 61508 SIL 3)                                     | Physically isolated, industrial-grade certified safety compute for AI inference | Machinery Reg Annex I.A №5/6 (AI safety components ensuring safety functions) · AI Act Art. 15 robustness/cybersecurity | Complementary, not overlapping — DWS does not ship robot silicon. KYA sandbox / MicroVM isolation is the _agent-layer_ analogue |
| **Halos OS / Halos Core** (Linux + QNX OS for Safety 8.0, deterministic)                            | Certified deterministic execution backing real-time AI decisions and control    | AI Act Art. 9 risk management · Art. 15 accuracy/robustness                                                             | Verification gates + deterministic-over-model rule (Rule 5: code answers, not the model)                                        |
| **Outside-In Safety Blueprint** (infra cameras + AI agents)                                         | Global work-cell awareness; modulates robot behaviour vs hard E-stop            | Machinery Reg safety-function requirements · AI Act Art. 14 human oversight                                             | SiteSense / `/site-safety` · Lifetime Fleet Control Room & Situation Room (`/remote-control`)                                   |
| **Halos AI Systems Inspection Lab** (ANAB-accredited, first of its kind, vs. IEC 61508 + ISO 13849) | Pre-assessment pipeline before third-party (Notified Body) certification        | Machinery Reg third-party conformity assessment · AI Act Annex I conformity · Art. 12 record-keeping                    | KYA trust tiers (TC-0…TC-4) + **Article-12-auditable signed reports** (Firehorse Report Engine) + `/compliance-check`           |

**Where DWS sits — the one-line ecosystem read:** Halos governs the _physical robot's_ functional safety (silicon → OS → safety blueprint → pre-cert). DWS IQ governs the _AI agents and the regulatory evidence_ — continuous, signed, Article-12-auditable compliance output that a Halos-equipped deployer still owes its auditors under the Machinery Regulation and AI Act. **Complementary, not competitive:** Halos gets the robot certified once; DWS keeps the deployment _continuously evidenced_ and surfaces the next regulation before it bites. The Outside-In infrastructure-camera coordination is the layer where DWS's SiteSense / Situation Room overlaps directly.

### 1.1 Ontology fit (Brain · Memory · Body · Hands + KYA)

Halos maps cleanly onto the DWS four-part architecture and the two governing ontologies, and the boundary is the same one as above — Halos owns the robot's safety; DWS owns the agents and the evidence:

- **Brain** (judgment): DWS agents reason about _compliance and the regulatory pipeline_ (`/compliance-check`, `/regulatory-radar`); Halos OS/Core makes the robot's _real-time safety_ decision. No overlap — different decision domains.
- **Memory** (regulatory-pipeline ontology): the regulatory nodes Halos addresses are modelled in code at `src/compliance/regulatory-timeline.ts` — `eu-machinery-reg` (CELEX 32023R1230, 2027-01-20), the `eu-ai-act` embedded-product node (CELEX 32024R1689, 2028-08-02), and `product-liability-directive` (CELEX 32024L2853, 2026-12-09). Halos itself is **not** a node — this ontology models regulatory _deadlines_, not mitigation assets — so the ANAB pre-certification pathway is captured in those nodes' `actionItems`. This brief is the human-readable seed record.
- **Body** (output): the deliverable is a signed, Article-12-auditable radar/compliance report via the Firehorse Report Engine — the artifact a Halos-certified deployer hands its auditor.
- **Hands** (actuation): Halos's Outside-In infrastructure cameras and IGX/Holoscan telemetry are an _ingestion surface_ for DWS SiteSense / Situation Room (`/remote-control`, Lifetime Fleet) — the one place the two systems physically touch.
- **KYA trust ontology:** a Halos-governed robot is a TC-tiered actor in an agent chain. Its SIL-3 Safety Island is the hardware analogue of KYA's MicroVM/sandbox isolation; a functional-safety fault maps to a KYA trust-tier drop (and, in the limit, a TC-4 Leash Snap) on the agents that command it.

## 2. Why this matters on the pipeline (urgency × risk)

| Instrument                                             | Status               | Next deadline                                  | Urgency    | Applicability                                        | Risk     | Note                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230**                | In force, applies    | **20 Jan 2027**                                | ~7 months  | direct (any robot/physical-AI maker selling into EU) | **high** | **ALERT.** AI safety components with self-evolving/ML behaviour (Annex I.A №5/6) move to **mandatory third-party Notified Body** conformity. Watch-item.                                                                                                                               |

**Normalisation effect (medium-high impact):** once an ANAB-accredited pre-certification pipeline exists for physical-AI safety, "we hand-rolled our own safety case" stops being the defensible default. This raises the floor every EU physical-AI deployer sells above — and gives DWS a concrete artifact to point at in deals ("Halos gets you certified; here is how we run and **sign** the ongoing Art. 9/12/14 evidence so you stay certified").

**Formal-radar classification:** the Machinery Regulation is the single **ALERT** (urgency ≤ effective runway AND risk = high). Halos itself is **not** a statute — it is a market-readiness signal that pulls the compliance baseline forward ahead of the 2027/2028 deadlines.

## 3. Recommended DWS actions

1. **Product (ecosystem fit):** scope a **physical-AI compliance-evidence path** that consumes Halos / IGX telemetry and emits signed Art. 9/12/14 evidence — the continuous layer on top of Halos's one-time certification. Treat IGX/Holoscan as a complementary hardware partner surface, not a build target.
2. **Sales (manufacturing & robotics):** the functional-safety wedge now has external air cover from a tier-1 vendor. Lead with the **throughput lever** (Outside-In safety modulates behaviour instead of halting the line → uptime) owned by Operations, backed by the **certification-delay cost** owned by Legal/compliance. Wedge matrix below; registry §4 row appended.
3. **Regulatory radar roadmap:** add **"pre-certification-pipeline availability"** as a scored input to the radar — when a vendor ships an accredited inspection lab for a domain, the buyer's expected baseline shifts ahead of the statutory deadline, exactly as a published reference implementation does (the Santander lesson, generalised).

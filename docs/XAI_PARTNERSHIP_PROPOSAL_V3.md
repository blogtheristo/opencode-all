## 4. Why EU Is the Highest-Stakes Market for Optimus

Tesla sells cars in Europe under hardware type approval. Optimus is categorically different: it is a **physical AI system acting autonomously in human environments.** That lands it simultaneously under:

| Regulation | Scope | Stakes |
|------------|-------|--------|
| **EU AI Act — Annex III High Risk** | Industrial AI in safety environments | Conformity assessment required before deployment |
| **Machinery Regulation 2023/1230** | Physical robots as "machinery" | CE marking, technical file, notified body |
| **GDPR Article 22** | Automated decisions with physical effect | Human oversight mandatory |
| **NIS2 Directive** | Critical infrastructure sectors | US-cloud AI politically and legally unacceptable |
| **EU AI Act Article 12** | Every AI decision automatically logged | On-prem logs, immutable, auditable |

**The core problem for Optimus in EU regulated industry:**

Tesla and xAI are US companies. The US CLOUD Act means US authorities can demand any data they store — even in EU datacenters. A German chemical plant, a French aerospace factory, a Finnish cement mill **cannot legally** let their floor-level operational data flow through US cloud infrastructure.

Without a sovereign compliance wrapper, Optimus waits in the hallway for legal clearance. **With DWS IQ, it walks in on day one.**

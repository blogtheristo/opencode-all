## Q1 — Why won't OpenAI / Anthropic eat this category from the model layer?

The model layer is the engine, not the car. OpenAI and Anthropic ship horizontal capability; **DWS 365 Skilled Agents** ship sixteen role-shaped workers that produce the signed artifact a regulator demands — CSRD assurance, CBAM declaration, EU AI Act conformity pack, EN 15978 LCA. That requires EU residency, audit trail, role-bound runtime trust (KYA TC-0…TC-4), Themis case coordination, and human-on-loop sign-off in the loop where the regulation says a human must sign. Foundation-model vendors are explicitly upstream of that. Falling model cost is a tailwind for our margin, not a threat — our anchor is the human labor cost, not compute. _(See AI-AS-LABOR-PRICING-STRATEGY §"Why the SaaS Lens Compresses Us".)_

## Q2 — Microsoft / Salesforce ship their own agents — why isn't this commoditized?

Microsoft and Salesforce sell the agent **runtime and distribution**; we sell **the role**. Their first-party agents are horizontal (M365 Copilot, Agentforce Sales / Service) — they do not produce a signed EN 15978 LCA, a CSRD double-materiality study, or a EU AI Act conformity assessment for a Tier-1 EU constructor. We **list inside both ecosystems** on purpose: Azure Marketplace gives us MACC-burn (committed-but-unspent Azure spend pays our sticker), AppExchange gives us "digital labor" vocabulary at a defensible per-agent-month price above their platform meter. The platforms are our distribution channel, not our competitor. _(SKILLED-AGENTS-PRICE-LIST-2026 §6 Channel Comparison.)_

## Q3 — Labor pricing sounds like consulting; what are your software margins?

It looks like consulting on the **anchor**; it behaves like software on the **economics**. Gross margin >80% at list because marginal cost is inference plus a thin human-on-loop review through Themis. Per-agent-month and per-outcome SKUs scale without adding human headcount — that is the whole point. The "Consult-plus-keep-the-agent" bundles use a one-time engagement as a land motion (Year 1 €30k–€250k); Year 2+ is the recurring agent subscription (€25k–€200k/yr) at software-style cost-to-serve. Net: revenue scales with cases / reports / sites, not with our headcount. (CONSULTING-PLUS-AGENTS-BUNDLES-2026 §2; PRODUCT-CATALOG-UPSELL-TIERS-2026 T0→T4 ladder.)

## Q4 — Why now? What's the EU regulatory time-window?

Four enforcement curves stack inside a 24-month window. **CSRD** first-wave reporting hits 2026/27 across ~50k EU undertakings; second wave 2028. **CBAM** full transition closes 2026 — importers of cement, iron, steel, aluminium, fertiliser, electricity, hydrogen carry financial liability from Jan 2027. **EU AI Act** high-risk obligations live August 2026; conformity assessments and post-market monitoring mandatory. **NIS2** fully operational across Member States Q4 2025 — essential / important entities need sovereignty + incident reporting. Pure tools cannot sign these artifacts; Skilled Agents + KYA + Themis can. The window opened; closing it requires a workforce. _(REGULATORY_TAILWIND.md in the data room.)_

## Q6 — What's your moat when fine-tuned models become free?

Three layers the model cannot give you. **(1) The artifact** — a signed CSRD report or EU AI Act conformity pack is a regulatory deliverable, not a model output. **(2) The runtime** — KYA binds every action to a verified human identity with TC-0…TC-4 trust classes and leash-snap chain termination; Themis routes the case across the sixteen agents and escalates to human-on-loop where the regulation requires. **(3) The distribution** — MACC-burn into committed Azure spend and AppExchange "digital labor" listings. Free models compress the copilot category; they do not compress the labor category. Our cost of inference falls, our price holds, margin expands. A fourth layer compounds behind these three: DWS has accumulated **four capital classes** (Prompt / People / Plant / Perception) that a new entrant with the same model still lacks — see Q13. _(AI-AS-LABOR-PRICING-STRATEGY §"Why SaaS Lens Compresses Us"; DWS_BRAND_STANDARD.md §23.)_

## Q7 — TAM: labor-pricing market vs SaaS market for the same workload?

Compare to the **human budget**, not the software budget. EU Big-Four ESG advisory revenue is roughly €30B/yr; EU CSRD/CBAM compliance officer payroll across in-scope undertakings is in the tens of billions; HSE / safety supervisor labor in EU construction is multi-billion. A 5–10% capture of those labor pools is an order of magnitude larger TAM than the equivalent EU SaaS workflow market. The TAM math in the data room is bottoms-up: per-FTE replacement × addressable role count × EU geography, cross-checked against advisory-spend baselines. We do not size the SaaS bucket; SaaS comps misprice us. _(TAM_REFRAMED.md.)_

## Q13 — What's your moat? Why can't a well-funded competitor catch up in 18 months?

We accumulate **four durable capital classes — the 4P model** (canon: DWS*BRAND_STANDARD.md §23). **Prompt Capital**: 30+ codified skills, a 50-agent foundry, eval datasets — a competitor starts with an empty `skills/` directory. **People Capital**: seven months of calibrated HIL judgment that they cannot hire around — they face the same HIL phase, seven months behind. **Plant Capital**: an EU-resident, Article-12-logged, mobile two-room Control Room + Situation Room stack — deployable at a site or in a fleet vehicle, not a rented demo. **Perception Capital**: a governed world model and closed sense→decide→act loop — the only class with data-network effects; a year of lineage-logged observations cannot be bought. Honest caveat: Perception is the youngest class and **still being proven** — we name it as the upside lever, not as banked moat. *(4P field note v2.0; §14.26–14.27 PhD.)\_

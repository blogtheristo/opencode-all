## The Shift in One Table

| Dimension | Tool Lens (SaaS, copilot)     | Labor Lens (DWS 365 Skilled Agents (on DWS IQ))      |
| --------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Buyer     | IT / line-of-business admin   | Department head with a headcount budget              |
| Unit      | Seat, edition, feature flag   | Task completed, case closed, report delivered        |
| Anchor    | Other software                | Loaded labor cost / consultancy day rate / BPO       |
| Pricing   | €X/user/month                 | €X/case, €X/report, €X/agent-month at SLA            |
| Value     | "X% faster for your team"     | "Replaces N FTEs at Y% of fully loaded cost"         |
| Procure   | Software budget               | Services / operations budget                         |
| Risk      | Adoption                      | Output quality, liability, audit trail               |
| Margin    | Compressed by seat saturation | Anchored to human cost — expands as model costs drop |

When the unit of value is **the work itself**, the price ceiling is set by what
that work costs today (human + overhead), not by what comparable software
costs. SaaS multiples ceiling out where labor multiples start.

### Gross margin

- **>80% gross margin** at list price; marginal cost = inference + a thin human-on-loop review by the Themis case coordinator.
- **Margin expands as model cost falls.** Inference-cost sensitivity (modeled in `data-room-2026Q2/PRICING_MODEL.md`): at 0.5× current inference cost gross margin moves to ~88%; at 2× it holds at ~73%; at 5× it stays positive at ~55%. The price holds against labor — it never has to follow inference cost down.
- **No per-seat cannibalization.** We sell the role, not access — no seat-saturation ceiling on a landed account.
- **Year-2 expansion**: +40–80% per landed account from the attach-rate motion (70% of T1 / T2 customers attach a second Skilled Agent within 6 months — see `data-room-2026Q2/PRICING_MODEL.md`).

## Why We Win

### 1. Regulation is the demand engine, not a tailwind talking point.

- **CSRD** — first wave reporting deadlines in 2026/27 hit ~50k EU undertakings; second wave 2028.
- **CBAM** — full transition closes 2026 with reporting + financial liability on importers of cement, iron, steel, aluminium, fertiliser, electricity, hydrogen.
- **EU AI Act** — high-risk obligations in force from Aug 2026; conformity-assessment and post-market monitoring mandatory for our customer base.
- **NIS2** — operational by Q4 2025 across Member States; sovereignty + audit trail required for essential / important entities.
- **Fit for 55** — material-emissions cap-and-trade pressure from 2026.

Every one of those regulations mandates **a piece of work**, not a tool. Skilled Agents produce the artifact the regulator asks for. (Full radar in `data-room-2026Q2/REGULATORY_TAILWIND.md`.)

### 2. MACC-burn distribution at Tier-1 enterprises.

Every named account on the Q1/26 manifesto target list (Skanska, STRABAG, HOCHTIEF, NCC, PORR, Bilfinger, Implenia, …) sits on an active **Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment**. MACC-burn means committed-but-unspent cloud spend pays our sticker without touching new budget. DWS lists on Azure Marketplace at a 3% gross-up — net to DWS ~100% after the MACC tailwind. Microsoft co-sell credit makes their field a distribution channel for us. (Math: `data-room-2026Q2/DISTRIBUTION_MOAT.md`.)

### 3. EU sovereignty moat.

- **Sovereignty Monitor** (one of the 16 Skilled Agents) audits its own stack and the customer's: data residency, CLOUD Act exposure, RLS posture, NIS2 readiness.
- **EU data residency by default** (Finland / Germany). No CLOUD Act subpoena surface on the data plane.
- For defense-adjacent and critical-infrastructure customers, sovereignty is **a buying criterion** — not a marketing line.

### 4. Themis + KYA produces signable artifacts that pure tools cannot.

- **Themis** is the case coordinator — routes work across the 16 agents, assigns sub-agents, escalates to human-on-loop where the regulation requires it.
- **KYA (Know Your Agent)** is the runtime trust layer — every action is bound to a verified human identity at trust class TC-0…TC-4, with TC-4 Leash Snap chain termination for anomalous chains.
- Together: the deliverable is **audit-trailed, signed by a human, and explainable** — the exact thing CSRD assurance, CBAM declarations, EU AI Act conformity, and NIS2 incident reports require. A copilot produces a draft; DWS produces the signable.

## The 4P Capital Classes — Why the Re-Rate Holds

DWS is not a SaaS vendor; it is a **System of Action operator**. The reason that is true — and the reason the labor multiple is the right multiple — is that the business accumulates four durable, non-purchasable capital classes. Each maps to a specific valuation effect.

| Capital Class (4P)     | What it is                                                              | Valuation lever                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prompt Capital**     | Codified intelligence — 30+ skills, 50-agent foundry, eval datasets     | Moves the comp from SaaS (~**4×** forward ACV) to vertical labor-AI (~**6×**). The base of the cap-expansion math.                                                                     |
| **People Capital**     | 7-month HIL-phase calibrated operator judgment (HIL → human-in-control) | Lowers the **execution / key-person discount** a fund applies — judgment is documented and codified, not trapped in one founder.                                                       |
| **Plant Capital**      | Sovereign, mobile two-room stack (Control Room + Situation Room)        | EU residency + Article-12 audit logging + deployable at-site / in-vehicle = **regulatory moat** and **ACV durability** on landed accounts.                                             |
| **Perception Capital** | Governed world model + closed sense→decide→act loop (the re-rate lever) | The **only class with data-network effects and a physical-AI character**. Pulls in industrial / physical-AI comps — it **defends the €5.5M mid-case and names the €6.1M upper bound**. |

**The re-rate, in one line.** Prompt and People move the comp set off the SaaS curve onto the labor curve (4× → 6×, the engine behind the €3.8M → €4.9M–€6.1M cap range, mid-case **€5.5M**). Plant makes the revenue durable enough to underwrite that multiple. **Perception is the lever that justifies the top of the range** — a competitor can buy edge hardware, but cannot buy a year of governed, lineage-logged observations. It is the only class that compounds with use.

### 4P as an M&A / diligence ontology

The same four classes are how a strategic acquirer or a fund should _score_ any agentic operator: rate the target on **Prompt** (is the intelligence codified, or living in chat history?), **People** (is operator judgment documented, or about to walk out?), **Plant** (is the stack sovereign and deployable, or a rented demo?), and **Perception** (is there a governed, compounding world model, or an empty graph?). The acute risk in a shrinking-team agentic company is **People + Perception walking out the door together** — the calibrated judgment and the world model that judgment built leave as a pair, and neither is on the balance sheet. (The Kaukopää tacit-ontology case: when the team that holds the ontology leaves, the ontology leaves with them.) DWS scores high on all four _because_ it endured the HIL phase rather than skipping it.

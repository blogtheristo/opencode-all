Why We Win

1. Regulation drives demand. CSRD (waves 2026/27), CBAM (close 2026), EU AI Act high-risk (Aug 2026), NIS2 (Q4 2025), Fit-for-55 — every regulation mandates a piece of work, not a tool. Skilled Agents produce the artifact the regulator asks for.

2. MACC-burn distribution. Every Tier-1 named account sits on an active Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment. Azure Marketplace listings burn committed-but-unspent cloud spend at our sticker; Microsoft co-sell credit turns their field into a distribution channel for us.

3. EU sovereignty moat. EU data residency by default (Finland / Germany). Sovereignty Monitor (one of the 16 agents) audits CLOUD Act exposure, RLS posture, NIS2 readiness. For defense-adjacent and critical-infrastructure customers, sovereignty is a buying criterion.

4. Themis + KYA = signable artifacts. Themis routes work across the 16 agents and escalates to human-on-loop where regulation requires. KYA binds every action to a verified human identity (TC-0…TC-4) with leash-snap chain termination. The deliverable is audit-trailed, signed, and explainable — what CSRD assurance, CBAM declarations, AI Act conformity, and NIS2 incident reports require.

The 4P Capital Classes — Why the Re-Rate Holds

DWS is not a SaaS vendor; it is a System of Action operator. The reason that is true — and the reason the labor multiple is the right multiple — is that the business accumulates four durable, non-purchasable capital classes. Each maps to a specific valuation effect.

Capital Class (4P) What it is Valuation lever
Prompt Capital Codified intelligence — 30+ skills, 50-agent foundry, eval datasets Moves the comp from SaaS (~4× forward ACV) to vertical labor-AI (~6×). The base of the cap-expansion math.
People Capital 7-month HIL-phase calibrated operator judgment (HIL → human-in-control) Lowers the execution / key-person discount a fund applies — judgment is documented and codified, not trapped in one founder.
Plant Capital Sovereign, mobile two-room stack (Control Room + Situation Room) EU residency + Article-12 audit logging + deployable at-site / in-vehicle = regulatory moat and ACV durability on landed accounts.
Perception Capital Governed world model + closed sense→decide→act loop (the re-rate lever) The only class with data-network effects and a physical-AI character. Pulls in industrial / physical-AI comps — it defends the €5.5M mid-case and names the €6.1M upper bound.

The re-rate, in one line. Prompt and People move the comp set off the SaaS curve onto the labor curve (4× → 6×, the engine behind the €3.8M → €4.9M–€6.1M cap range, mid-case €5.5M). Plant makes the revenue durable enough to underwrite that multiple. Perception is the lever that justifies the top of the range — a competitor can buy edge hardware, but cannot buy a year of governed, lineage-logged observations. It is the only class that compounds with use.

4P as an M&A / diligence ontology

The same four classes are how a strategic acquirer or a fund should score any agentic operator: rate the target on Prompt (intelligence codified, or living in chat history?), People (judgment documented, or about to walk out?), Plant (stack sovereign and deployable, or a rented demo?), and Perception (a governed, compounding world model, or an empty graph?). The acute risk in a shrinking-team agentic company is People + Perception walking out the door together — the calibrated judgment and the world model that judgment built leave as a pair, and neither is on the balance sheet (the Kaukopää tacit-ontology case). DWS scores high on all four because it endured the HIL phase rather than skipping it.
